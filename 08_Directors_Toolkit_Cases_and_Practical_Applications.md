## 08.01 How to use the toolkit: first establish legal obligations

### 08.01.1 Four levels of authority

| Level | Character | Examples | Board response |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Binding law and rights | Statute, regulation, operative court ruling; mandatory listing, prudential or licence requirements within their scope | Corporations Act, CATSI Act, WA associations law, prescribed-body-corporate regulations, ASX Listing Rule 3.1 | Establish applicability, responsible persons, required decision, timing, records, external legal advice. A toolkit never overrides this. |
| 2. Organisation's enforceable arrangements | Constitution/rule book, member resolutions, trust deed, binding agreements and properly made delegations | Appointing directors, member-reserved approvals, common law holder consent, grant restrictions, borrowing covenants | Read the instrument and verify version, quorum, notice, eligibility, voting, conflicts and any consent condition. |
| 3. Regulator guidance and voluntary standards | ASIC/ACNC/ORIC guidance; ASX governance *recommendations*, AICD, ISO/COSO/AIGI principles | Suggested board questions, annual work-plan, skills matrix, risk maturity, cultural legitimacy | Adopt proportionately; disclose departures where the applicable disclosure regime requires; do not describe a recommendation as universal law. |
| 4. This file's editorial tools | Original question sets, tables, flags, decision tests and workflows | Option comparisons, assurance maps, action tracker | Tailor to context; label assumptions; use as aids to competent judgment, never as safe-harbour certificates. |

### 08.01.2 Use sequence and outputs

1. **Identify the entity and the decision.** Record registration, jurisdiction, governing document, any charity, ASX, prudential or RNTBC status. An Indigenous-led entity is not necessarily CATSI registered; an NFP is not necessarily an ACNC charity.
2. **Run the legal gateway** (08.02 and relevant 08.10 kit). Separate a director's duties, the board's corporate powers, member approvals, any trust restrictions, and native title holders' consultation/consent rights.
3. **Choose one tool by risk or decision**, not because a pre-set calendar says every checklist must be completed. See 08.11 T01–T48.
4. **Request primary evidence.** Documents must be version-controlled, dated and attributable. Ask for an independent perspective when significance or uncertainty warrants it.
5. **Exercise judgment.** Distinguish legally prohibited, delegable, reserved, conditionally approvable and genuinely discretionary decisions. Where an essential fact or approval is missing, defer execution, not necessarily deliberation.
6. **Minute a decision trail.** State the issue, advice, alternatives, conflicts, participation/consent, evidence relied upon, resolution, responsible person, deadline and monitoring trigger. Keep privileged advice secure.
7. **Close the loop.** Verify implementation, control remediation, follow-up reports, stakeholder communication and lessons learned.

**Suggested working status codes:** `LEGAL STOP` (authority or legal prerequisite absent), `HOLD FOR EVIDENCE`, `CONDITIONAL APPROVAL`, `APPROVED`, `NOT APPROVED`, and `DELEGATED WITH LIMITS`. These are editorial labels, not statutory classifications. Each needs an owner and date.

## 08.02 Governance scenario triage: authority before analysis

### 08.02.1 The eight-gate decision worksheet (original)

| Gate | Director's discriminating question | Minimum evidence | Route if unresolved |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Legal identity** | What is the *actual contracting and decision-making entity*? Is this the parent's, subsidiary's, charity's, trustee's or RNTBC's decision? | ABN/ACN/ICN; current regulator extract, entity diagram | Obtain legal entity confirmation; do not rely on brand or group name. |
| **2. Governing authority** | Which statute, constitution/rule book, trust deed, grant, agreement or board resolution grants this power? | Controlled versions; reserved-powers/delegation schedules | Company secretary or counsel to map authority. |
| **3. Duty and conflict** | Which persons owe what duty, to whom, and who has a financial or duty conflict? | Interest register, related-party analysis, duty analysis, relevant permissions | Chair to resolve participation lawfully; obtain independent advice if needed. |
| **4. Decision owner** | Is approval board-reserved, committee-advisory, management-delegated, member-reserved or a holder-consent matter? | Authority map, quorum, minutes, proposed execution authority | Do not infer one approval substitutes for another. |
| **5. Evidence** | What would change the recommendation? What data contradicts it? | Signed paper, forecasts, advice, dissent, independent assurance | Hold or set evidence conditions with owner and date. |
| **6. External rights** | Who must be consulted, notified, heard or approve? By what lawful process? | Member notice rules, regulator requirements, affected common law holder identification, consent record | No irreversible step before legally required consultation/consent. |
| **7. Time and sensitivity** | Is there a legal deadline, safety risk, cash crisis, confidential information or immediate disclosure question? | Dated event log, time-critical advice, cash position, materiality record | Same-day chair/CEO/counsel escalation as warranted. |
| **8. Decision and review** | Is the lawful choice to approve, condition, refuse, delegate or defer, and what verifies the outcome? | Proposed minute, implementation plan, indicators, trigger points | Record owner, limits and a future board review. |

### 08.02.2 Triage routing rules

- **Red route: possible immediate harm or invalid act.** Examples: suspected insolvency and new debt; serious injury risk; potential market-sensitive undisclosed information; unapproved native title dealing; data breach with notification exposure; unmanageable conflict. Convene the right people urgently, preserve evidence, obtain legal/technical advice, consider regulator or emergency notifications, and do not sign an irreversible instrument prematurely.
- **Amber route: choice may be valid but evidence insufficient.** Seek options, stress tests, counterparty checks, affected-person consultation and a clear board paper. Record the absence rather than fill it with assumptions.
- **Green route: ordinary delegated operation within limits.** Management executes; board monitors policy, exception reports and performance. A board need not ratify every operational act.

**RNTBC decision test:** An apparently routine commercial contract may contain a native title decision or compensation component. Split the transaction into corporate approval, native title rights/consent, related trustee decisions and any separate agreement parties. Regulation 8 permits particular processes, with qualified alternatives under 8A and standing-instruction exceptions; regulation 9 requires relevant certification. Neither an RNTBC board resolution nor an AGM vote automatically evidences affected common law holder consent. AICD[RISK, 2025/26 V1, pp. 16–18]; ORIC decision-making guidance (12 October 2026); PBC Regulations 1999 regs 8–10, reviewed 28 October 2026.

**Triage record, one line:** `ID | event/date | entity/capacity | legal hook | decision class | conflict | affected rights | evidence missing | deadline | owner | interim safeguard | next decision`. Avoid recording privileged legal advice verbatim in general distribution.

## 08.03 Board appointment and onboarding kit

### 08.03.1 Pre-acceptance: two-sided due diligence

**Director investigates the organisation:** Verify legal form, purpose and constitution/rule book; membership and director appointment mechanism; regulatory and filing status; latest audited/reviewed accounts or available accounting records; rolling cash forecast; debt and guarantees; grant and trust restrictions; unresolved audit issues; sanctions and complaints; safety, cyber, privacy and legal incidents; disputes, related parties, insurers and claim notifications; executive turnover; strategy and risk appetite; board meeting quality; expected time and remuneration. Request meetings with chair, key executives, external auditor and relevant stakeholders within authorised confidentiality boundaries. For RNTBCs, ascertain the determination, trustee/agent status, rule book, affected common law holders and method for required consultation, without claiming to prescribe community practices.

**Board investigates the candidate:** Verify eligibility and disqualification status, consent to act and required director identification requirements, qualifications, sector knowledge, financial literacy, demonstrated conduct, time and conflicts, related-party relationships, independence where relevant and willingness to ask unwelcome questions. Test fit with the *future* strategy and stakeholder obligations, not just personal compatibility. Evidence: selection scorecard, references, candidate declaration, register checks, member/appointor correspondence. AICD[GOV, 2025/26 V1, pp. 21–34]; AICD[LEG, 2025/26 V1, pp. 19–22]; AICD[TOOL, 2025/26 V1, pp. 14, 42–46].

### 08.03.2 Appointment control sequence (original)

1. **Authority memo.** Who appoints or elects, eligibility, nominated/director class, term, notice/quorum, required votes, special arrangements? Confirm under actual law and governing document; do not substitute chair invitation for valid appointment.
2. **Conflict and capacity memo.** Map outside directorships, related-party transactions, employment/consultancy ties, funder or government roles, perceived duty conflicts and practical mitigation. Some conflicts make appointment unsuitable despite potential disclosures.
3. **Terms and protection.** Written appointment expectations, role limits, remuneration, confidentiality, interests, permitted advice and expense approvals. Review lawful deed of access/indemnity, directors' and officers' (D&O) policy limits, exclusions, claims-made basis, retroactive date and run-off cover; insurance cannot remove a statutory duty.
4. **Formalities.** Record nomination/consent, valid election or board appointment, director-ID verification and relevant ASIC/ORIC/association/ACNC notifications. Check responsible-person updating responsibilities rather than assuming one common form works everywhere.
5. **Induction in the first 90 days (illustrative schedule, not a legal deadline).** Before first meeting: constitution, board charter, most recent agenda/minutes, three statements, risk and legal registers, crisis plan and strategy. First 30 days: chair/CEO/CFO/auditor conversations, conflicts and disclosure briefing, operations/site exposure. By day 60: grants/trusts, major contracts, key control tests and stakeholder map. By day 90: skills-development plan, feedback and any information-access gaps.
6. **Annual refresh.** Reconfirm interests and eligibility, D&O cover, skill gaps, professional development, performance and succession planning.

**Appointment stop signs:** absent recent financials, unexplained financing or director exits, unclear beneficial interest, no legitimate appointment power, requested signature before access to documents, unaddressed safety/data breaches, conflicted chair control, unverified holder authorisation for RNTBC native title matters. A prospective director may decline or request more evidence without purporting to conduct a regulator investigation.

**Decision ownership:** Nomination or governance committee may recommend; the appointor/members/board decide as the law and rules require; secretary records and files; the individual director independently decides whether to accept. Distinguish employed executives' obligations from non-executive board oversight. Cross-reference File 01 §§01.04–01.05, 01.08, 01.17; File 02 §§02.07, 02.16; File 06 §06.05; File 07 §§07.05, 07.15; tools T03, T16–T17, T24.

## 08.04 Board operations kit: a disciplined decision system

### 08.04.1 Board charter and reserved-powers design

A board charter should specify purpose, role boundary, the chair and CEO relationship, lawful director access, reserved decisions, delegation framework, committee advisory scope, conflicts, information standards, review cadence and evaluation process. It is a governance document: it must not pretend to amend a statute, constitution, member right or native title consent regime.

**Original reserved-powers schedule** (confirm and tailor to actual legal authority):

| Decision family | Suggested board control | Can management prepare? | External gate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Organisation purpose and strategy | Approve and periodically reassess strategic direction, risk capacity, budget and material pivots | Yes; alternatives and execution | Member/holder or regulator rights where applicable |
| CEO and succession | Appoint, evaluate, remunerate and replace CEO; agree emergency coverage | Committee supports | Contract and employment requirements |
| Material capital and financing | Approve above defined risk/value thresholds, new guarantees and leverage exceptions | Due diligence, forecast and negotiation within limits | Shareholder, creditor, grant, trust or holder approvals as triggered |
| Reporting and assurance | Approve board declarations and reports when legally required; oversee audit independence | CFO, auditor and audit committee advise | Actual statutory sign-off and filing requirements |
| Risk and compliance | Set risk appetite, approve major policy and crisis oversight; review breaches | Design/operate controls | Mandatory notification where triggered |
| Entity and rights changes | Constitution/rule-book changes, merger/winding up and other transformational decisions | Develop proposals | Member special resolutions, regulatory consent; affected native title holders on native title decisions |
| Delegation and committees | Define limits, exemptions, prohibited subdelegation, reporting and review | Secretary maintains register | No delegation of decisions legally reserved elsewhere |

Source orientation: AICD[GOV, 2025/26 V1, pp. 52–79]; AICD[TOOL, 2025/26 V1, pp. 36–38]. Corporate management rule: Corporations Act 2001 (Cth) s 198A, **replaceable-rule/constitution qualification**, C149 reviewed 28 October 2026. CATSI and WA authority derive from their own statutes and rules.

### 08.04.2 Annual board calendar: original work-plan pattern

The secretary builds the work plan backward from the entity's **actual** statutory filings, member meetings, audit/assurance appointments, grant acquittals, licence renewals and native title holder engagement timetable. Allocate realistic board time for strategy, risk, liquidity, CEO succession and culture rather than allowing compliance to consume the agenda.

| Cycle | Illustrative board work (adjust year-end and reporting calendar) | Advance evidence |
|---|---|---|
| First quarter | Annual performance, audited/reviewed report as applicable, director declaration and cash runway; governance calendar confirmation | Close timetable, forecast, audit issues, obligations register |
| Second quarter | Purpose/strategy options, sector horizon, risk appetite and material funding/capital decisions | Scenarios, alternatives, stakeholder evidence, independent challenge |
| Third quarter | Culture, people and safety, board skills, succession, cyber/privacy/AI resilience and crisis exercise | Culture measures, workforce incidents, control tests, exercise results |
| Fourth quarter | Budget approval, multi-year capacity, delegations, committee reviews, board evaluation and next year's calendar | Rolling forecasts, project gates, assurance follow-up, evaluation plan |
| **Every meeting** | Conflicts; prior actions; material exceptions; solvency/liquidity signals; strategy delivery; reserved approvals | Short dashboard with variance, leading risks, decisions and owners |

Annual-quarter labels are **not** statutory due dates. For RNTBCs add a separately planned holder-consultation calendar; no board timetable can shorten required consent processes. AICD[TOOL, 2025/26 V1, pp. 3–11].

### 08.04.3 Board-paper architecture and meeting practice

**Original two-page executive paper skeleton:** `Decision sought (one sentence) → legal decision/consent class → why now → recommended option → credible counter-option and do nothing → benefits/costs/cash impact → baseline/downsides/assumptions → risk and control assurance → stakeholder and holder rights → conflicts → implementation owner and stop/return triggers → draft resolution → appendices/source provenance`. Material projects may require far more than two pages of annexes. The short front sheet is a navigation aid, not permission to hide risk.

**Agenda design:** preliminary formalities and interests; matters requiring decision; strategy and exceptions (with debate time); finance/solvency; risk/compliance/safety; CEO and culture; committee recommendations; in-camera discussion when appropriate; evaluation and action confirmation. Keep information-only papers off the main decision agenda when safe. Circulate with enough time for review; late material decisions require a reasoned urgency protocol, not automatic ratification.

**Minutes: decision record, not transcript.** Note date, attendees, apologies, quorum, material interests and how addressed, principal information and options, material questions, independent advice, required consultations/consents, resolutions, votes/abstentions where relevant, execution limits and time-sensitive follow-up. For contentious matters, document the substance of challenge and response without manufacturing unanimity or legal privilege. Draft promptly; approve according to rules; preserve safe document access and retention.

**Delegation register** (original fields): `power/source | delegator | delegate | dollar/exposure limit | prohibited matters | conditions | expiry | reporting cycle | escalation threshold | last review | instrument link`. Test an actual transaction against both dollar and qualitative exposure; prevent aggregation/splitting to bypass thresholds.

**Action tracker** (original fields): `resolution ID | deliverable/test | accountable executive/committee | due date | dependency/holder approval | status | evidence of closure | board acceptance | re-open trigger`. Status is not closure until evidence is verified. The chair owns meeting discipline, secretary documents authority and follow-through, CEO owns implementation within delegation, and the board retains collective oversight. AICD[TOOL, 2025/26 V1, pp. 24–26, 118–123]. Cross-reference File 02 §§02.03, 02.12–02.13; File 01 §§01.03, 01.11; tools T01, T05–T08, T14, T37–T40, T42.

## 08.05 Board effectiveness kit: test the quality, not just the meeting

### 08.05.1 Original six-part decision-quality test

Score each material board decision `green / amber / red` on (1) **right to decide** (authority, conflict, holder/member rights), (2) **truthfulness of problem framing** (real underlying issue and interests), (3) **options** (credible alternative and cost of doing nothing), (4) **evidence** (data sources, uncertainties, contrary signals and expertise), (5) **deliberation** (challenge, diversity, ability to disagree, ethical impacts), and (6) **execution/learning** (owner, monitor, reconsideration trigger). Any legal `red` blocks execution. A low evidence or options score normally requires further work unless urgency justifies a documented conditional decision. Avoid converting this into a mechanistic 'overall score' that conceals critical deficits. AICD[BE, 2025/26 V1, pp. 13–32, 64–88]; AICD[TOOL, 2025/26 V1, pp. 34–35, 100–101].

### 08.05.2 Skills matrix and succession (original format)

Use rows of *capabilities required by forthcoming decisions*: financial/reporting, enterprise risk/technology, sector/operations, law and regulation, strategy/capital allocation, workforce/safety, stakeholder/charity purpose, cultural governance/native title where relevant, inclusive chairing and commercial negotiation. Use columns `required depth`, `director with evidence`, `adequate collective coverage?`, `independence/conflict risk`, `next 24-month need`, `gap remedy`. Distinguish **direct experience**, **working literacy**, **external advice** and **not covered**. The matrix is confidential decision support; do not infer individual competence from demographic identity or class of membership. Remedy by induction, committee advice, recruitment or stakeholder-authorised participation, not only replacing directors. AICD[TOOL, 2025/26 V1, pp. 14–15, 81–88]; AICD[BE, 2025/26 V1, pp. 42–63].

### 08.05.3 Process for difficult board conversations

1. **Before:** chair identifies the substantive disagreement, decision constraint, conflict/privilege risks, who has not been heard and whether mediation, translation, advice or an authorised community process is needed.
2. **Frame:** describe decision facts and stakes without labelling a person 'difficult'. Let the proposer set out the strongest case and let a nominated director articulate the strongest alternative.
3. **Explore:** use precise questions: *Which assumption would reverse our decision? Who bears losses? What evidence supports the minority view? Are incentives changing the answer?*
4. **Protect participation:** invite quieter directors; avoid interruption, coercion and informal decisions outside the proper forum. Make support available for accessibility and language needs.
5. **Resolve or defer:** chair tests what is agreed, what remains contested, and whether a further independent review is warranted. Do not present culturally specific disagreement-resolution procedures as universal.
6. **Document:** record the material reasoning, conflict management, dissent when relevant, conditions and subsequent relationship/decision repair.

### 08.05.4 Meeting debrief, culture diagnostic and formal evaluation

After a complex meeting, ask directors privately: *Did we address the real decision? Did information arrive early enough? What was missing? Who challenged? Did the chair close debate prematurely? Were conflicts and rights respected? Which accepted challenge changed the decision?* Summarise two operational improvements and one follow-up owner, not individual clinical diagnoses. For organisational culture, triangulate complaints, hotline usage and retaliation signals, turnover/absence, safety incidents, external complaints, audit exceptions, incentive outcomes and firsthand/site exposure. A low reported-incident count may indicate suppression rather than safety. Protect whistleblower confidentiality and data privacy.

**Evaluation cycle:** agree scope (full board, chair, committees, individual director), independent facilitator or appropriate internal reviewer, confidential interviews + paper/meeting sample + stakeholder feedback, observations against the charter and strategy, prioritised action plan, quarterly progress check and next-cycle outcome evaluation. Separate board behavioural effectiveness from merely meeting compliance deadlines. Where an RNTBC selects its own legitimacy or engagement indicators, use its authorised rules and local choices, not asserted universal cultural benchmarks. AICD[BE, 2025/26 V1, pp. 89–104]; AICD[TOOL, 2025/26 V1, pp. 27–29, 68–80, 97, 104, 131–132]. Cross-reference File 02 §§02.04–02.18; File 07 §§07.06–07.07; tools T03, T07–T08, T13, T23–T24, T29, T31, T33, T43–T45.

## 08.06 Strategy and capital allocation kit

### 08.06.1 Strategy-option comparison: original decision table

Strategy is an integrated choice of activities, investment and constraints, not a collection of unrelated growth projects. The board sets purpose and strategic direction, reviews management's choices, approves reserved commitments and monitors outcomes. Management develops a testable plan and executes within authority. AICD[RISK, 2025/26 V1, pp. 19–86]; AICD[TOOL, 2025/26 V1, pp. 39–40, 102–103].

| Test | Defend/improve existing model | Build new activity | Partner/contract out | Exit/defer |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Value or purpose | Value retained/mission quality improved | Incremental value/outcomes net of cost | Shared outcomes, rights and margins | Value of capacity freed or loss avoided |
| Cash/capital | Maintenance and reinvestment | Upfront and follow-on funding, working capital | Payments, guarantees, counterparty exposure | Closure/transition costs; option preserved |
| Risk case | Competitive/mission erosion | Forecast, demand, execution, regulatory and technology risk | Dependency, governance, service and reputation | Foregone upside, stakeholder harm |
| Constraints | Liquidity and control capacity | Funding, skills, approvals, holder/donor restrictions | Rights, service quality, contract access | Contract, grant, employment, cultural and legal rights |
| Failure signal | Declining unit economics or service evidence | Negative scenarios, milestone failures | Counterparty distress or poor service | Cost of delay or unmet beneficiary duty |
| Review trigger | Margin/quality/mission threshold | Gate review before next tranche | Renewal/default/quality checkpoint | Re-entry/alternative proposal trigger |

**Financial test:** For a commercial capital proposal, distinguish positive operating contribution from value creation: compare incremental after-tax unlevered cash flows with the appropriate cost of capital; include capital invested, terminal value, closure costs, financing constraints and option alternatives. In a charity, appraise **cost per verified outcome, continuity, restrictions and solvency** rather than inventing a universal social return dollar multiple. In CATSI/RNTBC settings, financial merits do not replace native title rights, rule-book authority, benefit distribution or trust conditions.

### 08.06.2 Approval gates and board questions

**Gate 0, authority:** Is pursuing the option consistent with statutory purpose and objects, and who may decide or consent? **Gate 1, strategic logic:** What problem is solved? What alternatives were genuinely considered? **Gate 2, commercial/mission case:** Does the probability-weighted and downside case justify resource use; which economic assumptions dominate? **Gate 3, capacity and risk:** Are the people, balance sheet, systems, permissions, culture and controls ready? **Gate 4, commitment:** What is being approved *now*: concept, capped diligence spend, conditional execution or full funding? **Gate 5, monitoring:** Which leading signals, capital tranches, breach thresholds and termination rights compel return to the board? A no-go gate is useful only if management can realistically stop expenditure without penalty or a new approval.

Original board questions: *What is the least-cost reversible test? What other initiative loses funding? What would we do if this forecast fails? Is the value case dependent on a regulatory subsidy or one customer? Does every proposed growth stage add value after its additional capital? What cash must be maintained under stress? Have beneficiaries, members and rights holders been addressed through the correct decision channel?*

**Execution monitoring:** Keep an assumptions register (`hypothesis | baseline | source | accountable owner | early signal | threshold | decision if breached`); a capital pipeline reconciled to cash and commitments; a risk-appetite dashboard; and separate financial/non-financial leading and lagging indicators. Do not use a favourable net present value to disguise poor liquidity or invalid authority. Cross-reference File 03 §§03.02–03.07, 03.09, 03.13; File 04 §§04.09–04.11, 04.14 (three-way forecasts, financial performance, value and solvency; file-level reconciliation still pending); File 07 §§07.11–07.13; tools T15, T25–T26, T32, T34, T47.

## 08.07 Enterprise risk and resilience kit

### 08.07.1 Risk appetite: five practical tests

1. **Legal floor:** What outcomes are unlawful or incompatible with rights? A 'higher appetite' cannot authorise them.
2. **Capacity:** What is the worst credible financial, operational, human or legitimacy loss the organisation could withstand before critical purpose or solvency fails?
3. **Tolerance:** For material risk classes, what observable triggers require managerial action, CEO escalation or board intervention? Separate an aspiration (e.g., no injuries) from a measurable control/response threshold.
4. **Integration:** Are project approvals, budgets, incentives and delegation limits consistent with appetite, including stress correlations across projects?
5. **Evidence:** Which risk indicators and independent control tests allow the board to know whether it remains inside appetite, rather than merely accept a coloured heat map?

**Original appetite statement construction:** `risk domain | permissible exposure under specified conditions | boundary | leading/lagging metric | control owner | independent assurance | breach notification period | decision if exceeded`. For cash risk, consider minimum forecast liquidity, committed headroom and downside funding. For cyber, define maximum acceptable recovery times and critical-data exposure with technical advice. For WHS, define mandatory controls and escalation, not an imagined numerical permission for unlawful harm. AICD[RISK, 2025/26 V1, pp. 97–145]; AICD[TOOL, 2025/26 V1, pp. 16, 30, 89–92].

### 08.07.2 Control assurance and indicators

Use a **critical-control register**: `risk/event | preventive/detective/recovery control | accountable manager | proof of design | proof tested in operation | testing date | test failures | residual exposure | repair deadline | independent challenge`. Prioritise a small number of controls essential to avoid catastrophic or mission-ending outcomes; a long list of risks can conceal a short list of failing controls. Distinguish **inherent** and **residual** risk, and management assessment from independently tested effectiveness. The board or a properly constituted committee reviews exceptions and asks whether controls alter likely outcomes.

A useful board report shows **trend and cause**, not only an ordinal rating. Combine key risk indicators (e.g., overdue safety actions, cash headroom in downside cases, privileged-access exceptions, unresolved misconduct reports) with key performance indicators (service delivery/return) and observed incidents. Avoid falsely treating multiplying risk 'likelihood scores' by 'impact scores' as a reliable dollar loss or probability estimate; use scenarios for consequential decisions.

### 08.07.3 Cyber, privacy and artificial-intelligence oversight

Request one information-flow map showing sensitive data categories, origin, purpose, storage, access, vendors, retention and disposal; critical-system and data backups with recovery test evidence; supply-chain dependencies; incident response decision authority; cyber insurance restrictions; and whether a personal-data breach engages the **Privacy Act 1988 (Cth)**, Australian Privacy Principles or Part IIIC notifiable-data-breach scheme. Scope must be checked: not every organisation or dataset is covered identically. For artificial intelligence (AI), request the uses register (including unapproved tools), data-provenance and confidentiality controls, human-review points, testing for errors and discrimination, IP/license issues, change-control and the specific decision for which a model's output is relied upon. Distinguish experimentation from releasing automated decisions to the public. AICD[LEG, 2025/26 V1, pp. 103–112]; AICD[TOOL, 2025/26 V1, pp. 33, 98–99].

**Crisis exercise:** In 90 minutes (illustrative), work through detection, board notification, business continuity, affected-person safety, regulator/legal triage, finance/liquidity, stakeholder communications and recovery authorisation. Include one scenario in which management is unavailable or incident data is unreliable. Record failed assumptions, resource gaps, responsible remediation and retest date. For RNTBCs, keep native title consultation authority distinct from the emergency operational response; no generic crisis protocol cancels statutory holder rights. Cross-reference File 03 §§03.08–03.16; File 01 §01.15; File 07 §§07.08, 07.13; tools T04, T09, T12, T25–T26, T30, T34, T38, T43, T46, T48.

## 08.08 Financial interpretation and financial-distress kit

### 08.08.1 Integrate the statements, not isolated ratios

**Board question chain:** (1) What caused revenue/expense and operating-surplus changes? (2) Where did those accrual earnings appear as receivables, stock, provisions, debt and equity? (3) Which entries generated or consumed **actual cash**? (4) What financing, investment and non-cash accounting choices explain the difference? (5) Is near-term debt-paying capacity credible after unfunded commitments? (6) What does this mean for strategy, controls and the board's current decisions?

**Original integrated review table:**

| Topic | What to calculate or inspect | The director's challenge |
|---|---|---|
| Operating economics | Revenue and volume/price; gross margin; operating profit by activity; recurring vs exceptional items | Are costs deferred, one-offs overused or growth consuming margin? |
| Working capital | Receivables ageing, slow inventory, payables; working capital = current assets − current liabilities | Is reported profit becoming cash, or being financed by suppliers/grants? |
| Liquidity | Current ratio = current assets / current liabilities; quick ratio = (cash + near-cash receivables) / current liabilities (definition adjusted consistently) | When do liabilities fall due versus conversion of assets? A current ratio of 1.5 is **not law**. |
| Leverage and cover | Net debt/earnings; interest cover; debt maturity; covenant headroom | Is the denominator genuinely recurring, and how does debt refinancing work under stress? |
| Cash conversion | Operating cash flow / net profit or operating cash flow / earnings (definitions specified); maintenance capex and lease commitments | Is apparent cash strength driven by deferred supplier payment or once-off grants? |
| Cash available | Opening unrestricted cash + collected receipts − required disbursements; 13-week cash flow plus 12–18-month scenarios as warranted | What restricted cash cannot be used for salaries, debt or distributions? |
| Economic/mission value | Return on invested capital versus cost of capital (commercial); verified service outputs and funded reserves (NFP) | Is capital growth productive, and are mission outcomes genuinely incremental? |

**Source and definition discipline:** state accounting period, currency, consolidated versus entity-only basis, treatment of leases, restricted funds, contingent liabilities, seasonal working capital and numerator/denominator. Avoid assuming a fall in cash is adverse when it funds value-creating investment; avoid assuming positive accounting equity or a qualified audit opinion establishes solvency. Use notes, ageing, management accounts, bank statements, facilities and credible forecasts. AICD[FIN, 2025/26 V1, pp. 50–100]; AICD[IFS, 2025/26 V1, pp. 12–38]; AICD[TOOL, 2025/26 V1, pp. 53–59, 108, 124].

### 08.08.2 Financial-stress escalation protocol

**Before an event:** approve a rolling forecast policy, cash visibility, operating/banking controls, covenant reporting, escalation thresholds, who may stop new commitments and when legal/insolvency advice is required. **When a trigger occurs:** obtain contemporaneous bank balances, weekly receipts/payments, aged debtors/creditors, secured claims, statutory liabilities, guarantees, contingencies, grant restrictions and realistic refinancing options. **Decide:** whether continued trading and incurring debt is lawful; what protective, funding, restructuring or appointment options require professional advice; how to safeguard employees, beneficiaries and stakeholders; and how frequently to reconvene. **Record:** the information available *at the time*, advice, dissent, why a plausible plan is executable and actions undertaken.

**Non-equivalence:** Corporations Act s 588G/s 588GA and its conditions apply within their statutory scope; WA associations have a distinct debt-incurring provision (WA Act s 127); CATSI Act s 531-1 has its own regime; ACNC Governance Standard 5 expects the *charity* to take reasonable steps regarding insolvent operation. Do not mechanically apply Corporations Act safe harbour to an association or CATSI director. AICD[LEG, 2025/26 V1, pp. 47–54]; AICD[FIN, 2025/26 V1, pp. 90–100]. Cross-reference File 04 (financial statements, ratios, solvency); File 05 (reporting declarations); File 06 §§06.08, 06.11, 06.15; File 07 §§07.13–07.14; tools T18–T20, T36, T41.

## 08.09 Reporting, audit and pre-signing assurance kit

### 08.09.1 Original pre-signing enquiry: 30 / 14 / 7 / 0-day rhythm

These lead times are **good-practice planning**, not statutory deadlines or a reproduced AICD checklist. Adjust to legal reporting dates, reporting complexity and available auditors.

| Stage | Original board/audit committee inquiry | Evidence and disposition |
|---|---|---|
| **T−30** | Confirm entity's actual reporting regime, applicable accounting standards, audit/review obligations, reporting calendar and responsibility; identify major estimates, going concern and reporting-basis decisions. | Reporting-obligation map; trial balance; first full draft; group-structure chart; control-remediation log; audit plan. |
| **T−14** | Challenge material account movements and financial-statement links; assess revenue cut-off, impairment, provisions, litigation, related parties, debt classification and covenant compliance; confirm material incidents and contingent liabilities. | CFO reconciliation, impairment models/sensitivities, legal letters, cash/covenant forecast, auditor questions, unadjusted errors. |
| **T−7** | Decide whether audit evidence is sufficient, what remains uncorrected, independence threats, post-balance-date developments, climate disclosures where in scope, and whether director declarations can truthfully be made. | Updated draft, management representations, audit completion memorandum, independent audit committee recommendations, disclosure register. |
| **T0 / signing** | Each director reads the report and notes, reconciles it to their actual understanding of operations, asks final questions, considers available cash and solvency grounds, resolves outstanding exceptions and votes only on a report and any declaration **actually required by that entity’s applicable reporting regime**. | Dated board pack/resolution and signatories, contemporaneous cash forecast and challenge trail, executed reports and authorised filings. |

### 08.09.2 Assurance map and committee checks

**Management:** owns accounting records, reconciliations, forecasts, internal control and the accuracy of its representations. **Internal audit or equivalent assurance:** tests selected processes/controls and reports limitations and exceptions. **External auditor/reviewer:** gives the opinion or conclusion within the engagement's scope and standards; this does not replace the directors' own attention. **Audit committee:** recommends, challenges judgments, tracks unadjusted differences, non-audit services and audit independence, and assesses controls, fraud and whistleblower matters in its charter. **Board:** makes required declarations/approvals, reviews going concern, approves filing and monitors corrective action.

**Original eight-question chair's close:** *Which figures would most change if our assumptions are wrong? What does the auditor disagree with? What remains untested? Are all significant liabilities and related parties visible? Does the report reconcile with the risk register and market disclosures? What events happened since year-end? Can we pay debts as they fall due under plausible conditions? Which qualifications or dissent must be documented?*

**Audit committee versus risk/remuneration/sustainability:** A committee charter should identify membership and independence requirements applicable to the entity, scope, access to expertise, reporting lines and annual effectiveness. Committees provide scrutiny; they do not automatically acquire statutory board-reserved powers. A small association may use the full board with documented separation of duties rather than mimic a large listed company's committee architecture. AICD[TOOL, 2025/26 V1, pp. 17–23, 109–117, 125–127].

### 08.09.3 The 2016 *Fifty Matters* guide: history versus operative law

`FIFTY-2016` is **AICD, *Fifty Matters to be Considered Before Signing a Company's Financial Statements*, third edition, October 2016**, 12 pages. It asks valuable historical questions across reporting framework, controls, assets, liabilities, related parties, foreign exchange, dividends, going concern and auditors. AICD[TOOL, 2025/26 V1, pp. 47–52] contains the 2025/26 compiled teaching version. **Neither is a substitute for current Chapter 2M reporting law, ACNC/WA/CATSI requirements or up-to-date Australian Accounting Standards.** Avoid reproducing the 50 source questions as a list.

| 2016-era enquiry retained conceptually | Current-law and practice update to ask in 2026 |
|---|---|
| Financial report 'true and fair', compliance and solvency | Confirm the actual reporting entity and operative declaration under Corporations Act 2001 (Cth) ss 295–297 where applicable, **not** automatically for an ACNC charity, association or CATSI corporation. |
| Special-purpose versus general-purpose reports | Verify current AASB reporting frameworks, tier classification and changes since 2016; do not accept an historic SPFS assumption. |
| Assets, provisions and contract exposures | Test current estimates, business combinations, leases, impairment, decommissioning, financial instruments and relevant Australian Accounting Standards, rather than assume checklist coverage is comprehensive. |
| Listed-company disclosures | Reconcile financial-report events with mandatory ASX disclosure controls, reporting timetable and unaudited report verification. |
| Audit and assurance | Verify applicable auditor independence, assurance scope and unadjusted errors; consider mandatory **sustainability/climate reporting only for entities in legislative scope** and phase-in periods, not every entity in 2026. |
| Going concern and ability to pay debts | Use up-to-date cash flow, covenant terms, stressed funding and post-reporting events; historical reference to a 12-month forecast is a planning benchmark, not a universal proof of legal solvency. |

**Priority rule:** This section requires contemporaneous statutory and accounting-specialist advice at signing where scope or material judgments are uncertain. The exact AASB standard and reporting period are to be checked for the entity; no universal AASB 18 or climate commencement date is asserted. AICD[FIN, 2025/26 V1, pp. 23–49]; AICD[TOOL, 2025/26 V1, p. 47]; FIFTY-2016 pp. 4–12. ## 08.10 Five entity-specific practical governance kits

**Use instruction:** First confirm that the legal registration and capacity match a kit. Each is a separate operational route, not a claim that only one jurisdiction or regulatory system can apply to a real organisation. Every kit specifies the *ordinary corporate/committee authority* and approvals that sit outside it.

### 08.10.A ASX-listed Australian corporation

**Framework and status.** An Australian company registered under the **Corporations Act 2001 (Cth)**, the actual constitution, the **ASX Listing Rules** and, where applicable, securities, industry or prudential legislation. Material powers derive from the Act and constitution, not from AICD recommendations. ASX governance fourth-edition recommendations are a **disclose-or-explain** framework; a July 2026 proposed fifth edition was not yet operative at the verification date. Selected provisions: Corporations Act ss 180–184, 191–195, 198A, 295–297, 588G/588GA; ASX Listing Rule 3.1 on continuous disclosure and its applicable exceptions under 3.1A, which require particular legal assessment. Sources: [Corporations Act C149](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2004A00818/latest), [ASX governance status](https://www.asx.com.au/about/regulation/corporate-governance-principles-and-recommendations/asx-corporate-governance-council), checked 28 October 2026.

- **Board authority and other approvals:** Board approves major strategy, financial reporting and reserved decisions under valid powers/delegations. Verify shareholder approval where the constitution, Corporations Act or Listing Rules reserve it (for example, some capital, related-party, remuneration, significant transactions or governance matters). Audit committee review never displaces the full board's required declaration. Ensure conflicted-director notice, quorum and voting comply with the applicable rules and law.
- **Key documents:** constitution; board/committee charters; reserved-power and delegation register; continuous-disclosure policy and announcement register; D&O deed/policy; group and related-party registers; board papers/minutes; capital allocation framework; risk appetite; forecasts; auditor papers; sustainability-reporting applicability memo.
- **Recurring decisions:** financial-results and market announcements, budgets/major investment, CEO succession and incentives, capital management, material acquisitions/impairments, disclosure of performance change, assurance, cyber/safety response and board performance.
- **Financial oversight:** monitor operating cash versus accrual profit, impairment and provisions, financing and covenant headroom, expected returns relative to risk and capital employed, material judgments, climate-reporting obligations if scoped, and directors' declaration. Challenge incentives to reclassify costs or conceal deteriorating performance.
- **Common failures:** board packs that advocate only one option; unexplained cash shortfalls beneath headline earnings; slow disclosure escalation; committee-to-board information loss; rubber-stamped CEO/CFO assurances; systemic safety/culture issues; conflicts treated as an annual form rather than transaction-specific.
- **Practical questions:** *What is the market-sensitive fact and when did we become aware? What is the lawful disclosure pathway? Would the downside case breach funding capacity? Does this approval add value after capital cost? What material estimate did auditors contest? Who can reverse a delegated decision?*

**Minimal working kit:** T02, T05, T09, T14, T18, T25, T36, T38–T42. Cross-reference Files 01, 02, 03, 04 and 05.

### 08.10.B ACNC-registered charity that is a company limited by guarantee

**Framework and status.** The legal vehicle remains a company limited by guarantee under the **Corporations Act 2001 (Cth)**, with charity registration under the **ACNC Act 2012** and **ACNC Regulations 2022**. The charity's governing document, objects, member rights, grant/donor restrictions and trust arrangements are additional controls. **Governance Standard 5**, ACNC Regulations s 45.25, requires the *charity* to take reasonable steps so its Responsible People are subject to and carry out the specified duties. Some Corporations Act provisions are modified/disapplied for ACNC-registered entities; common law and certain criminal duties may continue. Do not re-import every general civil duty unqualified. [ACNC CLG guidance](https://www.acnc.gov.au/for-charities/manage-your-charity/other-regulators/companies-limited-guarantee), [Standard 5](https://www.acnc.gov.au/for-charities/manage-your-charity/governance-hub/5-duties-responsible-people), [Regulations](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2022L01301/latest), checked 28 October 2026.

- **Board authority and other approvals:** Directors oversee charitable purpose, stewardship, staff and service. Check what the constitution requires members to approve (appointments, rule amendments, winding up and specified asset decisions); check donor restrictions, grant deeds and any separate trust deed. An ACNC registration entry is not itself authority to depart from the constitution or divert restricted gifts.
- **Key documents:** ASIC and ACNC extracts; constitution and member register; charitable-purpose statement; Responsible People/conflicts records; grant and gift restriction register; program outcomes; policy and safeguarding controls; reserves and liquidity policy; management accounts; annual reporting obligations; related-party disclosures.
- **Recurring decisions:** program mix and mission performance, restricted-fund allocation, membership and appointments, fundraising and contracts, safeguarding, CEO oversight, annual information statement, audited/reviewed financial reporting where required, succession and reserves.
- **Financial oversight:** ACNC 2026 reporting size is based on **annual revenue**: small below $500,000, medium $500,000 to below $3 million, large $3 million or more. Medium charities generally need a reviewed or audited annual financial report; large charities an audited report; small charities generally have no mandatory ACNC financial-report upload, subject to other legal/governing conditions and special categories. Cash must be separated into unrestricted, legally restricted and purpose-committed amounts. Source: [ACNC 2026 guidance](https://www.acnc.gov.au/for-charities/annual-information-statement/2026-annual-information-statement-hub), reviewed 28 October 2026.
- **Common failures:** assuming 'non-profit' means tax/deductible-gift-recipient status or weak financial scrutiny; using restricted grants to fund unrelated overhead without authority; charity 'surplus' mistaken for usable cash; failing to disclose related-party service contracts; member consultation omitted; services expanded without sustainable funding.
- **Practical questions:** *Is this spend permitted by our charitable objects and funder conditions? Which members must approve? What is our unrestricted runway? Are the actual outcomes worth the full service cost? Are Responsible People trained and conflicts managed? What happens if the principal grant ceases?*

**Minimal working kit:** T01, T03, T05, T17–T20, T23, T36, T39–T41. Cross-reference File 06 §§06.03–06.15; File 01 §§01.06–01.08; File 05 (entity reporting).

### 08.10.C Western Australian incorporated association

**Framework and status.** **Associations Incorporation Act 2015 (WA)**, current consolidation 23 August 2025; **Associations Incorporation Regulations 2016 (WA)**, 1 July 2026 consolidation; *registered rules* or properly adopted model rules; any ACNC charity registration as additional overlay. This is **not** a company limited by guarantee and cannot be assumed to be governed directly by Corporations Act general director-duty provisions. WA Act ss 42–43 cover material-personal-interest disclosures and specific voting issues; ss 44–49 cover officers' duties and reliance; ss 50–51 AGM and special resolutions; s 127 concerns incurring debts. [WA Act](https://www.legislation.wa.gov.au/legislation/statutes.nsf/main_mrtitle_13715_homepage.html), [Regulations](https://www.legislation.wa.gov.au/legislation/statutes.nsf/main_mrtitle_13764_homepage.html), checked 28 October 2026.

- **Board authority and other approvals:** Management committee governs according to the Act and rules; members retain election, rules, special resolution and other specified rights. A special resolution is **not** a normal committee decision. Record eligible voters, notice, quorum, required majority and regulator lodgement/effect as applicable. Where ACNC registered, separately apply charity obligations; do not assume state/ACNC submissions substitute for each other without checking current recognition provisions.
- **Key documents:** incorporated-association register extract; rules and approved changes; members/office holders registers; committee minutes; conflict records; current financial tier assessment; annual accounts/reports; AGM documents; insurance; bank signatories; grant/venue/sponsorship agreements; volunteer and safety policies.
- **Recurring decisions:** annual general meeting within the statutory period, accounts to members, rule amendments, committee elections, service delivery, insurance, fundraising, premises/safety, grants and annual information requirements where applicable.
- **Financial oversight:** Under **WA Act s 64 and WA Regulations reg 11A** current revenue limits are **Tier 1 below $500,000; Tier 2 $500,000 to below $3 million; Tier 3 $3 million or more**, subject to statutory declarations/reclassification. Tier 1 prepares required annual statements; Tier 2 financial report and review unless an audit is lawfully required; Tier 3 financial report and audit. Apply WA Act ss 68–76, not an imported Corporations Act declaration. [WA Consumer Protection tiers](https://www.consumerprotection.wa.gov.au/inc-guide-incorporated-associations-western-australia/tiered-financial-reporting); verified 28 October 2026.
- **Common failures:** treating the president as sole decision-maker; no real committee quorum; constitution/rules not updated or supplied; volunteer cash collection without dual control; grants with undocumented restrictions; unbudgeted venue liabilities; missing member approval; financial stress disguised by future fundraising expectations.
- **Practical questions:** *What do our rules reserve to members? Are the right people eligible to vote? Which reporting tier applies this year? Can we pay committed expenses if next quarter's event fails? Who independently reconciles receipts? Was a material conflict properly disclosed and managed?*

**Minimal working kit:** T01, T06–T07, T10, T14, T19–T20, T21, T39, T41, T48. Cross-reference File 06 §§06.07, 06.11, 06.18 and File 01 §01.19.

### 08.10.D ACNC-registered CATSI corporation (not assumed to be an RNTBC)

**Framework and status.** **CATSI Act 2006 (Cth)**, compilation C2025C00681 (C27), 5 December 2025, corporation's rule book and Office of the Registrar of Indigenous Corporations (ORIC) registration; **ACNC Act/Regulations** for charity status and governance. CATSI ss 265-1, 265-5, 265-10 and 268-1 have their own duties and disclosure rules. Native title/PBC requirements **do not arise merely from CATSI or charity registration**. [CATSI Act](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2006A00124/latest), [ORIC reporting](https://www.oric.gov.au/for-corporations/reporting-and-stakeholders/annual-reporting), [ACNC charity obligations](https://www.acnc.gov.au/for-charities/manage-your-charity/governance-hub/governance-standards); checked 28 October 2026.

- **Board authority and other approvals:** Directors exercise valid CATSI/rule-book powers; members' rights under the rule book and CATSI Act may reserve director appointments, rules and other matters. Test conflicts, related-party transactions, language and accessible notice and any recognised local representative arrangements; do not invent one community's decision customs. ACNC governance is an additional charity layer, not a replacement for CATSI governance.
- **Key documents:** Indigenous corporation number and ORIC extract, current rule book, member/director records, meetings and conflict minutes, governance and grants compliance registers, charity purpose and Responsible People records, audited reports as triggered, controlled-entity and trust arrangements, cultural and stakeholder communication policy approved by this corporation.
- **Recurring decisions:** member/general meetings, elections and representation within the rule book, strategy/service programs, grants and benefit delivery, procurement/related parties, employee and board succession, regulatory reports, conflicts and financial sustainability.
- **Financial oversight:** ORIC **size classification** uses at least two of income/assets/employee tests: [ORIC corporation size](https://www.oric.gov.au/for-corporations/corporate-governance/corporation-size). **Reporting** also depends on consolidated gross operating income: [ORIC report matrix](https://admin.oric.gov.au/for-corporations/reporting-and-stakeholders/annual-reporting/what-reports-you-need-lodge). As of the verified guidance, all non-exempt corporations lodge a general report; financial/audit and directors' reports depend on size and income, with specified grant-based accommodations. ORIC-registered charities generally **do not file an ACNC Annual Information Statement**, but still must satisfy relevant ACNC governance and other obligations: [ACNC annual reports](https://www.acnc.gov.au/for-charities/manage-your-charity/obligations-acnc/reporting-annually-acnc/annual-financial-reports).
- **Common failures:** using ACNC size thresholds in place of ORIC reporting matrix; assuming Indigenous ownership alone determines corporate form; failing to test member qualification or related-party contracts; allowing program cash commitments beyond grant funding; making cultural legitimacy claims without the organisation's authorisation.
- **Practical questions:** *What is the exact rule-book decision? Who are the members entitled to vote? Has a candidate/member conflict been handled under CATSI? Is the expenditure lawful under the grant and charity purpose? Which ORIC reports are due this year? What feedback mechanism has our own membership authorised?*

**Minimal working kit:** T01, T03, T14, T17, T19–T21, T23, T39, T41, T46. Cross-reference File 07 §§07.02–07.07, 07.13–07.16; File 06 §§06.04, 06.16.

### 08.10.E Registered native title body corporate (RNTBC)

**Framework and status.** A CATSI corporation that is the nominated prescribed body corporate under a native title determination and is registered as an RNTBC. Apply **CATSI Act 2006**, **Native Title Act 1993**, **Native Title (Prescribed Bodies Corporate) Regulations 1999**, current rule book, the particular determination, applicable Indigenous Land Use Agreements (ILUAs), section 31 agreements, trust instruments and other obligations. PBC regulations version F2024C00961 (C05), 14 October 2024, regs **8, 8A, 8B, 9 and 10**, reviewed 28 October 2026. [PBC regulations](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F1999B00146/latest); [ORIC decision-making guidance, 12 October 2026](https://www.oric.gov.au/for-corporations/decision-making/decision-making-RNTBCs).

- **Board authority: ordinary corporate matters.** Directors govern strategy, staffing, budget, risk, operations, records and delegated commercial activity under CATSI and rule book, subject to separate member rights. The RNTBC may act as **trustee** or **agent**; identify which, and do not conflate the corporation's assets with held native title rights or separate trust moneys.
- **Additional rights: common law holders.** For **native title decisions** and specified **compensation applications**, the RNTBC must identify the affected common law holders, consult and obtain consent in the way the regulations require, follow lawful directions, and prepare the required certificate. Some high-/low-level decision pathways, lawful alternatives and standing instructions have technical exceptions: evaluate transaction-specific scope and advice. **Common law holders need not be corporate members**, and a member vote cannot be presumed to replace their consent. Other legally required ILUA/future-act procedures may also apply.
- **Key documents:** native title determination map and schedule; RNTBC trustee/agent status; ORIC register; rule book; common law holder identification process authorised for this matter; engagement notices, interpreters/access support if needed; legal advice; certified holder decisions and certificate under reg 9; agreement/benefit-trust register; title, custody and restriction reconciliations; conflicts and execution authority.
- **Recurring decisions:** corporate reports and elections; native title-related agreements and negotiations; holder instructions and compensation claims; beneficiary/rights-holder engagement; land and data stewardship; grant funding, contracts, benefits distributions under relevant trusts, resourcing and disputes.
- **Financial oversight:** use CATSI/ORIC reporting rules and, when charity registered, the applicable ACNC overlay. Track corporate unrestricted cash *separately* from grant-restricted, held-in-trust, compensation and other ring-fenced resources; test entitlement, trustee approvals, distributions, cash/runway and counterparty exposure. Native title itself is not assumed available to meet the corporation's debts.
- **Common failures:** directors signing an ILUA on assumed corporate authority before holder consent; excluding non-member holders; treating a community meeting as proof of a legally compliant decision without verifying the required process; using historical broad consent where the current decision falls outside its conditions; conflating corporation, trustee and beneficiary; failure to document affected-holder classes, consultation and certification.
- **Practical questions:** *What precise legal decision is being taken? Which native title rights, holder classes and land/waters are affected? Is it high or low level, a standing-instructions decision or compensation application? What consultation process lawfully applies? Where is the evidence of consent, directions and certificate? Is a separate trustee resolution needed? Does the board have valid execution authority after, rather than instead of, consent?*

**Original two-track sign-off:** `Track 1: corporation rule-book authority, conflicts, budget, solvency, contract execution` **and independently** `Track 2: native title/compensation decision classification, affected holders, consultation, consent, certificate and any further agreement-specific rights`. Record separate evidence and gate both before execution; independent native title legal advice is appropriate for consequential transactions. No assumed Aboriginal cultural practice is prescribed. Source: PBC Regulations 1999 regs 8–10 and ORIC guidance, checked 28 October 2026. **Minimal working kit:** T01, T03, T09, T14, T17, T21–T25, T29, T39–T41, T46–T47. Cross-reference File 07 §§07.09–07.18; File 01 §§01.03, 01.06, 01.19.

## 08.11 Practical reconstruction: the 48 director tools (T01–T48)

**Entry contract.** Each entry is independent and presents nine functions: exact AICD source; governance purpose; use trigger; **new** actionable procedure/questions; necessary evidence; decision owner; explicit cross-entity adaptation; legal/interpretive limits; and canonical corpus cross-reference. `Commercial` means an ordinary company (including a listed company, subject to additional rules); `charity/association` distinguishes an ACNC charity and a state incorporated association under their respective laws; `CATSI/RNTBC` identifies the CATSI overlay and adds native title holder rights **only where RNTBC status and decision class require**. Descriptions of original AICD subject matter are attributed; reconstructed processes are editorial originals.

### [08.T01] Annual board calendar

- **Source:** *Director tool: Annual board calendar*, AICD[TOOL, 2025/26 V1, p. 3] (source tool pp. 3–11).
- **Purpose:** Prevent critical board obligations, strategic questions and committee reviews being crowded out by routine business.
- **When:** Establish the next annual cycle and revisit after a major risk/event, change of year-end, new regulation or changed business/mission.
- **Original procedure:** (1) List actual filings, audit/report cycles, AGMs and hard deadlines; (2) add material strategy, risk appetite, capital and CEO decisions; (3) schedule preparatory committee work and stakeholder/holder engagement *before* decision dates; (4) estimate preparation and discussion time; (5) reserve meeting capacity for exceptions; (6) check closure each quarter. - **Evidence:** Notice, attendance and meeting records, current constitution/rule book, conflicts register, papers and attachments, applicable advice, consent/approval evidence, action tracker.
- **Owner:** Chair verifies the board's record; secretary or nominated officer drafts and preserves it; participating directors check accuracy; board or committee approves minutes according to the applicable process.
- **Applicability:** **Commercial:** observe Corporations Act and constitution, including any special conflict or circulating-resolution rules. **Charity/association:** comply with member/committee meeting and record requirements of the governing statute and rules. **CATSI/RNTBC:** preserve lawful resolutions and separately record common law holder consultation/consent and certification where required; ordinary RNTBC board minutes do not replace those rights.
- **Limits/misuse:** A well-written minute is evidence of process, not conclusive proof that directors fulfilled duties. Do not silently omit material dissent, retrospectively rewrite facts or presume a model's conflict ruling is lawful for every entity. Handle privileged and sensitive information appropriately.

### [08.T40] Sample board paper

- **Source:** *Director tool: Sample board paper*, AICD[TOOL, 2025/26 V1, p. 121].
- **Purpose:** Convert an executive recommendation into an intelligible and challengeable board decision with clear authority, alternatives and consequences.
- **When:** Submitting any material board decision; changing strategy, risk limits or policy; approving capital, contracts, appointments or public commitments.
- **Original procedure:** Require the sponsor to complete a concise **decision brief**: `decision sought and required authority; the problem and its materiality; available options including no action; rationale and disconfirming evidence; expected outcomes and costs; downside, sensitivity and cash consequences; strategy and appetite alignment; legal/ethical and stakeholder gates; implementation owner; success and stop triggers`. Present a draft resolution **only after** verifying legal and member/holder approvals. For financially material proposals, append a reconciled base/downside analysis and affordability test. Ask, “What would cause us to reject this recommendation?” Send a paper back if directors cannot identify the actual choice or monitoring plan.
- **Evidence:** Governing instrument and power schedule, options analysis, independent advice, financial model and cash forecasts, risk and control review, conflicts, consultation/consent records, implementation plan.
- **Owner:** Executive sponsor is accountable for accuracy; CEO or company secretary coordinates board submission; board owns its reserved judgment and approved limits.
- **Applicability:** **Commercial:** address capital value, continuous disclosure, related parties and financial capacity. **Charity/association:** evidence mission, restricted funds, member power and service impact. **CATSI/RNTBC:** confirm grant/native title money restrictions, affected holder rights and any decision-making certificate before implementation.
- **Limits/misuse:** The AICD source provides illustrative page/line limits and suggested headings, **not mandatory board-paper length or legal prerequisites**. This original alternative does not reproduce its proprietary pro forma. Concision must not suppress an adverse scenario or minority view.

### [08.T41] Solvency: key questions for directors

- **Source:** *Director tool: Solvency—Key questions for directors*, AICD[TOOL, 2025/26 V1, p. 124].
- **Purpose:** Prevent a false inference that reported profit, positive equity or an undrawn bank facility necessarily means debts can be paid **when legally due**.
- **When:** Every periodic cash review; signing financial statements; approving new debt/large spend; monitoring deterioration or dealing with creditor pressure.
- **Original procedure:** Require an **obligation-by-date cash ladder** covering payroll, tax, suppliers, interest, lease payments, debt maturities and contingencies. Reconcile opening bank cash to daily/weekly near-term flows and 13-week, 12-month and longer forecasts as warranted. Separate unconditional cash and reliable committed facilities from speculative funding or restricted balances. Check forecast errors against actual cash each period; test delayed receipts, creditor acceleration, covenant breach, lost funding and inability to roll facilities. Document whether payment arrangements are *actually agreed*, not merely assumed. If a material shortfall is credible, urgently obtain restructuring/insolvency advice, constrain new obligations and review statutory duties and options.
- **Evidence:** Bank reconciliations, aged payables/receivables, cash forecast and previous forecast accuracy, loan agreements, covenant compliance, grant/trust restrictions, tax arrears, contracts and creditor correspondence.
- **Owner:** CFO/treasurer prepares and validates; CEO escalates; each director must exercise judgment; board decides lawful response and obtains specialist advice as needed.
- **Applicability:** **Commercial:** statutory solvent-trading analysis under applicable Corporations Act provisions, including any fact-specific safe-harbour question. **Charity/association:** unrestricted cash and payment commitments matter; WA law has its own debt/committee provisions. **CATSI/RNTBC:** assess CATSI insolvent-trading duties and **separately segregate funds that cannot lawfully be used to meet general debts**.
- **Limits/misuse:** A 13-week forecast is a management tool, not a statutory solvency test. A profitable organisation may be insolvent; accounting insolvency or negative working capital is not by itself a legal finding. For companies, **Corporations Act 2001 (Cth) ss 95A, 588G, 588GA, 588H**, compilation C2026C00432; **CATSI Act 2006 (Cth) s 531-1**, compilation C2025C00681; **Associations Incorporation Act 2015 (WA) s 127**, as consolidated 23 August 2025, all checked 28 October 2026. Seek advice on exact triggering rules and relief.
- **Corpus:** 08.08–08.10; File 04 §§04.05, 04.09–04.10, 04.14–04.15 (cash, forecasts, ratios, solvency and distress; cross-file reconciliation pending); File 05 (financial sign-off); tools T18–T20, T36.

### [08.T43] Testing your risk personality

- **Source:** *Director tool: Testing your risk personality*, AICD[TOOL, 2025/26 V1, p. 128]. The source references Nicholson et al. (2005) and a personal risk-taking index.
- **Purpose:** Prompt self-awareness of how personal attitudes to uncertainty may colour directors' strategic judgments and debate.
- **When:** Board induction or development; after unusually polarised risk discussions; before material strategy or appetite reviews.
- **Original procedure:** Ask each director privately to describe two decisions: one where they moved too quickly and one where they resisted a justified risk. Record perceived probability, consequences, decision time and information used. Next, compare responses to **the same hypothetical organisational scenario**, independently before discussion. The chair asks: “What explains our divergence: evidence, values, consequences, experience or appetite?” Agree a collective evidence threshold and decision process rather than a preferred personality score. Revisit after a real decision to see whether predicted disagreement patterns occurred.
- **Evidence:** Voluntary self-reflections, anonymised scenario responses where useful, prior decision reviews, approved risk appetite, and meeting debriefs.
- **Owner:** Individual directors control their reflections; chair facilitates constructive discussion; board sets corporate appetite and decisions, not an individual's personality.
- **Applicability:** **Commercial:** investment, credit and safety trade-offs. **Charity/association:** appetite for financial loss versus continuity of services and volunteer capacity. **CATSI/RNTBC:** contextualise collective and cultural decision rights through authorised processes; do not infer cultural norms from personal answers.
- **Limits/misuse:** The source's personal questionnaire is **not reproduced** and should not be presented as a clinical diagnosis, validated hiring screen, independence test or predictor of directors' legal compliance. Do not collect sensitive personal histories unnecessarily or treat high/low scores as competence. A board's legally constrained risk tolerance is not an average of individual preferences.
- **Corpus:** 08.05–08.07; File 02 §§02.04–02.05, 02.09; File 03 (risk appetite); tools T09, T26, T45–T47.

### [08.T44] The Company Directors' Corporate Governance Framework™

- **Source:** *Director tool: The Company Directors' Corporate Governance Framework™*, AICD[TOOL, 2025/26 V1, p. 129]. AICD describes four domains: **individual, board, organisational and stakeholder**.
- **Purpose:** Diagnose gaps created when a board concentrates on compliance documentation but neglects director contribution, collective decisions, organisational outcomes or external legitimacy.
- **When:** Annual governance review; board evaluation; serious process failure; redesigning committee or stakeholder engagement arrangements.
- **Original procedure:** Conduct an **independent four-lens diagnostic**, not a copy of the AICD diagram. For the *individual* lens test preparation, candour, ethics and learning. For the *collective board* lens test trust, role clarity, dissent and decision accountability. For the *organisational* lens test strategy, cash, risk, control assurance, management succession and delivery. For the *stakeholder* lens test material impacts, truthful communication and lawful rights. For each, record one demonstrated strength, one verifiable weakness, the effect on decision quality, the owner of improvement and the evidence that will show a change in six months.
- **Evidence:** Agendas, minutes, strategy and financial records, risk reports, director development plans, culture/speak-up information, member/stakeholder records and valid consent evidence.
- **Owner:** Whole board leads governance; chair coordinates evaluation; governance committee or independent reviewer supports assessment; management responds to assigned improvements.
- **Applicability:** **Commercial:** investor and market integrity plus broader stakeholders. **Charity/association:** beneficiaries, members, volunteer integrity and purposes. **CATSI/RNTBC:** corporate and common law holder rights must be mapped separately; neither “stakeholder engagement” nor the diagram replaces a legally required holder decision.
- **Limits/misuse:** This is an **attributed AICD proprietary framework**, not a legislative governance standard; its graphic is not reproduced. A positive score in one domain cannot offset an invalid decision in another. Operational effectiveness and cultural legitimacy require their own evidence and context.
- **Corpus:** 08.01–08.05, 08.10; File 01 §01.03, File 02 §§02.01–02.03, 02.16–02.18; File 07 (legitimacy and holder rights); tools T02, T07, T23–T24.

### [08.T45] Understanding director styles: 'Convincer' strategies

- **Source:** *Director tool: Understanding director styles—‘Convincer’ strategies*, AICD[TOOL, 2025/26 V1, p. 131]. The source discusses differences in what people regard as persuasive evidence and how much time or repetition they need.
- **Purpose:** Make substantive challenge accessible to different directors and prevent urgency, status or presentation style from determining whose argument is heard.
- **When:** Designing board papers, chairing a difficult decision, changing meeting formats or integrating new directors.
- **Original procedure:** Ask directors **what evidence would change their view**, allowing a concise written paper, numerical table, discussion with the subject expert, site visit, demonstration or several comparative examples. Circulate the *same facts and balanced alternatives* to everyone. Use an initial meeting for difficult questions and a later approval if time lawfully permits; if time is short, state why and offer an explicit challenge opportunity. At the decision, test whether each director understands alternatives, implications and dissent, regardless of communication preference. Afterward evaluate which information channels improved accuracy rather than just persuasion.
- **Evidence:** Board-paper feedback, accessibility needs, meeting preparation records, relevant demonstrations/site notes, expert advice and contemporaneous decision record.
- **Owner:** Chair and secretary facilitate; management presents complete information; each director remains accountable for examining it and participating lawfully.
- **Applicability:** **Commercial:** suit complex investor, financing and technology decisions. **Charity/association:** offer readable information suited to volunteer board availability. **CATSI/RNTBC:** use accessible, appropriately authorised engagement processes and interpreters/advice where needed; do not invent community-specific methods or substitute communication style for consent.
- **Limits/misuse:** The categories in the source are **reflection prompts**, not validated or fixed personality types. Do not excuse failure to read material, infer a director's preferences from identity, or use persuasion techniques to suppress uncomfortable evidence. “Convincing” is subordinate to lawful, well-informed collective judgment.

### [08.T46] What is your risk competency?

- **Source:** *Director tool: What is your risk competency?*, AICD[TOOL, 2025/26 V1, p. 133]. Source credits Geoffrey Kiel (2022) and considers formal learning, senior risk-management experience and industry experience.
- **Purpose:** Distinguish the presence of a risk committee or an impressive résumé from the board's **demonstrated capacity** to oversee its material risks.
- **When:** Recruitment, board evaluation, changing risk exposure, post-incident review or review of committee composition.
- **Original procedure:** Build a competency-evidence table with one row per **risk critical to the entity** (solvency, safety, cyber, funding, legal compliance, native title decisions where relevant). - **Owner:** Chair/nomination committee coordinates; individual directors address competence gaps; board ensures appropriate collective capability.

### [08.T47] What is your strategic competency?

- **Source:** *Director tool: What is your strategic competency?*, AICD[TOOL, 2025/26 V1, p. 136]. Source credits Geoffrey Kiel (2022) and considers strategy education, senior strategic experience and sector experience.

### [08.T48] WHS actions

- **Source:** *Director tool: WHS actions*, AICD[TOOL, 2025/26 V1, p. 139]. The source acknowledges an AICD 2012 safety action list and includes psychosocial hazards.
- **Purpose:** Move board work health and safety (WHS) oversight from injury statistics and policy sign-off to evidence of identification, competent control, verification and accountability.
- **When:** Induction; periodic safety oversight; new operations/site acquisition; serious incident; material psychosocial hazard; contractor or volunteer exposure.
- **Original procedure:** Ask management to identify the most serious **foreseeable safety exposures**, who may be affected and who is the legal duty holder. Obtain a critical-control map: `hazard | possible severe harm | preventive control | control owner | how tested | last failure/near miss | next test | escalation trigger`. Examine workforce/contractor consultation, hazard reporting and psychosocial-risk controls, not only lost-time injury rates. Require independent or site-level verification of a sample of critical controls, evidence of remediation, director learning and escalation thresholds. Review CEO/senior management incentives for perverse safety signals and schedule visits focused on inquiry rather than staged reassurance. For an imminent danger, route immediately to those able to protect people and obtain legal advice on notification requirements.
- **Evidence:** WHS legal applicability map, inspections and incident trends, hazard and psychosocial assessments, worker consultation, contractor records, control-assurance tests, training, critical-action close-out, regulator notices, emergency exercises.
- **Owner:** Employer/person conducting a business or undertaking (PCBU) and workers have applicable duties; management implements risk controls; officers and directors discharge their **own** duties where the law applies; board oversees the system and leadership.
- **Applicability:** **Commercial:** apply the WHS statute of the place/sector, including officers' duties. **Charity/association:** volunteers, events and sites warrant fact-specific assessment of the organisation's PCBU and officer status; do not assume all volunteers are identical employees. **CATSI/RNTBC:** assess remote operations, members/volunteers, contractors and context-specific risks through actual engagement and evidence, without claiming universal cultural practices.
- **Limits/misuse:** **Work Health and Safety Act 2020 (WA) ss 19, 27**, consolidated 28 May 2026 (00-h0-02), establish PCBU primary duty and officer due-diligence provisions within WA scope (verified 28 October 2026), but not every entity's operations fall under WA jurisdiction. A checklist does not discharge duties, and zero injuries does not establish control effectiveness. Avoid using site visits as a substitute for independent assurance or worker consultation.

### [08.C03] CASE-03 — Slater & Gordon: acquisition-led growth, earnings quality and governance

1. **Title and exact source:** *Slater & Gordon Limited*, AICD[GAPP, 2025/26 V1, pp. 20–25].
2. **Case type:** **[HIST] Historical Australian listed-company narrative**, assembled in teaching appendices from public reporting; not itself a court judgment and not an adjudicated finding about individual directors.
3. **Relevant source facts:** The law firm listed in 2007; its early public form foregrounded obligations to courts and clients alongside investors. Expansion through acquisitions intensified. In 2015 it bought Quindell's legal-services activities for an approximately **A$1.3 billion** transaction and raised approximately **A$890 million** in equity; scrutiny followed over work-in-progress valuation, accounting, debt and forecast cash conversion. AICD recounts guidance deterioration, a substantial UK-related write-down, lender pressure and a 2017 debt-for-equity recapitalisation through which pre-existing shareholders retained a small post-transaction interest. Monetary figures, dates and narrative are source-era historical, not a current valuation.
4. **Central issue:** A high-growth strategy and reported earnings can mask deteriorating cash quality, leverage and execution capacity; professional obligations create additional constraints on revenue/capital growth.
5. **Principles and law:** Statutory company care and loyalty, continuous disclosure when applicable, accounts' true-and-fair presentation, director sign-off and proper acquisition due diligence. 6. **Board information needed:** Transaction-level cash-flow diligence; UK integration plan; contingent case outcomes and WIP ageing; report-to-cash bridge; customer/client obligations; purchase accounting, goodwill impairment and sensitivity; leverage policy and covenant headroom; cash down-side and refinancing alternatives; legal professional duties.
7. **Choices and trade-offs:** Slow or reject the acquisition (protect liquidity, forego scale); price/restructure acquisition with contingent consideration (risk sharing); raise more durable equity (dilution); buy and integrate quickly (potential scale but highest execution/leverage exposure); restructure after stress (survival against steep legacy dilution).

### [08.C18] CASE-18 — James Hardie and Centro: actual court holdings on director scrutiny

1. **Title and exact source:** AICD[TOOL, 2025/26 V1, **pp. 95–96**] for two historical lessons lists; p. 97 starts **a different tool** and must not be represented as part of the case-law text. Primary judicial sources: *Australian Securities and Investments Commission v Hellicar* **[2012] HCA 17**, [High Court judgment record](https://www.hcourt.gov.au/cases-and-judgments/judgments/judgments-1998-current/australian-securities-and-investments-commission-v-hellicar); *Shafron v ASIC* **[2012] HCA 18**, [High Court judgment](https://www.hcourt.gov.au/cases-and-judgments/judgments/judgments-1998-current/shafron-v-australian-securities-and-investments-commission); *ASIC v Healey* **[2011] FCA 717** (Centro), officially summarised in [Federal Court Annual Report 2011–12, Appendix 8](https://www.fedcourt.gov.au/about/corporate-information/annual-reports/2011-12/ar2012.pdf) and its subsequent penalty decision *ASIC v Healey (No 2)* **[2011] FCA 1003**, 31 August 2011.
2. **Case type:** **[JUD] Two separate groups of actual judicial proceedings**, not fictional studies, not APRA inquiry findings and not interchangeable holdings.
3. **Relevant facts and procedural history:** **James Hardie:** an asbestos-liability restructuring was accompanied by a misleading 2001 ASX announcement about funding sufficiency. In *ASIC v Macdonald (No 11)* [2009] NSWSC 287 the NSW Supreme Court found director breaches. The NSW Court of Appeal set aside material findings in *Morley v ASIC* [2010] NSWCA 331; **the High Court in *Hellicar* allowed ASIC's appeal in May 2012**, giving substantial evidentiary effect to formally adopted board minutes and rejecting the adverse inference claimed from ASIC's failure to call one solicitor; **remaining appeal issues on relief/penalty were remitted**, so the High Court decision should not be paraphrased as itself determining all final penalties. In *Shafron* the High Court dismissed the officer's appeal, treating his company-secretary/general-counsel responsibilities as a composite role for s 180(1). **Centro:** 2007 accounts of listed Centro entities incorrectly classified approximately **A$1.5bn** of short-term debt and omitted disclosure of material post-balance-date guarantees. In *Healey* [2011] FCA 717, Middleton J found directors had failed to discharge duties of scrutiny and reasonable steps under the applicable Corporations Act provisions, notwithstanding reliance on finance professionals and auditors; penalty/relief was separately addressed in **[2011] FCA 1003**.
4. **Central issue:** A director cannot discharge personally required scrutiny of a major market announcement or financial report simply because management, another committee or an expert handled the draft.
5. **Applicable holding and limits:** In *Hellicar*, contemporaneous approved minutes materially supported proof the board approved the draft; High Court's immediate disposition involved appellate error and remittal. In *Shafron*, actual assigned responsibilities, not one formal job label alone, defined care. In *Healey*, directors had to read and understand financial reports sufficiently to challenge obvious inconsistencies and ensure necessary corrections; *the court did not require all directors to become expert accountants*. These were civil proceedings applying the relevant law and facts, not universal rules that any adverse outcome is negligence.
6. **Board information needed:** Exact announcement drafts and approval path; ASIC/ASX communications; evidence of how minutes were approved; management representation and auditor reports; debt maturity/covenant schedule, subsequent-events guarantees and complete board packs.
7. **Choices and trade-offs:** Approve on assurance alone (fast but legally fragile); require independent explanation and redline of changed material claims; defer approval while outstanding matters resolved; provide qualified disclosure or market update within lawful reporting/disclosure timelines.
8. **Director-level reasoning:** Demand drafts *before* approval, ask clarifying financial questions, state unresolved material assumptions and correct errors. Minute **what** was approved, by whom, when and on what evidenced basis. A director must neither rely blindly nor substitute their own unaudited technical judgment for an appropriately challenged expert opinion. A board's urgent market obligations may require an accurate limited announcement rather than silence.
9. **Transferability:** Financial diligence and truthful recording generalise to charities, associations and CATSI corporations, but ss 180/344 and the cited holdings apply to their **actual company/managed-investment contexts**; transplanting the same statutory hooks to CATSI or WA association directors is wrong. RNTBC consent certificates additionally require independent native title rights evidence.
10. **Limits and connections:** The AICD **tool** is a historical commentary, not the ratio decidendi; primary court records control. Do not imply Centro directors were convicted criminally by this civil decision, or that *Hellicar* was the sole James Hardie litigation. Cross-reference 08.08–08.09, 08.10.A, 08.15–08.16; tools [08.T18], [08.T21], [08.T28], [08.T39]; Files 01 §§01.07, 01.09, 05 §§05.03, 05.14–05.15.

### [08.SIM-A] Simulation A — ASX issuer: cyber incident immediately before results

**Fact pattern [SIM].** Northern Quay Services Limited (NQS), a fictional Australian ASX-listed services company, will announce results for the year ended 30 September 2026 in November. At 07:00 on **29 October 2026**, its security team detects unauthorised access to a customer-identity database and disruption of its service portal. An outside forensic expert provisionally estimates that up to **84,000** customer records might have been accessed; the evidentiary range remains broad. A threat actor demands payment. A journalist requests comment; management has not determined if information was actually exfiltrated. The chief information security officer's earlier report described backups as compliant, but restoration tests were not completed. Several large customers have contractually required notification triggers that have yet to be classified. The CEO proposes waiting five business days for certainty before informing the board or market. That is **not** a defensible general rule.

**Fictional financial assumptions — A$ million unless otherwise specified.** Annual revenue **620**; expected profit before tax **61**; cash at bank **52**, of which **8** is unavailable for general treasury purposes under assumed contract restrictions; baseline accessible cash **44**; minimum board liquidity reserve **25**. Forensic/legal/containment cost estimate **2.0**; plausible remediation/customer losses **3.0–12.0**, with an **unmodelled severe tail** beyond this range. Assumed potential insurance recovery **0–4** subject to policy exclusions, retention and proof — **no recovery is committed or bankable now**. Immediate unrestricted cash headroom = `52 − 8 − 25 = 19`. Against an assumed A$12m cash downside, headroom declines to **A$7m**, before underlying business cash movements; at an A$20m incident-only cost it would be **negative A$1m**. These arithmetic illustrations are **not** evidence of actual value-at-risk, IFRS provision or disclosure materiality.

**Board-paper summary [SIM].** **Purpose:** preserve people/data, services, legal compliance and an accurate market/financial-reporting narrative. **Decision required now:** establish incident command and independent oversight; authorise emergency expenditure within defined limits; approve disclosure decision process; preserve market integrity; determine whether financial sign-off can continue on schedule. **Proposed board position:** immediate escalation and containment, not premature declaration of the event as definitely immaterial or definitely an eligible privacy breach.

**Competing courses of action:**

| Route | Potential advantage | Principal failure mode | Decision threshold |
|---|---|---|---|
| A1: CEO waits until full forensic report | Avoids an unsupported early estimate | May breach ASX continuous disclosure, customer notice duties and director supervision obligations; lost containment time | **Not recommended** as a default; the market decision must be ongoing and fact-specific. |
| A2: Immediate incident management; give market what is known when LR 3.1 applies, update subsequently | Protects stakeholders while preserving accuracy | Initial statement may omit later-discovered facts; requires disciplined updates | **Preferred conditional route**; legal/ASX advisers assess current material information and LR 3.1A exception continuously. |
| A3: Halt trading while assessing and preparing a statement | Can preserve orderly price formation | Halt not automatic, not a substitute for disclosure, may be unavailable or time-limited | Seek ASX agreement only if needed and appropriate, with clear release timetable. |
| A4: Pay ransom without proper review | Purported fast restoration | No guarantee, possible sanctions/AML/legal/ethical risk, extortion and reporting consequences | **No authority by default**; specialist legal review and a board-reserved decision if ever considered. |

**Missing information and first board requests:** incident time line and independent confidence rating; systems affected and service continuity; personal information categories and sensitivity; scope and likely *serious harm*; actual exfiltration evidence; restored-backup integrity; ransom demand; third-party and cloud processor exposures; cyber policy and counsel conflict checks; material customer contract notices; cyber and litigation cash scenarios; auditor's cut-off questions; audit-close access to reliable records; rumours/journalist statements; whether regulated critical infrastructure, APRA or other sector rules actually apply. The board should **not** routinely circulate detailed compromised personal data in ordinary board packs.

**Authority and decision-rights analysis [LAW + ANA]:**

- NQS is assumed to be a conventional Corporations Act public listed company. Under ss **180–184** directors and officers must exercise relevant duties; management operates the incident-command process under valid delegation; the **board retains governance oversight**, reserved capital/disclosure decisions and required report approvals. Directors do not personally conduct forensics.
- **ASX Listing Rule 3.1** requires immediate disclosure of information that a reasonable person would expect materially to affect securities price or value once the entity is aware, **subject to all conditions of LR 3.1A** where applicable. The inquiry evolves: the information available at 07:00 may differ from that available after the journalist's approach or confirmed exfiltration. Materiality is not measured solely as a percentage of EBIT. Guidance Note 8 includes a cyber-specific example and explains a changing disclosure position as facts and confidentiality change. [ASX, *Guidance Note 8 – Continuous Disclosure*, edition published 27 May 2024](https://www.asx.com.au/content/dam/asx/rules-guidance-notes-waivers/asx-listing-rules/guidance-notes/gn08-continuous-disclosure.pdf); [Australian Cyber Security Centre, listed-entity obligation](https://www.cyber.gov.au/srp/cyber-incident-materially-affecting-the-price-or-value-of-the-securities-of-an-asx-listed-entity). A trading halt is a separate ASX process, not a universal safe harbour.
- Under **Privacy Act 1988 (Cth) Part IIIC**, if NQS is a covered entity and an eligible data breach is suspected, take all reasonable steps to complete the assessment within **30 calendar days**; an eligible breach requires likely serious harm not effectively prevented by remedial action, with OAIC/individual notification **as soon as practicable** once the statutory threshold is met. Thirty days is *not* automatic permission to postpone containment or a confirmed notification. [OAIC, NDB Scheme](https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/notifiable-data-breaches/preventing-preparing-for-and-responding-to-data-breaches/data-breach-preparation-and-response/part-4-notifiable-data-breach-ndb-scheme).
- **Cyber Security Act 2024 Part 3** and the **Cyber Security (Ransomware Payment Reporting) Rules 2025** impose a distinct **72-hour reporting deadline after an in-scope ransomware payment**, not for every cyber incident. NQS's assumed revenue may engage the business turnover threshold if the other legal conditions and actual payment occur; do **not** treat the absence of a payment as a reportable payment. [Current 2025 Rules](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2025L00278/latest).
- The 30 September financial statements need analysis under the **then-applicable AASB 110** (adjusting versus non-adjusting event), **AASB 137** (provision/contingent liability), AASB 9 or other standards if relevant to impairments/recoverability, and reporting/audit requirements. A cyber incident discovered after balance date is **not automatically an adjusting event**; whether it reveals conditions existing at balance date requires evidence. The audit committee must oversee without relieving the full board of its required financial-report opinion and, where required, directors' declaration (Corporations Act s 295).

**Board discussion prompts:** What do we *know*, infer and not know? Could an investor reasonably price this differently even without a quantified loss? Have confidentiality and market speculation changed? Can we maintain essential customer services without compounding harm? What is our approved cyber risk appetite versus actual untested restoration capability? Are customer data and third-party systems legally in scope? What cannot the auditor rely on until evidence is restored? Which directors, executives and independent specialists can give credible assurance?

**Model director reasoning [ANA].** It is possible to lack proof of exfiltration while still having material, actionable disclosure information. The board should require a live market-disclosure log, counsel review, and controls on inconsistent staff/media statements. Treat cash downside as a financing scenario, not as a pre-approved accounting provision. Record dissent concerning quality of evidence, and prohibit management from using audit timetable pressure to narrow investigation scope. Risk appetite should never convert unlawful data processing or intentional misleading disclosures into permitted commercial risk.

**Illustrative resolution and conditions [SIM].** Subject to constitution/quorum, the board (i) notes notification at **07:00 on 29 October 2026** and identifies the incident commander; (ii) delegates up to **A$2m [fictional]** for immediate containment/independent advisers with CEO/CFO joint authorisation and daily reporting, subject to emergency exceptions under policy; (iii) directs legal/company secretary and market disclosure committee to test LR 3.1/3.1A on every material fact change and make any required ASX announcement without waiting for the next ordinary meeting; (iv) requires a privacy/contract/regulatory notification matrix; (v) directs audit committee and auditor to reassess accounting, evidence and reporting timetables; (vi) reserves any ransom, acquisition, extraordinary borrowing, material settlement and final reporting declarations to the proper decision body; (vii) reconvenes within **24 hours [fictional board condition]** and then at least daily during material uncertainty.

**Action/evidence record [SIM]:** `NQS-INC-01`: incident owner CISO/CEO (immediate); forensic scope and preservation independent lead (same day); ASX decision log general counsel/company secretary (each material update); NDB assessment data-protection counsel (prompt; statutory clock tracked); recovery and customer protection COO (daily); liquidity stress CFO (24h); financial/audit sign-off pathway audit chair/auditor (48h); approved communication versions company secretary (controlled); after-action root-cause and independent validation risk chair (30/90 days **illustrative**, never substitutes for legal deadlines). Escalate confirmed widespread exfiltration, journalist publication, loss of critical service, incomplete records, covenant headroom below board limit or failure to meet notification duties. Cross-references: 08.08, 08.10.A, 08.12 [08.C07], [08.C08], [08.C09], [08.C18]; Files 01, 03, 05.

### [08.SIM-B] Simulation B — WA charity / association: expansion versus safeguarding and unrestricted cash

**Fact pattern [SIM].** BridgePath Family Support Association Incorporated is a **fictional WA incorporated association** which is **also assumed** to have current ACNC registered-charity status. Its 10-member committee governs a local service for young people and families. A large government donor offers a conditional **A$1.0m** restricted grant to expand into two districts within three months. Staff have reported safeguarding process gaps and incomplete volunteer screening; an existing complaint is under independent investigation. A committee member's brother owns a property proposed for the new programme. The chair says that “we have more than a million in the bank” and proposes immediate approval.

**Fictional cash data — A$ million and monthly timing.** At the decision date total bank cash **1.40** = **0.90** assumed legally/contractually restricted to existing programmes + **0.50** unrestricted cash before already due commitments; baseline ongoing net unrestricted operating cash **–0.07 per month** (all unrestricted receipts less all existing unrestricted payments); expansion setup payment **0.35 upfront** and incremental unfunded recurring net cash **–0.12 per month**. Minimum unrestricted buffer set by this fictional board policy **0.25**; the proposed **A$1.0m** grant is **not yet signed or available** and would be restricted to specified services even if received. Without expansion, accounting for no further timing changes, simple cash-to-zero **0.50/0.07 = 7.14 months**, but buffer breach occurs after only `(0.50 − 0.25)/0.07 = 3.57 months`. If expansion begins and the setup amount is paid, remaining unrestricted cash is `0.50 − 0.35 = 0.15`, **already below the board's A$0.25m buffer**. At combined burn `0.07 + 0.12 = 0.19/month`, simple cash exhaustion would occur in `0.15/0.19 = 0.79 months`, ignoring debt timings. These figures are **fictional stress screens, not forecasts or statutory insolvency findings**.

**Board-paper summary [SIM].** **Strategic purpose:** improve access for vulnerable families, but maintain lawful purpose, safe services and financing capacity. **Decision:** approve, modify, defer or reject the expansion and resolve immediate safeguarding and cash control exposure. **Recommended:** defer the full expansion; authorise narrowly defined, resourced preparatory work only; independently verify affected persons' safety and financial viability before a return to committee.

**Choices and material trade-offs:**

| Route | Mission impact | Cash and safeguarding consequence | Conditional stance |
|---|---|---|---|
| B1: Full expansion now | Largest promised reach | Immediate unrestricted-cash buffer breach, unresolved screening and lease conflict | Reject on presented evidence. |
| B2: Phased pilot with signed restricted grant and explicit core-cost recovery | Tests delivery and impact | Only viable if grant legally permits full required costs, screening is resolved, and unrestricted downside is funded | Reassess after defined conditions. |
| B3: Defer new sites, remediate existing safeguards and protect current clients | Delays new reach | Reduces severe harm risk; buys limited time for cash turnaround | Preferred immediate position, with strict timelines. |
| B4: Cancel initiative and redirect restricted grant | Could breach donor deed if already accepted | May require grantor consent/repayment; does not automatically cure base-case cash deficit | Seek legal/funder advice, not unilateral transfer. |

**Missing information:** WA association rules, member-reserved powers and actual committee quorum; ACNC Charity Register status and reporting classification; executed grant conditions, clawbacks, matching, indirect-cost recovery and grant recognition; weekly payables, employee/super liabilities, receivables and creditor ageing; actual unrestricted cash reconciled to restrictions and deferred revenue; independent safeguarding allegations and complaint response; WWCC/other screening requirements applicable to roles; regulatory reporting/incident duties; lease pricing and relation/conflict matrix; volunteer supervision capacity and staff/volunteer experience; grant programme outcomes and realistic alternative delivery models.

**Authority [LAW + ANA].** This **association** operates under **Associations Incorporation Act 2015 (WA)** and registered rules, *not* generic Corporations Act company-board voting rules. As an **ACNC-registered charity**, it must also satisfy the **ACNC Act 2012**, **ACNC Regulations 2022** governance standards, relevant notification and reporting duties, subject to defined exceptions/overlaps. Standard 5 is a duty on the **charity to take reasonable steps** concerning Responsible People, not automatic application of Corporations Act ss 180–183 to association committee members. Both charity and WA revenue tiers must be assessed from actual period revenues; a fictional annual revenue of **A$2.4m for FY2026** would imply **ACNC medium** and **WA Tier 2** under the verified 2026 revenue bands (**A$500k–less than A$3m**), assuming no relief or extra reporting direction. A later actual revenue **at or above A$3m** would change ordinary category in the applicable period, not retrospectively. Grants remain restricted by their executed terms; lawful grant variation requires the grantor and affected other parties where stipulated. Committee member interests require statutory/rule treatment and independent procurement/approval. The safeguarding response must be tailored to applicable WA child protection, WHS, employment and contractual conditions; no universal “one report within X hours” is inferred without identifying the actual trigger. [WA statute](https://www.legislation.wa.gov.au/legislation/statutes.nsf/main_mrtitle_13715_homepage.html); [ACNC governance standards](https://www.acnc.gov.au/for-charities/manage-your-charity/governance-hub/governance-standards).

**Board discussion prompts:** What harm is currently occurring or reasonably foreseeable? Who is supervising staff/volunteers *today*? Is the new grant a legally enforceable agreement or only a verbal offer? What uses of its proceeds are permitted? Would expansion crowd out safety/finance work? What if the donor withholds or delays the next instalment? Who has authority to choose the conflicted landlord? Does the organisation face a current debt payment problem even if no expansion is approved?

**Model director reasoning [ANA].** The mission argument is valid but insufficient: serving more clients unsafely or by consuming money restricted to other beneficiaries defeats stewardship. At 0.50m unrestricted cash the base case already breaches the hypothetical 0.25m buffer in about 3.6 months; simply deferring expansion is not a complete turnaround. Require immediate independent safeguarding controls, a weekly 13-week **unrestricted** cash forecast, grant deed analysis, referral/continuity for affected clients, and expert review where inability to pay debts is suspected. A member's popular or well-intentioned related-party proposal does not cure conflicts; exclude the interested committee member from the applicable decision process and check whether remaining members can validly constitute the committee.

**Illustrative resolution and conditions [SIM].** The committee declines immediate lease execution and full expansion; records the member's declared property interest, non-participation and quorum check; directs safety lead to protect current service users and assess mandatory reports promptly; mandates a reconciled unrestricted cash / liabilities forecast within **five working days [fictional board target]** and specialist legal/insolvency advice immediately if due debts are in doubt; appoints an independent procurement reviewer; authorises only a low-cost feasibility/consultation stage within existing approved funds; requires an executed grant permitting the selected expenditures, documented workforce/screening capability, post-expansion stress funding above minimum reserve and member authority if required before re-vote.

**Action/evidence record [SIM]:** `BPF-EXP-01`: CEO safeguarding/incident response (immediate, documented external communications); treasurer/CFO restricted-funds reconciliation and 13-week forecast (five business days); charity officer ACNC/state reporting matrix (three business days); independent lease/conflict assessment (before any transaction); grant lead executed agreement, drawdown milestones and reserve-tested cash forecast (before re-vote); committee secretary minutes, member notices and abstentions (contemporaneous); chair organisational capacity assessment and independent assurance (four weeks **illustrative**). Escalate cash forecast below one payroll cycle, unremediated unsafe worker screening, potential serious reportable incident, grant clawback or unapproved related-party payment. Cross-references 08.10.B–C, [08.C01], [08.C15]–[08.C17], 08.15, 08.16; Files 04–06.

### [08.SIM-C] Simulation C — RNTBC: native title agreement, holder consent and benefit stewardship

**Fact pattern [SIM].** **Example Country RNTBC** is an intentionally fictitious corporation registered under the **CATSI Act** and assumed to be a registered native title body corporate (RNTBC) for a fictional determined native title area; neither the area nor its peoples are modelled on an actual Nation, and **no community decision-making custom is invented**. An infrastructure proponent proposes a **20-year** land-access agreement that would affect native title rights and benefit arrangements. The corporation has **65 registered members [fictional count]**, but its affected common law native title holders comprise a wider and incompletely established population. One director proposes immediate execution because six directors support the transaction; a separate director's relative may be engaged to deliver project monitoring. This board vote **does not authorise** the underlying native title decision.

**Fictional financial assumptions (AUD nominal, before tax/fees; not a valuation of native title rights).** Proponent offer: **A$3.2m upfront plus A$0.60m at each year-end for 20 years, with no CPI indexation assumed solely for this numerical illustration**. Chosen illustrative nominal discount rate **7.0%** is *not* a statutory, cultural or market discount rate. Formula `PV of contractual receipts = 3.2 + 0.60 × [1 − (1.07)^−20]/0.07 ≈ A$9.556m`. Nominal receipts sum **A$15.2m** (3.2 + 0.6 × 20), which is *not* the present value. Alternative **[SIM]**: A$4.5m upfront plus A$0.35m annually on same terms, PV ≈ **A$8.208m** and undiscounted total **A$11.5m**. Both exclude monitoring/environmental costs, conditionality, enforcement risk, expected payment timing, leakage to management fees, taxation, inflation, any effect on rights or heritage, and the entire range of non-financial consequences. **No financial calculation can price away a holder's legal decision right.**

**Board-paper summary [SIM].** **Purpose:** establish whose authority is engaged and whether affected holders wish to negotiate, authorise, condition or reject the proposal; independently assess benefits and stewardship. **Immediate decision sought:** a legally valid process to identify affected holders, preserve community-defined consultation and obtain independent advice; **not final approval to execute**. **Recommended:** authorise only process funding/independent scoping, with absolute hold on execution pending all legally required holder decisions and corporate/third-party approvals.

**Competing options:**

| Choice | Potential benefit | Non-substitutable constraint / risk |
|---|---|---|
| C1: Directors sign the agreement immediately | Quick access to offered payments | **Not acceptable** where holder consultation/consent and other conditions are unmet; corporate vote is not the legal substitute. |
| C2: Facilitate holder-led negotiation after independent legal, ecological and valuation reviews | Enables informed choice and improved terms | Cost and schedule; must confirm affected holder population and correct authorised process. |
| C3: Negotiate alternative footprint, shorter term, periodic approvals and stronger protective covenants | Preserves more options and reduces long-lived exposure | Counterparty may reduce financial payment or decline; **holders decide** matters legally reserved to them. |
| C4: Seek no agreement or reject proposed impacts | Protects rights and avoids exposure | Foregone payments and possible future offers; the absence of agreement is a legitimate holder outcome. |

**Missing information and authority gateway:** Obtain the actual **Federal Court determination**, boundaries and defined common law holders, whether the corporation is a trustee or agent, current registered CATSI **rule book**, identity of affected classes, land-access/proponent rights, nature of the native title decision and any **high-level decision** classification, cultural and environmental impacts as understood by the peoples concerned, whether any **Indigenous Land Use Agreement (ILUA)** and Native Title Act registration process is proposed, and whether compensation application/regulation 8B is implicated. Request evidence of an **affected-holder-authorised decision process** under traditional laws and customs where applicable or a legally permitted adopted/agreed process; whether alternative r 8A is genuinely valid; accessible information, independent legal/financial advice, consent/consultation records, dissent, certificates under **r 9**, holder-defined confidentiality and grievance handling. Do **not** assert that registered membership or attendance is the exhaustive list of affected holders.

**Authority and legal distinctions [LAW + ANA]:**

- **Corporate capacity:** CATSI Act 2006 governs director duties, corporation execution and registered rule book. Directors must manage the corporation lawfully, address material personal interests under ss **268-1, 268-20** and any related-party financial benefits under **Part 6-6** if engaged. The **limited exception in CATSI s 268-5** means holding native title *as such* is not necessarily a notifiable personal conflict, but an interested monitoring contractor or relative requires a separate fact-specific assessment. A board member appointed by one family remains a corporate director for the corporation and has additional RNTBC obligations.
- **Holder authority:** *Native Title Act 1993* and *Native Title (Prescribed Bodies Corporate) Regulations 1999*, particularly **rr 8, 8A, 8B, 9**, impose decision-class-specific consultation, consent and certification requirements. **Affected common law holders may be non-members**. A valid corporation resolution and a certified holder process may both be required, with distinct evidence and legal effect. A customary or agreed method must be established by the affected holders and applicable law — never invented in a template. [PBC Regulations](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F1999B00146/latest); [ORIC, RNTBC decision-making](https://www.oric.gov.au/for-corporations/decision-making/decision-making-RNTBCs), updated 12 October 2026.
- **Agreement approvals:** An ILUA, future act, trust benefits arrangement, land-access agreement and government/third-party approval each has its own legal conditions, including affected-party authorisation and registration where applicable. A PBC r 9 certificate is evidence **of the required process**, not a power to override a missing holder decision. For major agreement execution, specialist native title counsel should map every required approval and its correct order.
- **Benefit owner:** “Agreement money” may belong to the corporation, be owed to holders, be payable to a trust, or be constrained by agreement; those are not interchangeable. Any benefits trust must be assessed under its deed and trustee law; obtain advice on corporate solvency, insurance, distributions, conflicts, custody and long-term accountability.

**Board discussion prompts:** Which rights are affected, and who holds them? Who identified the decision-making group, and from what authority? Does any person lack a fair opportunity to understand the terms? Could a high headline PV conceal restrictive rights clauses, unpriced environmental effects or intergenerational obligations? Has the proponent's financier assumed an authorisation which cannot be delivered? Could consultants have divided loyalties? How will benefits be controlled, transparently distributed and reported without disclosing protected knowledge?

**Model director reasoning [ANA].** The financial offer is relevant to an *informed* assessment, not a mandate. The directors can properly require negotiation evidence and recommend safeguards, while refusing to pass off a six-person board majority as affected-holder authorisation. An independent expert must explain the commercial assumptions and costs in an accessible format consistent with arrangements chosen by the affected peoples. Where native title holder views are disputed, the corporation must not resolve identity/cultural authority simply by imposing a corporate membership vote. Separate the decision to undertake *preparatory process* from the native title decision, and distinguish whether the corporation executes as trustee or agent.

**Illustrative **process-only** board resolution [SIM].** The board: (i) records that **no native title agreement or high-level native title decision is authorised or executed**; (ii) commissions independent native title and financial advice, subject to valid budget/engagement authority and conflict review; (iii) directs the corporation to work with affected common law holders to establish the lawful decision-making method and accessible information; (iv) requires completion and independent verification of applicable r 8/8A/8B consultation and consent, r 9 certificate, any ILUA and other approval pathway; (v) requires separate conflict/related-party and benefit-trust reviews; (vi) reserves any signing authority for a further board decision **after** the required holder and external approvals are proven, without binding holders to the directors' preferred answer.

**Action/evidence record [SIM]:** `EC-RNTBC-01`: governance officer obtains determination and rule book (before engagement); independent native title lawyer produces affected-holder/decision-class/ILUA matrix (before process design); holder-authorised process participants design and conduct consultation (timing **established by holders and law**, not fictitious calendar); independent valuer evaluates payments, counterparty credit, restrictions and alternatives (before informed holder decision); board secretary keeps **corporate** minutes and separate confidential holder-consent evidence (throughout); independent reviewer checks conflicts, related-party benefits, certificates and trust terms (before signing); trustee/benefit manager sets investment, custody, reporting and audit controls (before receipt); board and holders receive separately appropriate ongoing benefit/impact and compliance reports. Escalate contested holder population, defects in consultation, missing consent, beneficiary conflicts, material footprint change, non-payment or emerging cultural/environmental concerns. Cross-references 08.10.D–E, 08.16; [08.T20], [08.T32], [08.T39]; Files 01 §§01.03, 01.08, 07 §§07.04, 07.10–07.12, 07.18.

## 08.14 Original self-learning questions and board/executive role-play

**Use:** Answer without looking at the explanation, then test the legal entity and evidence requirements. Every question below is **[ANA] new and independently written** for private study; it does not copy the AICD exam/assignment, its questions or marking guide. Appropriate answers are evidence-sensitive, not mechanical certificates of compliance. The explanations show **reasoning**, not the one correct sentence to submit for an assessment.

### 08.14.1 Director duties, conflicts, judgment and escalation

**[08.Q01] A director's family company proposes a contract at a price below other tenders. Is a cheap price sufficient authority to approve?**  
**Reasoning:** No. Confirm the decision-maker's statute/constitution, director's interest, required disclosures, whether the conflicted person may attend or vote, independent quotation/quality evidence and related-party approval rules. A discount may make the transaction attractive but does not cure an improperly authorised one. For a CATSI corporation examine **Part 6-6** separately; for a charity test purpose/private benefit; for an RNTBC consider holder/trust restrictions if relevant. Record the independent choice, not merely the director's recusal. [08.C01], [08.C05], Files 01, 06, 07.

**[08.Q02] A chair says an adverse internal-audit finding is ‘for management’ and excludes it from the board pack. What should a director do?**  
**Reasoning:** Ascertain materiality, statutory/reporting implications, recurrence, risk appetite and whether management's remedy is effective. Request the report, its owner, deadlines and independent verification. Escalate through audit/risk committee, chair, board or independent advice as appropriate. Do not assume a concern is misconduct or seize operational authority; the board is accountable for whether its oversight framework works. [08.C04], [08.C09], Files 02–03.

**[08.Q03] Six directors unanimously approve a market announcement; an expert drafted it. Can each sign without reading it?**  
**Reasoning:** No. Every director must bring an independent informed mind to material approval. *Hellicar* concerns approval and evidentiary significance of adopted minutes; *Healey* concerns reading and understanding accounts and spotting inconsistencies. Reasonable reliance on an expert is not an automatic release from the particular duty. A redline, explanation of key claims, testing of disputed matters and accurate approval record matter. [08.C18], Files 01, 05.

**[08.Q04] An overseas opportunity has an excellent estimated IRR, but the proponent imposes a 24-hour deadline. Approve?**  
**Reasoning:** Decide only after sufficient legal, commercial and control evidence proportionate to irreversibility. Test sources of urgency and valuation, legal enforceability, downside, finance and any personal interest. Consider buying exclusivity, a staged option, or declining. The business judgment rule is conditional; a positive result or high IRR alone neither proves care nor authority. [08.C06], Files 01, 03–04.

### 08.14.2 Strategy, capital allocation and financial capacity

**[08.Q05] Sales rise 12%, reported gross margin improves, but operating cash and interest cover deteriorate. Which result best measures strategy success?**  
**Reasoning:** None alone. Disaggregate acquisitions/price/mix, working capital, capex, debt maturities, lease cash, operating margin and risk-adjusted return on capital. A growth strategy needs sustainable economic or mission outcomes **after** paying for resources and risks, not headline turnover. Validate ratios' definitions and forecasts. [08.C11]–[08.C15], Files 03–04.

**[08.Q06] A company has a current ratio of 1.9 and net assets of A$30m. Can directors certify that it is solvent?**  
**Reasoning:** Not on those facts. Australian cash-flow insolvency asks whether **debts can be paid when due**; receivables, stock, restrictions, due dates, covenants and future committed receipts determine the answer. Seek a time-phased forecast at the relevant entity level and professional advice where there are reasonable grounds for concern. Accounting going concern and s 95A solvency are related but separate judgments. [08.C13], Files 04–05.

**[08.Q07] The board approved a five-year plan. Two years later a key assumption fails; does governance mean staying the course until the next strategy review?**  
**Reasoning:** No. The plan is conditional on its assumptions. Specify signposts, stop/pivot thresholds, opportunity cost, capital available to alternatives and risk appetite; request management's refreshed options. The board decides significant changes while executives plan execution. Sunk costs do not justify future expenditure. [08.C08], Files 03–04.

**[08.Q08] Is an EBITDA-positive retailer that has negative free cash necessarily solvent and creating value?**  
**Reasoning:** Neither follows. EBITDA excludes maintenance capex, working-capital increases, financing and many lease-related cash requirements; solvency depends on actual due debts and finance. Value creation must consider return on capital versus funding cost. Compare FY2023 Godfreys EBITDA decline, cash self-generation and loan dependence before making any inference. [08.C13], Files 03–04.

### 08.14.3 Financial reporting, audit and disclosure

**[08.Q09] The auditor gives an unmodified opinion; should the board remove the financial-report approval discussion from the agenda?**  
**Reasoning:** No. External audit delivers reasonable assurance in its defined scope; statutory declarations remain directors' responsibility. Require significant judgments, cash forecasts, debt maturities, disclosure reconciliation, uncorrected misstatements and the auditor's unresolved concerns. *Centro* illustrates why professional sign-offs do not replace directors' informed review. [08.C18], File 05.

**[08.Q10] A cyber intrusion is detected after balance date and before results. Must the financial statements record a provision, and is ASX disclosure deferred until a number is known?**  
**Reasoning:** Both are separate, fact-dependent tests. Investigate whether the event reveals a condition existing at reporting date and whether recognition, estimate or note disclosure criteria are met. Independently consider price-sensitive market information, LR 3.1A conditions, notification to affected individuals and operational harms. Materiality is not just a possible charge against profit. [08.SIM-A], Files 01, 05.

**[08.Q11] Two ‘quick ratios’ differ because one excludes inventory only and the other excludes inventory and prepayments. Which is wrong?**  
**Reasoning:** Neither should be labelled without definition. The AICD Nick Scali teaching ratio subtracts inventory; a stricter acid test also removes prepayments and illiquid ‘other’ assets. Directors must demand the components, treatment of restricted cash and consistency with peer comparisons rather than treating a printed benchmark as a universal definition. [08.C12], 08.15.

### 08.14.4 NFP, CATSI/RNTBC, culture and risk

**[08.Q12] A charity has A$1m in bank cash and a A$700k grant obligation. Can its committee use A$800k to pay ordinary wages?**  
**Reasoning:** It depends on the *executed* grant deed, grant-recognition treatment and how much cash is actually available for those wages. Donor/contractually restricted cash is not discretionary just because it is on the same bank statement. Identify allowed programme wages and indirect costs, other creditor claims and an unrestricted cash forecast; do not assume A$300k is unrestricted merely by netting balances. [08.SIM-B], File 06.

**[08.Q13] A WA sporting association is registered with the ACNC. Does its board now operate solely under the Corporations Act?**  
**Reasoning:** No. WA incorporation remains governed by WA association law and registered rules; ACNC charity registration is an overlay. Charity Responsible People, governance standards and reporting duties must be reconciled with association committee duties, tiers, member rights and any reporting overlap relief. Classification must precede any vote or sign-off. [08.C15], 08.10.B–C, Files 01, 06.

**[08.Q14] A CATSI RNTBC has 60 registered members and 40 vote ‘yes’ to a native title access agreement. Is consent proven?**  
**Reasoning:** No. Determine **affected common law holders**, who may not be corporate members, and classify the native title decision under PBC Regulations rr 8–9, applicable alternative and other agreement procedures. Obtain valid holder-authorised consultation and consent, certification and corporate execution authority separately. An ordinary membership ballot may perform one task but does not automatically perform the other. [08.SIM-C], File 07.

**[08.Q15] Does an RNTBC director have to declare a conflict merely because they are a common law native title holder?**  
**Reasoning:** CATSI s 268-5 has a specific exception for an interest merely arising from that holder status in the relevant RNTBC setting. That does **not** excuse an interest in a contracting firm, special family benefit or an unrelated material personal interest. Check CATSI ss 268-1, 268-20, Part 6-6 and rule-book safeguards separately, and never label ordinary cultural belonging as misconduct. [08.SIM-C], File 07.

**[08.Q16] A CEO meets all performance targets but staff report chronic stress and service errors. Can the board reward speed alone?**  
**Reasoning:** The metrics may be badly designed. Examine whether workload, privacy errors, unsafe psychosocial conditions, employee silence and victim outcomes changed. Require independent assessment, critical controls, reporting and revised incentives. Legal WHS/notification and cultural obligations need actual entity/jurisdiction checks; efficiency is not proof of safe, ethical delivery. [08.C07], [08.C09], Files 02–03.

**[08.Q17] Is a company board obliged to accept an environmentally risky project if its net present value is positive?**  
**Reasoning:** No. Value estimates must be tested against law, physical and transition exposures, enforceable rights, the company's actual purposes, financial capacity and authorised risk appetite. For an RNTBC, no NPV calculation displaces affected common law holders' native title consent rights. Compare the alternative paths including ‘no agreement’. [08.SIM-C], Files 01, 03, 07.

**[08.Q18] A director strongly disagrees but the majority intends to proceed; what should they do?**  
**Reasoning:** State evidence and principle calmly, request missing independent advice, identify material legal preconditions, and ensure the board understands alternatives. If a valid decision is reached, request accurate minutes recording reasons and any dissent, then consider escalation obligations where unlawful conduct or imminent serious harm is involved. Recording dissent is evidence, not a universal immunity, and resigning does not erase an earlier duty. [08.C19], 08.16, Files 01–02.

### 08.14.5 Three role-play formats and observation rubric

**Role-play 1 — listed cyber board (20–30 minutes, original).** Assign chair, independent director, CEO, CISO, company secretary, CFO/auditor adviser and observer. At first, management knows only that a vendor reported possible data exposure; at minute 10 the observer introduces a *fictional* credible journalist's enquiry; at minute 15 the auditor states financial-record integrity is not yet validated. Each person must separate evidence, legal decision rights and uncertainty. **Chair:** bring forward opposing views before a preferred answer; **CEO/CISO:** recommend containment and credible timelines, not an unfounded ‘no materiality’ guarantee; **company secretary/legal:** run continuous disclosure and NDB clocks; **director:** articulate reasons to approve, defer or condition release; **observer:** score whether revisions of judgment were evidence-driven. Debrief: what event changed market/confidentiality decisions? [08.SIM-A].

**Role-play 2 — charity/association growth committee (20–30 minutes).** Assign chair, treasurer, safeguarding lead, CEO, interested property-owner committee member, beneficiary advocate and observer. The CEO argues for immediate expansion to retain a conditional grant; treasurer unveils the restricted-cash split; safeguarding lead reports a missed screening control. An effective board distinguishes **approval of mission** from **authority to spend and safe capability to deliver**. Require the interested member to declare interests and verify voting/quorum requirements, not simply leave without documenting process. Debrief: did the board address the baseline cash shortfall as well as the expansion? [08.SIM-B].

**Role-play 3 — RNTBC process design (30–40 minutes).** Roles: RNTBC chair, another CATSI director, governance officer, **an independently appointed representative of affected holders for the purpose of this invented exercise only**, proponent, independent native title lawyer and observer. Do **not** assign fictional cultural rules or pretend this role-play creates real holder authority. The proponent wants immediate signature; the lawyer separates board, member and affected common law holder decisions. Participant representing holders may challenge process adequacy without purporting to make a real native title decision. Debrief: which evidence proves who is entitled to decide, rather than who attended a meeting? [08.SIM-C].

**Observer rubric [ANA, not AICD scoring]:** Record five qualitative dimensions, `strong / emerging / missing`: (1) entity and decision authority correct; (2) material facts versus assumptions separately stated; (3) legality, rights, solvency, safety and risk appetite addressed; (4) alternatives and minority views fairly tested; (5) executable resolution with conditions, consent, owners and triggers. Document *one example* of observed conduct for every rating. Do not average a high financial score with a failed legal-consent gate.

## 08.15 Financial formula directory and independent calculation audit

### 08.15.1 Scope, conventions and arithmetic controls

**Source basis:** FIN pp. 109–113 (Nick Scali 2021–2024 teaching series); FIN pp. 120, 123, 125 (Godfreys 2021–2023 and **seven months to 30 January 2024**); IFS pp. 52–55 / DISC pp. 2–5 (fictional Discount Depot FY2024 and FY2023); FAPP pp. 8–10 (SLSQ real historical FY2024/FY2023 extracts). **All source amounts are Australian dollars in thousands (A$'000), unless a row expressly converts to millions/percent/times or identifies an alternative scale.** No market prices, data beyond these periods or missing balances are imputed. Exact inputs and independently computed outputs follow.

**Audit method [ANA]:** Input values are transcribed from identifiable source tables. Arithmetic is independently performed as decimal calculations, including ratio, growth, reconciliation and financing bridges; identities are checked separately where figures permit. A reported percentage is not simply copied as if verified. Computations present rounded results and retain exact numerator/denominator. A figure that cannot be replicated from the provided aggregated source is tagged **UNRESOLVED**, not forced to match. A “—” denominator means the arithmetic is a difference or sum rather than a ratio. ### 08.15.2 Compact formula directory — define the metric before calculating

| Formula / measure | Definition and board-use caution |
|---|---|
| Accounting equation | `Equity = Assets − Liabilities` at the **same date**; does not value a business or identify spendable funds. |
| Revenue growth | `(Revenue_t / Revenue_(t−1) − 1) × 100`; use comparable full years, accounting policies and consolidated scope. |
| Gross margin | `(Sales − Cost of goods sold) / Sales × 100`; source expense classification controls. |
| EBITDA and EBITDA margin | `EBIT + depreciation + amortisation` when classifications align; divide by revenue for margin; omits working capital, capex, debt, most lease cash. |
| EBIT margin; NPAT/surplus margin | `EBIT / sales × 100`; `NPAT / sales × 100` or `NFP surplus / total income × 100`. EBIT definition may differ in AICD teaching and statutory presentation. |
| Return on assets/equity | `EBIT / (average or ending assets)`; `NPAT / (average or ending equity)`; state which. Negative equity makes ordinary ROE misleading. |
| Net working capital | `current assets − current liabilities`; includes inventory and sometimes restricted/non-cash items. |
| Current ratio | `current assets / current liabilities`; balance sheet timing and debt classification can dominate. |
| Inventory-excluded ‘quick’ ratio | `(current assets − inventory) / current liabilities`; may still include prepayments, derivatives and restricted funds. |
| Stricter acid-test ratio | `(cash eligible + readily collectible trade receivables + liquid investments) / current liabilities`; **only** if components/disclosures permit. |
| Gearing — separate definitions | `total liabilities / equity` is **not** bank debt/equity; distinguish `(current + non-current borrowings) / equity`, `gross debt / EBITDA`, or lender-defined covenant. |
| Interest cover | `EBIT / cash/accrual interest`, explicitly **including or excluding lease interest**. Compare matched periods and conventional financing definition. |
| Receivable, inventory, payable days | `365 × average eligible balance / appropriate annual flow` (sales for trade receivables; COGS/purchases for inventory/payables) with defined balances, GST and average/closing convention. AICD examples sometimes use **closing** balances. |
| Cash-conversion cycle | `days receivables + days inventory − days payables`; apply comparable definitions and customer deposits where relevant. |
| Operating cash conversion | `net operating cash flow / NPAT`; a very high value can reflect advances, delayed expenses or classifications, not necessarily excellent sustainability. |
| Illustrative cash after capex | `CFO − cash payments for PPE − cash intangibles`; **not** a universally defined free cash flow; account for acquisitions, leases, dividends, financing, restricted cash. |
| Cash-flow reconciliation | `CFO + CFI + CFF (+ FX/other separately disclosed) = movement in cash`; `opening + change = closing`. |
| Present value of level receipts | `upfront + periodic payment × [1 − (1+r)^−n]/r`; use internally consistent nominal/real cash flows and rates. Not a legal authorisation test. |
| Solvency (not a ratio) | **Corporations Act s 95A:** ability to pay debts as and when due. Requires time-specific liabilities, genuinely available cash/finance and forward evidence, not just a current ratio. |
| Going concern (accounting basis) | Assess use of the going-concern basis and material uncertainties under applicable reporting standards and entity circumstances; distinct from statutory insolvency and auditor opinion type. |

## 08.16 Original adaptable director decision-and-evidence record

### 08.16.1 Purpose, scope, use and authority caveat

This section is an **original model governance record [ANA]**, not a copied AICD template, legally prescribed minutes, certified native title consent instrument or a filled AICD assessment response. Its purpose is to make the chain from *proposed decision → legal authority → evidence and uncertainty → reasoned choice → conditions and follow-through* visible to another director, auditor, regulator, member or rights holder with a legitimate right of access. A decision can require **several separate approvals**, and documenting one does not validate the others. Adapt the record to the entity's actual constitution, rule book, statute, contract, trust, regulator, privacy regime and any community-authorised engagement requirements.

**Record control:** The company secretary or accountable governance officer assigns a unique identifier, saves the controlled board-paper version and retains an amendment log. Sensitive employment, legal-privilege, cyber, health and cultural material belongs in access-controlled annexures, with the public minutes identifying that an annexure was considered without exposing protected information. Do not invent an entitlement to legal privilege; obtain legal advice about its availability and preservation. Record contemporaneously, then approve and preserve minutes according to the actual entity law and rules. A summary of discussion is not a verbatim transcript or automatic evidence of every individual director's view.

### 08.16.3 Decision completion is not consent completion or risk closure

Before the chair confirms a motion is effective, the governance officer should check: **(a)** legal capacity, quorum and conflicts; **(b)** all separate member, regulatory, funder, lender, trustee or rights-holder preconditions; **(c)** evidence contemporaneous with the decision; **(d)** any public or participant notification deadlines; **(e)** authorisation and conditions for financial commitments; and **(f)** an owner and review date. If the board approves subject to conditions, mark the record **“conditional—not yet executable”** until the stated independent verifier signs the evidence and all required legal approvals are complete. Some legal conditions require a *fresh* resolution even when management has satisfied the underlying information request.

Directors should receive an exception-based closure report showing condition IDs, evidence, overdue items, breaches and changed assumptions. A satisfied control does not imply a past decision was risk-free; a failed control does not itself prove a civil wrong. Maintain an accurate decision trail, correct a mistaken record transparently, and preserve privileged, personal and cultural material according to lawful access arrangements.
