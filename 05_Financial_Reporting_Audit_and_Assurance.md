**Scope:** Corporations Act companies, listed entities, Australian Charities and Not-for-profits Commission (ACNC) charities, companies limited by guarantee (CLGs), state/territory incorporated associations and Corporations (Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander) Act 2006 (CATSI) corporations.  
**Status:** Original research synthesis for private study and directors' professional preparation. It is not legal, accounting or audit advice for a particular engagement.

> **Central proposition.** Financial statements are not an administrative product for directors to endorse after management and the auditor finish. Where statute requires board approval and a directors' declaration, directors must form their *own, informed opinion*, based on an intelligible financial story, resolved accounting judgments, reliable records, a defensible liquidity assessment, and demonstrable scrutiny. An external audit strengthens the evidence base; it does not transfer the board's accountability. Different entity laws prescribe different reports, levels of assurance and deadlines. A company, charity, incorporated association and CATSI corporation must therefore be classified **before** applying any checklist.

### Controlling distinctions

| Distinction | Correct interpretation | Board consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Directors / management / auditor | Management prepares and supports the numbers; directors govern, challenge and make required declarations; auditors independently opine on specified subject matter. | Obtain three distinguishable lines of reasoning, not the same management story repeated by three people. |

## 05.01 Financial reporting objectives, users and the board's non-delegable responsibilities

### 05.01.1 What the financial report is for

**Law/standards.** A general-purpose financial report serves existing and potential investors, lenders and other creditors unable to command tailored reports. In a not-for-profit (NFP), the relevant users also include resource providers and service recipients who need accountability about resources and purpose. The four primary statements describe a distinct aspect of one entity and period: financial position; financial performance and other comprehensive income; changes in equity/accumulated funds; and cash flows. Notes turn aggregated amounts into interpretable information by explaining measurement, classifications, risk, estimates and commitments. Australian Accounting Standards (AAS) and the relevant legal reporting obligation determine the actual package. [ST1, ST2, ST6]

The annual report is not identical to the *financial report*. Under Corporations Act s 295 the financial report consists of financial statements, notes and directors' declaration. A directors' report, audit report and, for an in-scope entity, sustainability report are legally distinct documents. Users also read the operating review, market announcements and explanatory information as a connected narrative. The board should ask whether numbers, narrative and risk statements tell a coherent story, rather than merely whether totals add up. [L1, ss 295–300; AICD[FIN, 2025/26 V1, pp. 23–35]; AICD[IFS, 2025/26 V1, pp. 5–11]]

**AICD teaching.** Financial literacy is a director skill, not a requirement to reproduce the chief financial officer's (CFO's) technical work. The FIN notes treat the balance sheet, income statement and cash flow statement as an interconnected account of past performance, while management accounts, budgets and forecasts progressively reveal the present and expected future. The course specifically links statutory sign-off, internal controls, audit and financial performance. An accurate year-end result that contradicts eleven months of board reporting is a substantive governance issue, even if an auditor accepts it. [AICD[FIN, 2025/26 V1, pp. 7–18, 23–44, 50–79]; AICD[IFS, 2025/26 V1, pp. 8–11, 35–38]; AICD[FSH, 2025/26 V1, pp. 5–12]]

### 05.01.2 Who actually owes what duty

**Law.** The entity's obligation to prepare a report under Chapter 2M is distinct from individual officers' duty of care and the directors' resolution required to make a declaration. Corporations Act ss 180(1), 189, 190, 286, 295(4)–(5), 297 and 344 are central. Section 344 concerns taking reasonable steps to secure compliance with financial reporting and accounting-record provisions; neither delegating draft preparation nor receiving a clean auditor's report automatically establishes those steps. Directors can rely on apparently competent people within s 189's conditions, but that provision is not permission to ignore facts they should understand. Other legal regimes impose different statutory hooks. [L1; J1]

| Actor | Responsibility in a conventional Chapter 2M audit | Evidence to directors |
|---|---|---|
| Board collectively | Oversee systems, resolve accounting issues, approve statutory reports and directors' declarations where applicable; consider solvency and continuous disclosure. | Approved accounting policies and significant judgments; final statements; minutes; written exceptions and resolutions. |
| Individual director | Read and understand the report, apply business knowledge, identify inconsistencies and ask follow-up questions before participating in the resolution. | Personal board pack, responses to questions, reconciliations, professional views, updated cash and covenant analysis. |
| CEO/CFO and management | Maintain records, choose and implement policies, prepare estimates and drafts, certify controls within applicable requirements, correct errors. | Signed representation/attestation, close-control results, accounting papers, reconciliations, forecasts and correction log. |
| Audit committee | Analyse reporting, controls, auditor independence, audit plan, disputed issues and recommendations; report to full board. | Committee report, identified unresolved matters, recommendations and private auditor meeting notes. |
| External auditor | Plan and perform audit/review under applicable standards, gather sufficient appropriate evidence, communicate significant findings and issue the assurance report. | Engagement and planning letters, significant findings, misstatement schedule, independence declarations, signed auditor report. |
| Internal audit | Independently assess controls and operations within an approved mandate; not a substitute for management control ownership. | Risk-based plan, reports, overdue remediation and validation of closure. |

**Analysis.** The board should distinguish three questions: *Were transactions properly captured? Were choices and estimates appropriate? Does the final communication fairly convey the resulting position?* Successful bookkeeping is necessary but insufficient. A classification error may change solvency perceptions without changing cash; an omitted note may conceal a major risk without changing reported profit. A board unable to articulate the major movements or judgments has not used the assurance it purchased. [AICD[FIN, 2025/26 V1, pp. 35–37]; AICD[TOOL, 2025/26 V1, pp. 47–52]; J1]

### 05.01.3 Minimum personal inquiry

Before approval, each director should be able to answer: (1) Which law and accounting basis govern this particular report? (2) What changed in the business, balance sheet and cash since last year? (3) Which two or three estimates could change the result materially? (4) Which obligations, guarantees or exposures might be missing? (5) What cash is actually available, subject to what restrictions and commitments? (6) What has the auditor challenged and how was it resolved? (7) Which statements, climate claims and previous market disclosures must reconcile? An unanswered material question is a request for further evidence, not a reason to rely on the majority or the deadline. Cross-references: 05.06–05.10, 05.14–05.16.

## 05.02 Which reporting regime applies to which entity and under what conditions

### 05.02.1 Classify legal form, then overlays, then year

**Law.** A legal-form decision tree is essential. Identify the Australian company registration, whether proprietary/public/CLG, whether listed or otherwise disclosing, control by a foreign company, Australian Financial Services licence and other prudential status, whether ACNC-registered, whether registered under CATSI (including a registered native title body corporate), and state/territory association incorporation if applicable. Next determine group boundaries, revenues, assets, employees, grant terms, constitutional requirements and the *start* of the reporting period. ACNC registration and CATSI incorporation are not interchangeable. Charity status is an overlay on an entity, not itself a company form. [L1–L4; R1–R5]

### 05.02.2 Legal/reporting applicability matrix (base case, not every exemption)

| Entity and threshold test | Primary annual financial-report duty | Ordinary assurance and destination | Separate climate/reporting question |
|---|---|---|---|
| ASX-listed Australian company | Chapter 2M, Corporations Act ss 292–300; directors' report and declaration. | Statutory audit, ASIC filing, member and ASX publication; half-year audit **or review** as applicable. | Apply s 292A/group dates to determine sustainability report; ASX LR 3.1 and Chapter 4 separately. |
| Unlisted public company | Chapter 2M annual report unless an express exemption/relief; CLG special rules. | Audit and ordinarily ASIC filing, subject to particular statutory relief. | s 292A thresholds and any NGER route. |
| Large proprietary company | Meets at least **two of three** consolidated measures: revenue **$50m**, gross assets **$25m**, employees **100** for the relevant period (at or above respective value). | Chapter 2M annual report/audit and generally ASIC lodgement, subject to applicable relief. | Size thresholds for climate differ and apply only when reporting under Chapter 2M. |
| Small proprietary company | Ordinarily no Chapter 2M annual report, unless shareholder/ASIC direction, foreign-control and other statutory triggers, or applicable special provision. | Do not infer an audit from mere incorporation; other regulator/funder/construction duties may apply. | Ordinarily not in s 292A if not required to prepare Chapter 2M report. |
| Small CLG (not ACNC registered) | Special s 285A/s 292 and reporting exemptions; test company size and directions rather than applying listed-company rules. | Specific CLG thresholds, member directions, ASIC direction and constitutional requirements govern; no universal audit. | Only if first a Chapter 2M annual reporting entity and climate tests met. |
| ACNC-registered charity, including a CLG | ACNC Act 2012 and Regulations 2022 annual information statement; financial reporting depends on **annual revenue**, not assets/headcount. | Small < $500k: no ACNC annual financial report required; medium $500k–< $3m: annual financial report **reviewed or audited**; large >= $3m: **audited** financial report, subject to defined exceptions. | Do **not** automatically impose Chapter 2M mandatory sustainability reporting merely because charity revenue is high. Confirm whether s 292A applies through an independently operative Chapter 2M reporting obligation. |
| CATSI corporation / RNTBC | CATSI Act 2006 + ORIC; corporation size uses **two of income/assets/staff tests**, then reporting volume separately depends on consolidated gross operating income (CGOI). | All ordinarily lodge a general report; extra financial/audit/directors' reports depend on category and CGOI; possible government-funding alternative. | No automatic Corporations Act Chapter 2M climate obligation. NGER and any other specific duty separately assessed. |
| WA incorporated association | Associations Incorporation Act 2015 (WA); tier measured by **revenue**. | Tier 1 < $500k: no general statutory review/audit; Tier 2 $500k–< $3m: **review**; Tier 3 >=$3m: **audit**; member/Commissioner directions can add audit. | No general Chapter 2M s 292A duty just because association large. |
| Other state/territory incorporated association | The state's own association Act, regulations, size tests and charity harmonisation. | NSW, Vic, Qld, SA, ACT, Tas, NT **differ**, documented at 05.12. | Separate overlays, no assumed Chapter 2M adoption. |

**Sources/qualification:** [L1, ss 45A, 45B, 285A, 292–294B, 292A; R1; L2; R3; L3; R4–R6; R7–R13]. Corporate thresholds should be assessed over the legally specified group, dates and conditions, not against an individual subsidiary's conveniently chosen figures. The matrix is a triage tool; it does not extinguish industry-specific obligations or constitutional/member/grant requirements.

### 05.02.3 Concrete examples of overlapping forms

- A $2 million-revenue registered charity incorporated as a WA association is *medium* under the ACNC revenue definition and *Tier 2* under the WA association definition; investigate ACNC/WA duplication relief and review requirements, rather than filing two reports blindly.
- A $7 million-revenue CATSI corporation with $200,000 of assets and four employees is not necessarily *large* merely from income: the ORIC **two-of-three** classification must be completed; however, CGOI of $7 million independently triggers the more extensive ORIC reporting package whatever its registered size. [R5, R6]
- An unlisted proprietary company with $60 million revenue, $15 million assets and 80 employees satisfies only **one** of three large-proprietary tests, so is not large on that test. Other directions and foreign-control/other regimes remain possible.
- An ASX-listed reporting group with revenue $520 million but assets $900 million and 490 employees meets only one of the Group 1 *corporate size* climate criteria. A separate NGER emissions trigger might still cause earlier entry. [L1, s 292A; R2]

## 05.03 Annual/half-year reports, directors' reports, declarations, group disclosures, filings and deadlines

### 05.03.1 Report contents and legal resolution

**Law.** For a Chapter 2M annual financial report, Corporations Act s 295 requires the statements, notes and the directors' declaration. Under s 295(4), directors' opinions include compliance with accounting standards, true and fair view and reasonable grounds that debts can be paid when due. Include any required additional declaration about a consolidated entity disclosure statement (CEDS) under the applicable amended provisions; the CEDS is a separately specified statement concerning relevant consolidated entity information, not a replacement for consolidated accounts. The declaration is made under a *resolution of the directors* and signed/dated in the required way (s 295(5)). It is not the auditor's declaration. Sections 296–297 require compliance with standards and true and fair reporting; if compliance alone is insufficient, additional disclosure may be required. [L1, ss 295–297; AICD[FIN, 2025/26 V1, pp. 29–35]; AICD[NICK, 2024 report, pp. 50–52]]

The directors' report is governed by ss 298–300A, not just a voluntary chairman's letter. Its relevant content includes a review of operations, results and significant changes, events since period-end, dividends, material environmental information where required, and for listed companies the operating/financial review and specified remuneration disclosures. Some items attract tailored relief and protections for commercially prejudicial information; *non-disclosure must be legally justified*, not simply described as sensitive. The independent auditor's report is a different statutory deliverable. [L1; AICD[FIN, 2025/26 V1, pp. 33–35]]

**Group disclosures.** Consolidated accounts under AASB 10 capture controlled entities; control is an economic/accounting test of power, exposure to variable returns and ability to affect them, not merely majority voting rights. Eliminate intercompany transactions and balances. Assess associates under AASB 128, joint arrangements under AASB 11, foreign-currency translation and non-controlling interests. A CEDS, a parent-entity disclosure and subsidiaries list in notes each answer distinct legal questions. Directors should reconcile subsidiaries added/closed/acquired to legal records, tax, banks, beneficial ownership and sustainability perimeter. [ST3, ST4; AICD[FIN, 2025/26 V1, pp. 32, 76–77]; AICD[FSH, 2025/26 V1, p. 11]; AICD[NICK, 2024 report, pp. 44–51]]

### 05.03.2 Annual/half-year timing for ordinary Chapter 2M entities

| Obligation | Usual statutory requirement | Additional practical test |
|---|---|---|
| ASIC annual lodgement by a **disclosing entity** | Within **three months** after year-end (s 319, subject to applicable conditions). | Compare ASX listed timetable separately; annual report to members has its own rule. |
| ASIC annual lodgement by other Chapter 2M reporting entities | Generally within **four months** after year-end (s 319, subject to special entity/relief rules). | Check ASIC relief instruments and whether an exemption from lodgement, rather than preparation, exists. |
| Half-year report for Chapter 2M **disclosing entities** | Half-year report and audit/review under ss 302–306; ASIC lodgement usually within **75 days** of half-year-end (s 320). | ASX Chapter 4 may require release within **two months**, which is earlier. |
| Members' annual report | Send in the form/timing required by ss 314–317, generally at least **21 days before AGM** and subject to statutory timing alternatives. | Confirm annual-report and AGM calendar rather than assuming ASIC lodgement is the member-delivery date. |
| ASX half-year / preliminary final | Commonly LR **4.2A** and **4.3A**, by **two months** from relevant period-end (with special categories/waivers). | Preliminary market results and financial reports have distinct status and assurance. |
| ASX annual accounts | ASX Chapter 4 commonly requires annual report/accounts within **three months** after financial year-end; LR 4.10 sets report extras. | Check ASX rule version, security category and waiver before announcing a deadline. |

**Law/regulator:** [L1, ss 302–306, 314–320; R1; X1]. ASIC's Regulatory Guide 43 was updated 24 April 2026 and is useful on ASIC lodgement and relief, but not a replacement for statutory text. The example 30 June 2026 ASX entity normally targets the preliminary final announcement **31 August 2026** and a 30 September annual statutory deadline, subject to calendar-day interpretation and exceptions; an unlisted Chapter 2M company typically has a **31 October 2026** four-month outer date. Do not infer that all companies have half-year reporting. [R1, X1]

### 05.03.3 Signatures, governance and version control

The company secretary should maintain a single controlled document manifest: entity and ABN/ACN/ICN, reporting period, board-approval and signature time, director declaration, directors' report, statements, notes, CEDS if applicable, audit opinion and independence declaration, sustainability report if required, and market-release files. Ensure the approved text matches the auditor's signed text and filed version. Where editorial changes follow the board resolution, classify them as immaterial mechanical correction versus substantive revision; substantive amendments must return for appropriate approval and audit assessment. The board should minute its *exact decision*, not simply that the annual report was 'noted'. [L1; AICD[TOOL, 2025/26 V1, pp. 3–12, 118–124]]

## 05.04 Completeness, true-and-fair presentation, materiality, notes and disclosure judgments

### 05.04.1 Completeness has multiple dimensions

**Law/standards.** “True and fair” under s 297 is a whole-report judgment, not a synonym for arithmetic accuracy. A report can balance and still be misleading through missing obligations, incorrect current/non-current classification, premature revenue, weak impairment assumptions, selective segment aggregation, insufficient related-party disclosure or omitted subsequent-event notes. Financial statements are accrual-based except the cash flow statement; estimates are *necessary* where a standard requires them, but should be supported, unbiased and appropriately explained. The IFS teaching shorthand that statutory numbers contain no 'guesstimates' should not be read as prohibiting accounting estimates. [L1; ST1–ST4; AICD[IFS, 2025/26 V1, pp. 8–11]]

Materiality is judged by whether an omission or misstatement could reasonably affect primary users' decisions, individually or in aggregate. Small dollar values may be material because of their nature: a debt covenant breach, fraud, related-party dealing, remuneration, regulatory sanction, changed accounting policy or loss of licence. An accounting audit materiality threshold is **not** a board's permission to suppress qualitatively important facts. For climate reporting, use AASB S2 Appendix D's distinct but connected disclosure materiality principles. [ST1, ST5, ST9; AU1]

### 05.04.2 Questions about notes and narrative consistency

| Disclosure question | Evidence to demand | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Is the accounting basis appropriate for this entity and year? | Reporting-entity memo; AASB tier selection; ASIC/ACNC/CATSI classification; going-concern paper. | A valid treatment in an old SPFS may not be permissible in current GPFS. |
| Are the most material judgments obvious to an informed reader? | Accounting judgment register; sensitivity tables; independent valuations; disclosure map. | Boilerplate may bury the assumptions that drive reported profit/equity. |
| Are liabilities and commitments complete? | Legal claims schedule; contracts; purchase orders; leases; guarantees; subsequent payments; bank letters. | Omitted or misclassified obligations can distort leverage and solvency. |
| Are notes consistent with board-approved strategy and forecasts? | Last board forecast, budget, capital allocation papers and risk appetite; signed assumptions map. | An impairment model with optimistic volume and margin assumptions may contradict the board's base case. |
| Does reporting reconcile to public communications? | Announcement and presentations register; investor guidance; sustainability and grant reports. | Selective disclosure or inconsistent denominators create misleading impressions. |
| Have management and auditors aggregated immaterial differences properly? | Corrected/uncorrected misstatement schedule; qualitative materiality assessment. | Several 'small' errors may combine into a material whole. |

**Director response.** Require a redline of each significant disclosure change against the previous report, an exception-by-exception resolution record and a separate 'omitted matters' certification from finance, legal, risk and operations. Materiality analysis must consider users and disclosures, not only the net profit effect. Request additions to notes where necessary; do not accept the claim that users can find a fact in another document unless incorporation/cross-reference is legally effective. [AICD[FIN, 2025/26 V1, pp. 74–79]; AICD[FIFTY, 2016, pp. 4–12]; L1, ss 296–297]

## 05.05 Reporting frameworks, tiers, permitted financial-report types, accounting periods and AASB 18 transition

### 05.05.1 Entity/reporting framework is a decision, not a label

**Standards/law.** Australia applies the Australian Accounting Standards Board (AASB) standards, including for many NFP entities with sector-specific modifications. Tier 1 GPFS uses full recognition, measurement and disclosure requirements; Tier 2 GPFS retains the relevant recognition and measurement requirements with simplified disclosure under AASB 1060 where eligible. Eligible reports are not necessarily the same as large-reporting entities. AASB 1057 defines application; AASB 1053 concerns tiers; AASB 1060 establishes the current simplified-disclosure standard. The entity's enabling legislation, governing documents and regulator determine whether GPFS, Tier 1/Tier 2 or a still-permitted SPFS is acceptable. [ST1, ST6, ST7; L1–L3]

For-profit private-sector entities required by legislation or constituting document to prepare financial statements complying with Australian Accounting Standards generally moved away from SPFS for relevant periods **beginning 1 July 2021** under the AASB changes, subject to transitional/detail qualifications. ACNC's current charity guidance still recognises SPFS for a charity that does not meet reporting-entity criteria, or GPFS under the relevant tiers, subject to regulatory requirements and changing standards. Never assume 'small charity' means SPFS must be used or that a Tier 2 GPFS is inherently a special-purpose report. [ST6–ST7; R3]

**Prospective changes for NFPs.** AASB 1061, *General Purpose Financial Statements – Tier 3 Not-for-Profit Private Sector Entities*, and AASB 2026-2's accompanying NFP SPFS changes are **future** at this verification date, with broad commencement for periods beginning **1 July 2029**, subject to precise instrument and exceptions. These are not retrospective reasons to declare a 2026 charity's historic SPFS unlawful. A director must maintain an accounting-standard commencement calendar, especially if a charity is considering changing legal form or reporting year. [ST8]

### 05.05.2 Accounting periods, comparatives and first reports

A Corporations Act financial year ordinarily lasts 12 months, subject to first-year and other adjustments under s 323D. A year ending 30 June 2027 **began 1 July 2026**, which is the critical date for both AASB 18 and climate thresholds. A standard beginning 1 January 2027 does **not** automatically govern that 30 June 2027 year. A year ending 31 December 2027 **began 1 January 2027** and may differ. The first year of application commonly requires restated comparatives under the standard's transition rules. For finance groups, reconcile parent and subsidiary balance dates, consolidation adjustments and prior-period corrections. [L1; ST1; ST9]

### 05.05.3 AASB 18: verified operative periods

| Type of entity / period | Date AASB 18 first applies, absent early adoption | Practical consequence |
|---|---|---|
| For-profit entities preparing financial statements to which AASB 18 applies (reporting-tier disclosures may differ) | Annual periods **beginning on or after 1 January 2027**. | A calendar-year 2027 report ordinarily applies AASB 18; a 30 June 2027 report ordinarily does not. Check Tier 2 disclosure amendments separately. |
| NFP private/public-sector entities and superannuation entities applying AASB 1056 | Annual periods **beginning on or after 1 January 2028** under Australian-specific deferral/2026 amendments; inspect entity-specific relief. | A calendar-year 2027 NFP report is not swept in merely because for-profit reporters start in 2027. |
| Eligible early adopters | Earlier application permitted within standard provisions and where entity law allows. | Approval paper should record adopted edition, transition effects and comparatives. |

**Standards.** AASB 18, issued **7 June 2024**, replaces AASB 101 for applicable periods and changes the structure of the statement of profit or loss, notably categories of operating, investing, financing, income taxes and discontinued operations; defined subtotals including operating profit; principles for aggregation/disaggregation; and notes explaining management-defined performance measures (MPMs) when applicable. Classification for banks and entities with specified main business activities can differ. AASB 18 also drives consequential AASB 107 changes; the 2026 targeted amendments and Tier 2 simplified disclosure requirements must be read together. [ST1; ST10]

**Director questions for transition:** (1) Which legal entities apply in which year? (2) Which products, banking/investment activities or foreign units change category? (3) What operating-profit figure will market guidance now reference? (4) Which 'underlying' or adjusted measures are MPMs requiring notes and reconciliations, subject to tier requirements? (5) Which comparative restatements, controls, enterprise resource planning changes and audit judgments are needed? A company cannot escape a more transparent subtotal through a presentation-only 'non-GAAP' adjustment. For an ASX company with a 30 June year-end, the 2027–28 year beginning **1 July 2027** is typically the first mandatory for-profit AASB 18 year, not the 2026–27 year. [ST1; X1]

## 05.06 Major accounting policies, judgments, estimates, impairments, provisions, related parties, group accounts and going concern

### 05.06.1 Start with economic substance and cut-off

**Standards.** Accrual accounting recognises revenue when applicable performance obligations are satisfied and expenses or liabilities when recognition requirements are met, not simply when cash changes hands. Under AASB 15, ask whether a contract and enforceable rights exist, what is being delivered, whether consideration is variable, and when control passes to the customer. Under AASB 1058 and AASB 15, NFP grants and donations need a different analysis: some amounts are current-period income, while enforceable grants with sufficiently specific performance obligations may give rise to liabilities and income over performance. [ST3, ST4; AICD[FIN, 2025/26 V1, pp. 64–69]; AICD[IFS, 2025/26 V1, pp. 12–17]]

Cut-off matters especially where sales, projects or grants accelerate near year-end. Compare dated dispatches, delivery confirmation, contract terms, bank receipts and post-year-end returns. Determine whether major journal entries, reserve releases or related-party transfers reflect genuine economic activity. Ask whether an acquired company's results are consolidated from the correct date and whether pro forma/underlying presentations disclose their assumptions. Statements can be faithful to an accounting policy but not to the substance of a transaction if the initial facts are incomplete.

### 05.06.2 Balance sheet and cash: original director evidence table

| Financial assertion | Director's analytical question | Minimum primary/independent evidence | Escalate if… |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cash and restricted funds | How much cash is *usable* today, after pledged deposits, trust funds, grant restrictions and bank offsets? | Bank confirmations/reconciliations, financing documents, restricted-funds register and forecast. | Closing 'cash' includes inaccessible balances or forecasts double-count overdraft facilities. |
| Receivables | Are revenue and receivables both collectable? What was recovered after year-end? | Aged ledger, subsequent receipts, disputes, expected-credit-loss model and major-customer status. | Revenue rises while ageing, overdue debt and write-offs worsen. |
| Inventory | What evidence supports quantity, cost and net realisable value? | Signed stocktakes, cycle counts, obsolete-stock analytics, subsequent selling prices and return claims. | Slow stock accumulates or count variances remain unresolved. |
| Property and intangibles | Do assets exist, remain productive and generate recoverable economic benefits? | Asset register, site reports, valuations, use-life analysis, budgets and impairment-model inputs. | Forecasts assume revenue/capacity not approved or achievable. |
| Leases | Have all material arrangements and changes been captured? | Lease-contract population, right-of-use/lease liability roll-forward and discount rates. | Hidden extensions, property commitments or current amounts distort liquidity. |
| Borrowings | Is all principal correctly classified and are facilities actually available? | Bank confirmations, debt agreements, repayment schedule, covenant calculations, waivers and security register. | A pre-year-end covenant breach is 'cured' only by a waiver obtained afterward without examining classification rules. |
| Provisions and commitments | What existing obligations have we incurred, irrespective of invoices received? | Legal letters, HR accruals, environmental assessments, grant obligations, purchase contracts, after-date invoices. | A risk memo identifies a probable claim but finance has no corresponding assessment. |
| Equity/reserves | Which movements represent profit, issuance, revaluation, foreign exchange or minority interest? | Equity reconciliation, share registry, valuation history, board resolutions and OCI schedule. | Reported 'growth in net assets' is driven by fair-value movements or a new capital raising rather than performance. |
| Cash conversion | Why did operating cash diverge from reported profit? | Profit-to-cash reconciliation, working-capital bridge, lease cash and tax/interest treatment. | Large profits coexist with a continuing cash deficit and no credible explanation. |
| Cash use | Are dividends, acquisitions, capex and debt service funded sustainably? | Operating/investing/financing cash flow, board capex approvals, dividend forecast and short-term treasury model. | The entity funds recurrent distributions or operating costs by one-off borrowing or asset sales. |

**AICD teaching, with qualification.** FIN pp 50–79 and IFS pp 18–38 use balance-sheet, profit and cash-flow reviews to identify changes, not merely closing balances. An indicative current ratio of 1.5 or a positive free-cash-flow 'self-generation' result can be useful as a prompt but is **not** a universal financial-health threshold. A grant-funded charity can carry substantial cash that cannot be used for general debts; a negative-working-capital retailer can be solvent because cash receipts precede payments; a capital-intensive project may have planned negative cash conversion. Always analyse maturity, restrictions and the business model. [AICD[FIN, 2025/26 V1, pp. 50–79]; AICD[IFS, 2025/26 V1, pp. 18–38]; AICD[TOOL, 2025/26 V1, pp. 53–59]; AICD[METHOD, 2025/26 V1, pp. 1–4]]

### 05.06.3 Impairment: where board strategy meets accounting

**Standards.** AASB 136 requires the appropriate impairment assessment: indicators for relevant assets and annual testing of goodwill and indefinite-life intangibles. The recoverable amount is the higher of value in use and fair value less costs of disposal. Testing often uses cash-generating units (CGUs) and a defensible asset perimeter; goodwill allocation and CGU changes require scrutiny. A valuation used for internal strategy is not necessarily the same as a compliant accounting valuation. Changes in impairment often affect profit, equity, covenants, remuneration and market disclosure without immediate cash movement. [ST4; AICD[FIN, 2025/26 V1, pp. 53–64, 75–79]]

| Impairment assumption | Required challenge | Evidence / independent check |
|---|---|---|
| Forecast revenue and volumes | Is this consistent with most recent board-approved forecast, contract order book and observed year-end run rate? | Reconciliation between approved plan, valuation forecast, post-year-end actuals and prior forecast accuracy. |
| EBITDA margin and costs | Does a 'recovery' rely on unexplained pricing power, inflation relief or efficiency savings not yet funded? | Commercial price curves, historical cost bridge, approved saving plans, downside sensitivities. |
| Capital expenditure and working capital | Does the valuation include asset-sustaining capex, remediation and required funding? | Asset integrity plans, fixed-cost obligations, funding and operating cash schedule. |
| Discount rate | Is risk reflected in cash flows or discount rate without double-counting or omitting risk? Is currency/inflation consistent? | Weighted average cost of capital paper, market inputs, currency/tax basis, independent valuation opinion. |
| Terminal value | What proportion of valuation depends on post-forecast growth and assumed asset life? | Terminal cash-flow decomposition, contract/technology/regulation restrictions, alternative exit values. |
| Climate / energy / environmental assumptions | Do carbon prices, transition demand, asset life and closure dates match external commitments and AASB S2 disclosures? | Climate-to-finance assumptions matrix, external price data, approved emissions pathway and sensitivity tests. |
| CGU and goodwill allocation | Has an acquisition, divestment or reorganisation changed the aggregation or goodwill allocation? | Legal/group chart, segment management reporting, purchase-price allocation and management review map. |

A useful challenge is to request valuation headroom and the *break-even change* in each material assumption that would eliminate it. A mere 'no impairment' conclusion without headroom, sensitivity and prior forecast-versus-actual evidence is weak. Audit committee recommendations should distinguish expert valuation evidence from management's commercial beliefs. Cross-reference 05.11 on climate consistency. [ST4; AU1; AICD[FIN, 2025/26 V1, pp. 55–64]]

### 05.06.4 Provisions, debt, related parties and group judgments

Under **AASB 137**, a provision normally requires a present obligation from a past event, a probable outflow and a reliable estimate. A *possible* obligation may instead require contingent-liability disclosure; a merely contemplated future expenditure is not an automatic provision. Obtain legal advice and operational evidence for litigation, warranties, employee entitlements, rehabilitation, environmental obligations, onerous contracts and restructuring. A board-approved intention alone does not necessarily create a qualifying restructuring obligation. Compare provisions with actual claims and settlements after period-end. [ST4]

Under **AASB 9/7 and AASB 132**, verify financial liabilities, expected credit losses, hedge designation, fair-value hierarchy, liquidity concentration and derivative positions. Counterparty creditworthiness, termination rights and collateral demands are as important as market valuations. Under **AASB 16**, identify the entire lease population and classification; a lease's present value and actual cash payments differ, and readers need both. **AASB 112** deferred-tax assets need evidence of recoverability. **AASB 124** calls for careful identification of related parties and qualifying disclosures; Corporations Act related-party approval laws are distinct from the accounting disclosure test. [ST3, ST4; L1, Ch 2E; AICD[FIFTY, 2016, pp. 9–11]]

**Director transaction evidence table:**

| Transaction | Questions | Evidence / decision |
|---|---|---|
| Business combination | What is acquired, on what date, for what total consideration and assumed obligations? | Executed sale agreement, acquisition-date management accounts, fair-value/PPA working papers, contingent consideration, goodwill calculation, post-deal integration milestones. |
| Disposal or discontinued operation | Was the asset really held for sale? Is classification justified and has the exit been completed? | Sale approval, binding contract, AASB 5 assessment, subsequent-event evidence, gain/loss bridge. |
| Related-party sale/loan/contract | Who benefits, which conflicts were disclosed, and is pricing supported? | Register of interests, counterparty ownership, independent benchmark, approval minutes, accounting note and related-party compliance opinion. |
| Debt refinancing | Is refinancing contractually committed as of relevant classification date? | Original facilities, signed waivers/extensions, counterparty confirmations, covenant test and disclosure. |
| Hedge/commodity derivative | Is the contract hedging a genuine exposure or creating new risk? | Exposure schedule, policy limits, approvals, mark-to-market, hedge accounting memo, counterparty and collateral analysis. |
| Dividend/distribution | Is distribution permitted by law and constitution, and prudent after next-quarter cash demands? | Corporations Act s 254T assessment where applicable, capital/creditor test, 13-week forecast, covenants, restricted-funds map. |

**Analytical interpretation.** The most dangerous late accounts adjustments are often ones the board did not anticipate: changes to assumptions, classification, acquisition-date allocations, reserves, revenue cut-off or lease term. Ask management for a board-specific *top ten entries* listing every material late or manual adjustment with preparer, reviewer, rationale, evidence and auditor response.

### 05.06.5 Going concern is not the s 95A insolvency test

Under **AASB 101 paras 25–26**, for periods where that standard governs, management evaluates whether the entity is a going concern, using all available information about the future for **at least 12 months from the reporting-period end**, not necessarily exactly 12 months. A material uncertainty that may cast significant doubt must be disclosed even when going-concern accounting remains appropriate; if going concern is inappropriate, another basis and its reasons must be disclosed. Future AASB 18/AASB 108 presentation changes do not erase the underlying inquiry. [ST2, ST1]

Under **Corporations Act s 95A**, a person is solvent if able to pay all debts as and when they become due and payable. Section 588G concerns directors' duties in respect of insolvent trading, with its own elements, exceptions and potential defences. Neither negative equity nor an accounting net loss automatically establishes insolvency; neither positive equity nor an unqualified auditor opinion proves solvency. The statutory directors' declaration under s 295(4) requires reasonable grounds concerning debt payment, which should be assessed against an up-to-date and appropriately challenged forecast. The 2016 AICD *Fifty Matters* recommends a rolling cash forecast for at least 12 months **from signing**: a prudent governance challenge, not an exact universal statutory forecast horizon. [L1, ss 95A, 295, 588G; AICD[FIFTY, 2016, p. 11]; AICD[FIN, 2025/26 V1, pp. 90–100]]

**Liquidity, covenants and going-concern evidence:**

| Test | Request | Director's critical question |
|---|---|---|
| 13-week short-term cash | Weekly opening bank cash, receipts, payroll, tax, creditors, debt interest and headroom; compare budget versus actual. | What payment fails first in the downside case, and on what date? |
| 12–18-month cash forecast | Monthly scenarios from the current signing date, including loan maturities, leases, capital commitments, grants with restrictions, tax, working capital. | Are bridge funding, asset sales or government support merely hoped for? |
| Committed borrowing | Executed facilities, security, expiry and availability conditions; lender confirmations. | Is the headroom legally drawable in each scenario, not just shown in a treasury dashboard? |
| Covenants | Definitions from executed agreements, historical and projected calculation, headroom and cure provisions. | What is the tightest covenant and the break-point in sales, EBITDA, interest or asset value? |
| Grant/NFP liquidity | Restricted and unrestricted cash; funding conditions; prepaid service obligations. | Are funds held for service beneficiaries incorrectly relied upon for general creditors? |
| Debt maturity/current classification | Reporting-date rights to defer settlement, breaches, waivers and subsequent refinancing. | Does financial-statement classification reflect the rights existing at the reporting date? |
| Going-concern judgments | Board papers, downside plan, auditor challenge, sensitivity and explicit disclosure assessment. | Is a material uncertainty transparently disclosed even if management expects to survive? |
| Insolvency exposure | Matured debt schedule; aged creditors; arrears/tax; legal advice; recovery plan and options. | Has the board obtained specialist advice and taken timely action if payment incapacity is plausible? |

If liquidity is unresolved, the board should demand revised cash forecasts, independent solvency/restructuring advice where warranted, lender evidence, contingencies and a formal follow-up decision. It should not approve a declaration solely because management offers comfort or because the auditor has not modified the opinion. Record the adverse scenario, alternatives, responsible officers, trigger dates and treatment of new debts. See File 04 for restructuring and insolvent-trading depth. [AICD[TOOL, 2025/26 V1, p. 124]; L1]

## 05.07 Director enquiry, representations, accounting papers, professional advice and documented challenge

### 05.07.1 Enquiry must create an evidential trail

**Law/cases.** Sections 189 and 190 permit reasonable reliance and delegation under specified conditions; they do not replace direct comprehension of material financial-report content. In *ASIC v Healey* (Centro), directors failed to discharge basic obligations by treating management and audit sign-off as sufficient where the accounts exposed misclassification and omitted material guarantee disclosures. The standard is not being a technical valuation specialist: it is reading, questioning and understanding what a director reasonably ought to recognise. [L1, ss 180, 189, 190, 344; J1; J2; AICD[LEG, 2025/26 V1, pp. 38–46]; AICD[FIN, 2025/26 V1, pp. 35–37]]

A well-run committee maintains a **judgment register** from early in the close, not a late cover note. Each material issue has: (1) business fact and accounting question; (2) applicable standard/section; (3) alternatives rejected and why; (4) quantified P&L, balance sheet, cash and covenant effect; (5) evidence and source owner; (6) management conclusion; (7) auditor position; (8) director challenge and resolution; (9) required note/market announcement; and (10) final sign-off owner/date. The board sees the *exceptions*, not thousands of line-item sign-offs.

### 05.07.2 Management letters and professional advice

- **CEO/CFO attestation:** records were properly maintained, key controls operated, all relevant matters and significant judgments were disclosed, forecasts and liquidity were assessed, and the financial report is complete to management's knowledge. For listed companies the statutory **s 295A** declaration must be examined separately from **ASX Governance Recommendation 4.2**'s broader control-based declaration, and its exact applicability established.
- **Divisional/functional representations:** treasury, legal, tax, HR, operations, sustainability, IT, external claims, procurement and acquired subsidiaries each confirm population completeness and known issues. A representation without ownership, substantiation or exception schedule has limited evidential value.
- **Technical advice:** independent accounting, valuation, tax or legal opinions should state the facts assumed, scope, alternatives and qualifications. Advice based on management's unsupported assumptions does not validate those assumptions.
- **Auditor written representations:** ASA 580 requires management's written representations within the audit; this does not replace other evidence and is distinct from board enquiry. Directors should know which matters relied heavily on management representations and why. [AU1]

**Analysis.** Ask an adviser, “What facts, if changed, would reverse your conclusion?” Ask management, “What evidence makes the auditor's alternative wrong?” Ask the auditor, “What is the strongest contrary evidence you obtained?” Where there is disagreement, arrange joint presentation of the same question, followed by an in-camera auditor session. The decision record must identify the actual basis for the board's conclusion rather than state that it relied on 'expert advice'. [AICD[TOOL, 2025/26 V1, pp. 17–23, 47–52]; AICD[FIN, 2025/26 V1, pp. 35–44]]

### 05.07.3 When information is missing, contradictory or late

A material missing note, unsupported impairment forecast, unexplained cash movement, undisclosed debt covenant, disputed auditor adjustment or inconsistent climate assumption is a *sign-off blocker until resolved or appropriately reflected in the report*. Directors should: identify the exact assertion at risk; obtain primary documents; require a written reconciliation; consider independence of reviewers; request alternative scenarios; involve legal/audit specialists; give the auditor any information relevant to its audit; and ask the chair to defer approval if there is no reasonable basis for an opinion. An unresolved uncertainty may require disclosure or an alternative accounting basis; not every uncertainty can be 'fixed' by adding a general caveat. If the board proceeds despite a dissenting view, minute the substance of dissent and consider individual legal advice. Never backdate the declaration or conceal the fact that evidence arrived after board consideration.

## 05.08 Internal control, fraud, accounting records, management assurance and deficiencies

### 05.08.1 Records, controls and systems

**Law.** Under Corporations Act s 286 a company must keep written financial records that correctly record/explain transactions and financial position/performance and enable true and fair financial statements to be prepared and audited. Seven-year retention is generally required. Section 290 supplies directors' access rights. Sustainability records have a distinct statutory provision in s 286A and associated requirements when a sustainability report is mandated; account for those separately. CATSI, ACNC and association recordkeeping rules have their own statutory basis and should not simply be cited as s 286. [L1–L3]

**Control architecture.** Management ordinarily implements controls; the board oversees whether the architecture and monitoring are appropriate for organisational size and complexity. Important controls include segregation of duties, bank account approvals, privileged-system access, timely reconciliations, revenue/cut-off checks, debt/covenant monitoring, master data changes, acquisition consolidation, journal-entry review, estimates committee and protected whistleblowing. Cyber resilience is a financial-reporting issue because corrupted ledgers, ransomware, unauthorised payment changes and weak access logs can undermine the existence and integrity of accounting evidence. Manual workarounds deserve explicit audit attention. [AICD[FIN, 2025/26 V1, pp. 14–22]; AICD[FSH, 2025/26 V1, pp. 6–8]; AICD[TOOL, 2025/26 V1, pp. 47–52]]

### 05.08.2 Fraud inquiry beyond the annual representation

Fraud may involve dishonest revenue recognition, intentional understatement of liabilities, false vendors, asset theft, related-party diversion or management override of controls. Under **ASA 240**, auditors assess fraud-related risks and respond under audit standards. The board's role is to establish ethical incentives, credible reporting channels and an investigation/escalation regime. Controls do not deliver zero fraud risk; senior management may override them. Auditors do not guarantee detection. External whistleblower allegations, litigation and regulator enquiries must be evaluated for financial-reporting implications even when management disputes the accusation. [AU1; AICD[LEG, 2025/26 V1, pp. 55–73]]

| Control area | Evidence of operation | Red flag and intervention |
|---|---|---|
| Close process | Calendar, task ownership, reconciliations completed before approval, reviewer logs. | Late unreviewed journals; introduce heightened journal analytics and audit enquiry. |
| Treasury/payment | Bank-signatory controls, dual approvals, bank reconciliations, payment testing. | Unusual beneficiary changes; independent verification and forensic scoping. |
| Revenue/receivables | Contract-based booking controls, cut-off tests, overdue reports and credit notes. | Fourth-quarter spike followed by returns; broaden sample to post-year-end. |
| Procurement and contracts | Vendor due diligence, tender exceptions, commitments register. | Related vendor or split invoices; review conflicts and unrecorded obligations. |
| Consolidation | Group chart, component returns, intercompany elimination, foreign exchange controls. | Unreconciled subsidiary or entity outside consolidation; involve group auditor. |
| Technology/data | Access reviews, segregation, logs, backup tests, interface reconciliations. | Senior finance admin accounts without logging; compensating controls and independent testing. |
| Sustainability datasets | Organisational boundary, metering/source evidence, emission factors, approvals. | Estimates unsupported or inconsistent with financial activity data; remediate before assurance. |

### 05.08.3 Control-deficiency governance

Management, internal audit and external audit may each discover deficiencies. Directors need classification of severity, potential and actual misstatement, responsible owner, interim mitigation, closure date and **independent validation**. An issue is not closed because management says the policy changed; ask whether the control operated over a meaningful period. Repeated weaknesses in the same subsidiary, business process or executive team justify escalated investigation. When deficiencies affect the signing period, management should explain any additional substantive testing and whether the auditor's scope, opinion or communications changed. The board should minute its response to significant deficiencies and unmet remediation, particularly if signing notwithstanding a weakness. [AU1; AICD[FIN, 2025/26 V1, pp. 16–18, 38–44]]

## 05.09 Internal audit, audit committee, charters and assurance mapping

### 05.09.1 Internal audit has a different mandate from external audit

**AICD.** Internal audit evaluates internal controls, risk management and operational processes and helps directors gain a view beyond management's routine reporting; external audit expresses a statutory assurance opinion on a defined report. Internal audit can perform thematic reviews of procurement, cyber, grants, payroll, investment approval, data controls and fraud response. It should be independent of the processes it tests, with direct access to the audit committee/chair. Not every small organisation needs a permanent internal audit department; a risk-based external co-sourced review or board-led oversight may be more proportionate. [AICD[FIN, 2025/26 V1, pp. 38–44]; AICD[TOOL, 2025/26 V1, pp. 17–23]; AICD[FSH, 2025/26 V1, p. 10]]

### 05.09.2 Audit committee: legal requirement versus governance recommendation

**Binding ASX Listing Rule 12.7.** A listed entity included in the **S&P All Ordinaries Index** at the start of its financial year must have an audit committee. A listed entity in the **S&P/ASX 300** must also meet the applicable ASX Corporate Governance Council Recommendation 4.1(a) committee composition/operation requirements, subject to new-entry timing exceptions. Outside those categories, ASX fourth-edition recommendations are generally subject to the disclose-and-explain framework, not a uniform statutory requirement to have a particular committee. Do not infer the same obligation for small charities, associations or CATSI corporations. [X2, X3]

**Fourth-edition Recommendation 4.1 (governance practice, binding for relevant ASX 300 through LR 12.7):** audit committee at least three non-executive members, majority independent, chaired by an independent director who is not board chair, plus disclosures of charter, skills and meeting attendance. Recommendation 4.2 requires CEO/CFO statement with controls context; Recommendation 4.3 addresses unaudited reports. Recommendation 7.3 addresses disclosure of internal audit arrangements. The **fourth edition** remains the operative basis as of 27 October 2026; a consultation draft for a fifth edition is not yet an adopted obligation. [X3; AICD[GAPP, 2025/26 V1, pp. 5–9]]

### 05.09.3 Original charter test

A credible audit committee charter should specify purpose and reporting relationship; skills, independence, appointment and succession; authority to inspect records and retain independent advisers; auditor appointment/reappointment, fees and non-audit services; audit plan approval/assessment; financial report and significant estimates review; fraud/whistleblower escalation; risk and internal control coordination; climate assurance perimeter and data ownership if relevant; open executive and private auditor sessions; annual performance evaluation; and reporting unresolved recommendations to the full board. Avoid giving the committee implicit authority to approve the accounts where statute reserves a directors' resolution. [AICD[TOOL, 2025/26 V1, pp. 17–23, 125–127]]

### 05.09.4 Assurance map: do not count the same evidence twice

| Risk or assertion | First line: management evidence | Second-line oversight | Third line / independent assurance | Gap the committee should identify |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Financial reporting completeness | Close reconciliations, control certifications, accounting judgments. | CFO controller, legal/tax/risk review. | Internal audit of close controls; external financial audit. | Who validates late consolidation and unusual manual entries? |
| Liquidity/covenants | Treasury forecast and loan calculations. | Finance risk/treasury committee. | Independent treasury review, audit going-concern procedures. | What independent evidence confirms lender support? |
| Asset valuation | Valuation models and asset team forecasts. | Investment/technical risk review. | Independent valuer, auditor specialists. | Are both using management's same optimistic demand curve? |
| Grant funds / NFP | Restricted grant ledger, acquittals, service-delivery reports. | Grant compliance function. | Funding-body verification, internal review, external financial audit. | Are restrictions built into liquidity and use-of-funds test? |
| Scope 1/2/3 and climate scenario | Metering, estimates, emission factors, model assumptions. | Sustainability/enterprise risk and finance validation. | ASSA 5000/5010 assurance on in-scope information. | Is assurance staged, and are unassured disclosures plainly identified? |
| Cyber financial data integrity | Access logs, interface reconciliations, backups. | Information security / risk committee. | Internal audit, penetration tests, auditor IT specialists. | Who determines whether an incident affects accounting record integrity? |

An assurance map should identify the provider, *exact claim tested*, frequency, limitations and independence. Three groups each reviewing the same management spreadsheet are not three genuinely independent tests. Committee reports must communicate critical gaps to all directors, including those who are not accounting specialists. [AICD[FIN, 2025/26 V1, pp. 38–44]; AICD[TOOL, 2025/26 V1, pp. 17–23]]

## 05.10 External audit, independence, audit planning, reasonable assurance, review engagements, findings and modified opinions

### 05.10.1 What the opinion does and does not mean

Under **ASA 200**, a financial statement audit seeks *reasonable assurance* whether the report as a whole is free from material misstatement due to fraud or error, then issues an opinion according to an applicable reporting framework. Reasonable assurance is high, not absolute: sampling, estimation, fraud concealment, professional judgment and the inherent limitations of controls mean a clean opinion is not a warranty. ASA 700 sets the reporting framework, ASA 705 modified opinions, ASA 706 emphasis/other-matter paragraphs; ASA 701 may require key audit matters for listed entity audits. [AU1]

A half-year **review** commonly applies ASRE 2410 and provides **limited assurance**, using enquiries and analytical procedures with a negative-form conclusion; it is not an audit. ACNC medium-charity review and WA Tier 2 review similarly have a lower assurance level than a full audit but may operate under different engagement requirements. Sustainability assurance has its own **ASSA 5000/5010** requirements in 05.11. Comparing these assurance engagements as though they tested identical propositions is misleading.

| Report/communication | What it indicates | What it does **not** establish |
|---|---|---|
| Unmodified (clean) audit opinion | Auditor concludes report complies materially with applicable criteria. | Every figure correct; no fraud; entity safe from future distress. |
| Qualified opinion | Material but **not pervasive** misstatement or inability to obtain sufficient appropriate evidence in a defined respect. | Overall report is wholly sound; qualification can be ignored if small net-profit impact. |
| Adverse opinion | Material and **pervasive** misstatement. | A defensible basis for directors to present a clean-compliance narrative. |
| Disclaimer of opinion | Auditor cannot obtain evidence and possible effects are material and pervasive, or other prescribed conditions. | Auditor has effectively 'passed' the accounts. |
| Emphasis of matter | Important, appropriately disclosed issue drawn to reader's attention without modifying opinion. | Problem is resolved or absence of further risk. |
| Material uncertainty related to going concern | Auditor identifies relevant material uncertainty and assesses disclosure/reporting under ASA 570. | Company is legally insolvent, or continued trading is necessarily inappropriate. |
| Limited-assurance review conclusion | Nothing came to attention causing reviewer to believe criteria materially unmet, within narrower procedures. | Positive reasonable-assurance audit opinion. |
| Key audit matters | Most significant matters in the auditor's professional judgment during that audit. | Separate opinions on each matter or list of every area of board concern. |

**Independence.** Corporations Act Part 2M.4 contains auditor independence/appointment requirements and professional ethical rules; s 307C concerns the auditor's independence declaration. Assess financial interests, related parties, extended tenure, partner rotation, non-audit services, management roles and fee dependency. For audit committees, a one-line declaration is not sufficient where a complex advisory relationship exists: require the actual services and safeguards. [L1; AU1; AICD[FIN, 2025/26 V1, pp. 40–43]]

### 05.10.2 Audit planning and the questions a director must ask

At planning, request: scope and reporting entities; group/component audit coverage; materiality and performance materiality; key fraud and management-override risks; IT and cybersecurity reliance; revenue, impairment, acquisition and provisions approach; use of specialists; internal-control deficiencies; timetable; independence review; climate-financial interface; and proposed significant audit matter communications. The committee should ask which significant business changes risk an accounting failure that is *not* in last year's audit plan. Under ASA 315/330 the auditor identifies and responds to assessed risks; the board needs to understand where the audit's evidence is likely to be weakest, not dictate a technical audit sample size. [AU1; AICD[TOOL, 2025/26 V1, pp. 17–23]]

**Audit findings evidence table:**

| Finding | Evidence the board should obtain | Required director action |
|---|---|---|
| Corrected misstatement | Adjustment log, pre-/post-correction effect and cause. | Confirm correction also updates comparatives, tax, narrative, forecasts and controls. |
| Uncorrected misstatement | Aggregated and individual dollar amounts, qualitative assessment, auditor explanation. | Independently consider materiality and request correction or clear disclosure; ask why management refused. |
| Significant accounting disagreement | Management memo, auditor alternative and technical/adviser opinion. | Understand both positions; defer if unresolved and material. |
| Scope limitation or delayed evidence | Outstanding request list, affected balances, realistic completion date. | Require records and access; do not pressure for an opinion unsupported by evidence. |
| Significant control deficiency | Cause, period of exposure, compensating procedures and auditor testing. | Assign remedial owner/date and independent verification; consider assurance implications. |
| Fraud or suspected breach | Protected investigation summary, external advice, reporting obligations. | Preserve evidence, manage conflicts, ensure regulator/law-enforcement obligations considered. |
| Going-concern matter | Scenario cash/forecasts, lender commitments, disclosures and ASA 570 conclusions. | Resolve accounting basis, debt-payment opinion and potential immediate trading decisions independently. |
| Modified opinion or emphasis | Draft exact report wording and regulatory/financing consequences. | Consider whether board can approve, disclose reasons, seek advice and communicate accurately. |

### 05.10.3 Auditor dialogue and final sign-off

The committee, then full board as necessary, should meet the engagement partner without senior management. Questions include: “Which estimate has the lowest headroom?” “Were representations substituted for documentary evidence?” “What was the largest waived or uncorrected adjustment?” “Which subsidiary or data pipeline was difficult to audit?” “Did management restrict access or delay records?” “What would lead to a changed opinion?” “Do we have a reliable post-balance-date information process?” Where a modified opinion is anticipated, assess financial, covenant, regulatory and market consequences before voting. An audit opinion often follows directors' declaration approval chronologically; coordinate the agreed audit completion and signature sequence so no party attests to a different draft. [AU1; AICD[FIFTY, 2016, pp. 11–12]; AICD[TOOL, 2025/26 V1, pp. 17–23]]

## 05.11 Climate and sustainability reporting: thresholds, AASB S2, declarations, assumptions, assurance and liability

### 05.11.1 Statutory trigger and phased groups: the date that matters is the **period start**

**Binding law.** The Treasury Laws Amendment (Financial Market Infrastructure and Other Measures) Act 2024 introduced Chapter 2M sustainability reporting, including Corporations Act ss 286A, 292A, 296A–296D and 301A and transitional Part 10.77. Section 292A is not a blanket obligation on large organisations. First, the entity must be required to prepare an **annual financial report under Chapter 2M**. Second, it must meet the applicable *corporate size*, National Greenhouse and Energy Reporting (NGER) or qualifying registered-scheme, registrable superannuation entity (RSE) or retail corporate collective investment vehicle (CCIV) asset test. The thresholds crystallise by reference to the particular financial year, so acquisitions or group changes late in the year matter. The financial report and a separate sustainability report are connected, but do not merge into one statutory financial statement. [L1, ss 292A, 296A–296D, 1707B; R2, RG 280.32–.36]

**Verified corporate-size entry matrix** (at least **two** of revenue, consolidated gross assets and employees, measured on the legislated basis; `>=` includes equality):

| Reporting cohort | First financial-year **start** within the interval | Consolidated revenue | Consolidated gross assets | Employees | Independent alternative gateways |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| **Group 1** | **1 Jan 2025–30 Jun 2026** | >= **$500m** | >= **$1bn** | >= **500** | NGER-registered/registrable entity meeting the specified NGER s 13(1)(a) group threshold. Registered schemes, RSEs and retail CCIVs excluded from the Group 1 corporate-size route. |
| **Group 2** | **1 Jul 2026–30 Jun 2027** | >= **$200m** | >= **$500m** | >= **250** | NGER registered/registrable under s 12(1); registered scheme/RSE/retail CCIV consolidated assets >= **$5bn**. |
| **Group 3** | **On or after 1 Jul 2027** | >= **$50m** | >= **$25m** | >= **100** | Additional entity-specific routes remain relevant; some qualifying Group 3 reporters may make an evidence-based no-material-risk disclosure under s 296B. |

This is a cohort-entry guide, not an assertion that a company qualifies solely by meeting a number. Group 1 NGER circumstances differ from Group 2; schemes/RSEs/CCIVs may qualify by other routes even where they are excluded from the early size route. The legal reporting period and entity's group must be fixed before assigning a row. [L1, ss 292A, 1707B; R2, RG 280 Table 2, pp. 12–13]

**Examples:** A listed for-profit enterprise with 31 December 2025 year-end (year began 1 January 2025) and revenue $600m, assets $1.1bn and 520 employees ordinarily enters Group 1. A 30 June 2026 year-end started 1 July 2025 and could also be Group 1 if it qualifies. A 30 June 2027 reporter began 1 July 2026 and enters no earlier than Group 2 on the corporate size route if it meets Group 2 levels. A 30 June 2028 year began 1 July 2027 and first tests Group 3. A small registered charity with a high-value property holding is **not** automatically subject to any of these merely because it is 'large' under ACNC size rules. [R2; R3]

### 05.11.2 AASB S2, AASB S1 and **which edition** applies

**Australian Accounting Standards Board (binding where called up by law):** Original **AASB S2 Climate-related Disclosures**, issued **20 September 2024**, applies to the initial mandatory financial years that **begin on or after 1 January 2025 but before 1 January 2027** (subject to permissible earlier adoption of the subsequent revised standard). AASB **S2025-1**, issued **15 December 2025**, revised the greenhouse-gas disclosure requirements; the amended AASB S2 version is **operative for annual periods beginning on or after 1 January 2027**, with early application permitted for periods beginning from 1 January 2025. Thus *Group 2 reporters whose year begins 1 July 2026* ordinarily use the original version unless they validly early adopt the revision. A 1 January 2027 period normally uses the revised standard. A 1 July 2027 Group 3 commencement normally uses the revised standard. [ST9, ST11]

**AASB S1** *General Requirements for Disclosure of Sustainability-related Financial Information* is **voluntary** in Australia. The mandatory AASB S2 does **not** require adoption of the whole of AASB S1; **AASB S2 Appendix D** instead supplies mandatory general requirements specifically for climate-related financial disclosures. Directors should not mistake a voluntarily marketed 'IFRS-aligned' sustainability document for fulfilment of all statutory AASB S2 requirements. AASB S2's Australian modifications include specific rules about consolidated/parent reporting and industry-specific metrics; IFRS S2 text is not automatically the Australian binding text. Under s 292A(2) a relevant parent may elect the permitted consolidated sustainability reporting approach with consequences for group members; explicitly document that election and boundary. [ST9–ST11; L1, s 292A(2)]

### 05.11.3 Content and accountable board judgment

**AASB S2** covers the four core pillars: **governance** (oversight and management roles, reporting, skills and controls); **strategy** (material physical and transition risks/opportunities, current and expected financial effects and resilience); **risk management** (identification, assessment and integration); and **metrics and targets** (including Scope 1, Scope 2 and Scope 3 greenhouse-gas disclosures where required, targets, methodology and assumptions). Section 296D specifies statutory climate content and scenario-analysis conditions; the model must reflect legally required scenarios and applicable standard paragraphs, not a single management favourite. First-year comparative information and Scope 3 relief need to be checked against the operative AASB S2 transitional clauses, rather than dismissed as permanently voluntary. [L1, ss 296C–296D; ST9, AASB S2 paras 6, 8–37 and Appendix D]

The most material director review is **financial connectivity**. If a climate report describes accelerated closure, declining long-term demand, emission-cost exposure or major adaptation capital expenditure, the financial statements' useful lives, impairment cash flows, provisions, tax assets, contracted revenue and debt projections must be independently reconciled with those assumptions. Different purposes and horizons can explain differences, but differences must be **identified and justified**, not left invisible. A scenario is a plausible conditional path, not management's base-case forecast; its effect on reported carrying values needs an accounting analysis rather than mechanical substitution. [ST4, ST9; R2, RG 280]

| Climate disclosure claim | Evidence needed before approval | Financial/accounting connection | Director challenge |
|---|---|---|---|
| Organisational and Scope 1/2 emissions | Metering, fuels, energy purchases, legal/operational boundary, emission factors and reconciliation to production or expenses. | Purchased energy and fuel costs, regulated facilities, capex and exposure. | What share is measured vs estimated; where are control gaps? |
| Scope 3 emissions | Value-chain category materiality, activity data, proxy assumptions, supplier/source records, omitted category explanation and first-year relief assessment. | Sales volumes, product mix, supply chain, customer use and underlying growth case. | Are emissions estimates consistent with reported sales volumes and forecast mix? |
| Physical risk | Site-specific hazard, geospatial exposure, insurance, downtime, capital and resilience plans. | Asset useful lives, expected maintenance, impairment and provisioning. | Which material site risk is absent from the cash-flow model? |
| Transition risk | Demand curves, carbon policy, technology, market share and decarbonisation cost assumptions, with scenario provenance. | Forecast cash flows, WACC, capital allocation, stranded assets and decommission. | Is an optimistic internal price assumption inconsistent with the disclosed external pathway? |
| Climate resilience scenarios | At least the statutory scenario requirements, model architecture, boundary, sensitivity and governance sign-off. | Downside value and liquidity analysis and near-term funding. | Which assumptions are evidence-based, and where are scenario limitations disclosed? |
| Targets and transition plan | Approved targets, base year, inventory, gross versus net emissions, carbon credit assumptions, execution capex and owners. | Future capital commitments and useful life/operating-cost estimates. | Are targets funded and actionable, or aspirations labelled as financed commitments? |
| Governance/controls | Board charter, committee agenda, competency assessment, finance/sustainability reconciliation, control testing. | Directors' declaration and assurance reliance. | How does information reach the board and who corrects an error? |
| 'No material climate risks' assertion | Enterprise-wide risk screening, scenario and materiality tests, board challenge and contemporary evidence. | Impairment, going concern, risk register and public disclosures. | What contrary evidence exists, and why would a reasonable user still not find the risk material? |

### 05.11.4 Directors' declaration: reasonable-steps transition versus future full opinion

**Law.** For sustainability reporting financial years **commencing from 1 January 2025 to 31 December 2027**, s **1707C(2)** modifies s 296A(6): directors declare whether, **in their opinion, the entity has taken reasonable steps** to ensure the sustainability report (excluding the declaration) accords with law and AASB S2. For years **commencing on or after 1 January 2028**, the ordinary s **296A(6)** opinion about accordance with legislation and standards applies. Section 296A(7) requires a board resolution, signature and date. The first three years do **not** create permission to approve information without reasonable steps. [L1; R2, RG 280.57–.60, p. 21]

**Liability.** Part 10.77 includes modified private civil liability for specific *protected statements*: Scope 3 emissions, scenario analysis and transition plans in sustainability reports for years commencing **1 January 2025–31 December 2027**, and a narrower one-year class of forward-looking climate statements for years commencing **1 January–31 December 2025**. These are not a blanket immunity; **ASIC and criminal action remain possible**, and scope/location/statement rules must be satisfied. They do not automatically shield voluntary advertisements, investor presentations or every green claim. Even outside mandatory reporting, directors face ordinary misleading-report and duty-of-care risks. [L1, ss 1707C–1707D; R2, RG 280.61–.68]

### 05.11.5 Sustainability assurance: **ASSA 5000 and amended ASSA 5010**

**Binding assurance standards.** **ASSA 5000**, *General Requirements for Sustainability Assurance Engagements*, provides the engagement framework. **ASSA 5010**, originally made **28 January 2025** (F2025L00109), sets the statutory phase-in; it was **amended 17 December 2025** by **ASSA 2025-10** (F2025L01654), effective **24 December 2025** and reflected in the AUASB compilation prepared **5 January 2026**. Do **not** use an outdated phase-in chart that precedes this amendment. The amended version applies to financial years commencing 1 January 2025–30 June 2030. The Corporations Act s **301A** requires a sustainability audit for financial years commencing **on or after 1 July 2030**; ASSA 5010 phases assurance earlier. [AU2, AU3; L1, ss 301A, 1707E]

| ASSA 5010 reporting stage | Assurance required by amended paragraph 10 | Important qualification |
|---|---|---|
| First reporting year as defined for Group 1/2/3 | **Limited assurance (review)** on AASB S2 governance; specified strategy risks/opportunities paragraphs; Scope 1 and Scope 2 emissions and related information; any assertion of no material climate risks/opportunities. | Other disclosures may be unassured in this first-year stage. Communicate the assured perimeter clearly. |
| Second and third reporting years | **Limited assurance on all** disclosures in the sustainability report (including applicable AASB S2 Appendix D general requirements). | Narrower than reasonable assurance; unassured comparatives may have relief under para 11. |
| Fourth reporting year onward | **Reasonable assurance (audit) on all** sustainability disclosures. | For Group 1, ASSA-defined fourth period first begins **1 July 2028**; Group 2 **1 July 2029**; Group 3 **1 July 2030**. Check cohort-period definitions where financial year is not 1 July–30 June. |

**Exact ASSA 5010 stage windows** under paragraph 9: Group 1 first-year commencement *1 Jan 2025–30 Jun 2026*, second *1 Jul 2026–30 Jun 2027*, third *1 Jul 2027–30 Jun 2028*, fourth *from 1 Jul 2028*; Group 2 first *1 Jul 2026–30 Jun 2027*, second *1 Jul 2027–30 Jun 2028*, third *1 Jul 2028–30 Jun 2029*, fourth *from 1 Jul 2029*; Group 3 first *1 Jul 2027–30 Jun 2028*, second *1 Jul 2028–30 Jun 2029*, third *1 Jul 2029–30 Jun 2030*, fourth *from 1 Jul 2030*. Where an entity has changed year-end, elected early reporting or moved between groups, apply paragraph 9's full definitions and any specific provisions rather than merely counting annual reports. [AU3, paras 9–11]

**Director assurance questions:** What part of the report is assured this period? Which metrics are not? Is the assurance provider legally qualified and independent? Where did the provider depend on management estimation? Are source data and records preserved? How were material measurement limitations reported? What would prevent reasonable assurance at the fourth stage? The board should commission readiness reviews before the first mandatory report; first-year limited assurance is not a reason to delay designing robust controls. [AU2–AU3; R2]

## 05.12 ACNC, CATSI/ORIC and state incorporated-association reporting, audit, review, thresholds and exemptions

### 05.12.1 ACNC: annual **revenue** is decisive

**Binding law and regulator.** Registered charity size is determined from annual **revenue**, not the balance-sheet assets or the employee thresholds used elsewhere. For ACNC reporting periods from 2022 onward the sizes are: **small under $500,000; medium $500,000 to under $3 million; large $3 million or more**. Small charities submit the Annual Information Statement (AIS) but ordinarily need not lodge an annual financial report with ACNC; medium charities submit a report **reviewed or audited**; large charities submit an **audited** annual financial report. There are Basic Religious Charity and specified other exemptions, transitional reporting arrangements and regulator discretion. For medium and large charities, the ACNC guidance recognises certain permissible SPFS or GPFS types subject to eligibility. [L2; R3, ACNC *Charity size*; R14]

Charities ordinarily file the AIS and any annual report **within six months** after the reporting period end, subject to regulator-approved extensions. **Specific verified exception:** for charities with financial year ending **30 June 2026**, ACNC has announced **31 January 2027** as the AIS due date instead of the standard **31 December 2026**; check the financial-report submission conditions and current ACNC notice for that year. This is a **2026-specific deadline concession**, not a permanent seven-month rule. [R15]

The ACNC Act, Regulations 2022 and Governance Standards apply according to their own structure; **Governance Standard 5** requires charities to take reasonable steps to ensure their Responsible People meet duties. Do not confuse that charity-level obligation with automatically applying every Corporations Act s 180–184 duty personally to every committee member. A charity's constitution, sector regulator, grant agreements and other duties can impose additional requirements. Registered charity CLGs generally have specific ASIC/ACNC reporting harmonisation; the mere legal form 'company limited by guarantee' is not a licence to ignore the ACNC register. [L1–L2; R3, R14]

### 05.12.2 CATSI/ORIC: **two separate tests**, not the ACNC charity bands

**Binding statute and regulator.** Under the CATSI Act a corporation's registered size uses *at least two* of income, assets and staff conditions. As verified from ORIC guidance (2 May 2025):

| CATSI corporation classification | CGOI (size test) | Consolidated gross assets (size test) | Employees (size test) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| **Small** | < **$100,000** | < **$100,000** | < **5** |
| **Medium** | **$100,000–< $5m** | **$100,000–< $2.5m** | **5–24** |
| **Large** | >= **$5m** | >= **$2.5m** | >= **25** |

These are classification components, **not** a rule that all three must be satisfied. Check ORIC's register and applicable CATSI regulations for an entity whose criteria straddle thresholds, and whether special RNTBC reporting applies. [L3; R4]

**Reporting is a second-stage test:**

| Registered CATSI size plus CGOI for year | Reports lodged with ORIC, ordinarily by **31 December** after 30 June year-end |
|---|---|
| Small and CGOI **< $100,000** | General report only. |
| Small and CGOI **$100,000–< $5m** | General report, financial report, audit report (or legally qualifying government-funder alternative). |
| Small and CGOI **>= $5m** | General, financial, audit **and directors'** reports. |
| Medium and CGOI **< $5m** | General, financial and audit reports (or qualifying funding alternative). |
| Medium and CGOI **>= $5m** | General, financial, audit and directors' reports. |
| Large (any CGOI) | General, financial, audit and directors' reports. |

**Important boundary-source inconsistency.** ORIC's current *financial reports* page says a small corporation with CGOI **$100,000 or more** needs the financial report; its *audit reports* page says a small corporation needs an audit for **more than $100,000** and says **$100,000 or less** does not. ORIC's 2020 reporting guide and 2025 reporting table support the **$100,000 or more** interpretation for the financial/audit package. For a corporation exactly at **$100,000**, obtain the applicable CATSI regulation and **written ORIC confirmation**, rather than claiming the website conflict has been conclusively cured. [R5, R6, R16]

ORIC permits a specified alternative in certain small/medium cases if **more than 90%** of total income came from government grants and the report meets the required conditions; this is not a general waiver of financial accountability. **CATSI/ACNC overlap:** registration as a charity does not automatically switch the primary CATSI reporting package to ACNC's revenue-based tiers. ACNC guidance recognises CATSI corporations' distinct reporting treatment, including AIS exemptions in relevant circumstances. The registered native title body corporate (RNTBC) also must account for native title decision-making and beneficiary responsibilities, which are not replaced by a cash-flow audit. [L2–L3; R5–R6; AICD[LAPP, 2025/26 V1, pp. 5, 9–14]]

### 05.12.3 Eight state and territory association regimes: verified comparison

The AICD legal appendix's comparative table is explicitly **current as at March 2024**. The following is a **2026 research update**, not an extension of that historical table. In every case, the association's own Act and regulations, constitution, charity harmonisation, licensing and funding conditions matter independently. [AICD[LAPP, 2025/26 V1, p. 5]; R7–R13]

| Jurisdiction / Act | Current verified classification and assurance position | Reporting to members, regulator and charity overlay |
|---|---|---|
| **WA** — *Associations Incorporation Act 2015* | Revenue **Tier 1 < $500k** (no routine statutory audit/review); **Tier 2 $500k–< $3m** (review); **Tier 3 >= $3m** (audit). Tier 2 audit may instead be required by members' resolution or Commissioner; annual report includes committee declaration for Tiers 2/3. | Financial statements/review/audit presented to members at AGM. State revenue methodology and possible Commissioner discretionary tier relief matter. [R7] |
| **NSW** — *Associations Incorporation Act 2009* | Revised **27 Jun 2025**: **Tier 1** gross receipts **> $500k OR current assets > $1m**, ordinarily audit; **Tier 2** gross receipts <= $500k AND current assets <= $1m, less demanding. | Generally annual financial summary within **1 month of AGM** and no later than **7 months after year-end**. ACNC-registered charity reporting can fully substitute from **1 Apr 2026** only if explicit conditions met, including AIS/financial compliance, publicly available reports and **not** part of an ACNC-reporting group. [R8] |
| **Victoria** — *Associations Incorporation Reform Act 2012* and 2023 Regulations | For FY **ending on/after 1 Jul 2024**: Tier 1 **< $500k** revenue; Tier 2 **$500k–$3m** (review); Tier 3 **> $3m** (audit); older $250k/$1m bands superseded. | Financial statements to AGM members (normally AGM within 5 months); ACNC-specific exemption from CAV annual statement may apply. | **Queensland** — *Associations Incorporation Act 1981* | Uses gross receipts **and** current asset tests. Broadly Level 1 where receipts > $500k **or** current assets > $1m; Level 2 for intermediate amounts (e.g., $150k–$500k receipts / $300k–$1m assets); Level 3 for small associations. [R10] |
| **South Australia** — *Associations Incorporation Act 1985* | 'Prescribed associations' generally where statutory **gross receipts > $500k**, with exclusions for membership subscriptions, bequests and non-trading asset-sale proceeds; require annual **audit** and periodic reporting, subject to relief. | An ACNC-registered prescribed association that has lodged AIS and financial report with ACNC need not separately lodge the periodic return with SA CBS under the published rule. [R11] |
| **Australian Capital Territory** — *Associations Incorporation Act 1991* and Regulation 2023 | Following reforms effective **1 Feb 2024**, associations still prepare annual statements and maintain supporting documents for seven financial years, but the former blanket annual-return lodgement to Access Canberra **no longer applies**. Review/audit obligations depend on operative Act/rules and entity facts; exact monetary review-tier cut-offs are **not independently established** in this source review. | Documents must be produced to Registrar-General on request, generally within two weeks. ACNC reporting overlay may reduce duplication. Obtain current Act/guide for audit test. [R12] |
| **Tasmania** — *Associations Incorporation Act 1964* | Regulator guidance uses annual revenue **$250k** as key annual financial audit threshold: below threshold audit removed unless constitution requires; above threshold annual audit with statutory report. [R13] |

**Why the distinctions matter.** A $400k-revenue WA incorporated club is Tier 1 and not ordinarily audited. A $400k-revenue NSW association with $1.2m current assets can be Tier 1 and subject to a larger-association audit regime. A $140k-revenue NT non-trading association may still need an *independent check* even though a comparably sized WA association might not. An ACNC-registered NSW charity might be exempt from NSW filings but only if every statutory harmonisation condition is satisfied. A state-law tier does not dictate ACNC charity size or CATSI status. [R7–R17]

### 05.12.4 Director decision gate for non-corporate entities

Ask for a one-page legal-form and regulator map; size calculation with underlying ledger; annual legal filing/AGM calendar; permitted statement type; external review/audit qualification and independence; grant or constitutional conditions requiring a stronger assurance level; and a committee declaration phrased under the **correct law**. For a very small association without a compulsory professional audit, board members should still insist on dual bank-signatories, bank-to-ledger reconciliation, restricted funding schedules, cash runway and transparent member reporting. Proportionality reduces cost, **not** the requirement to keep accurate records and account to members. For RNTBCs, add reporting to common-law holders and other affected people in ways authorised by the relevant rulebook and native-title framework; do not infer all such people are corporation members. Cross-references: Files 06 and 07.

## 05.13 Listed-company announcements, continuous disclosure, unaudited results and governance reporting

### 05.13.1 Continuous disclosure and the financial-report close

**Binding law/rules.** ASX Listing Rule **3.1**, subject to its exceptions including LR **3.1A**, requires disclosure of market-sensitive information in accordance with the rule. Corporations Act **ss 674 and 674A**, as applicable, address continuous disclosure, with statutory elements and defences. Directors should not defer market-sensitive information merely because the accounts are awaiting audit. Revenue impairment, a change in debt maturity/covenant position, material forecast downgrade, undisclosed acquisition liability or materially inconsistent climate/asset assessment may each raise a separate market-disclosure question. Disclosure must be timely, accurate and balanced, even where estimates are preliminary. [L1; X1–X3; AICD[LAPP, 2025/26 V1, pp. 6–8]]

In-house disclosure committees can triage information rapidly but must provide escalation paths to the board, chair, CEO and company secretary. Directors should request an announcement-to-financial-report reconciliation: all significant announcements considered for recognition/disclosure, and all material year-end issues evaluated for prior or immediate announcement obligations. Confidential incomplete negotiations may qualify for LR 3.1A only if *all relevant conditions* remain met; a blanket 'audit embargo' does not exist.

### 05.13.2 Preliminary/unaudited numbers and governance processes

**ASX LR 4.3A** preliminary final report (Appendix 4E) and **LR 4.2A** half-year report (Appendix 4D) create time-sensitive reporting obligations. The preliminary full-year announcement can precede the final signed financial statements. This is legitimate if accurately identified and the numbers have appropriate internal verification. A material later difference requires prompt board consideration of correction or further market disclosure and evaluation of any record/control failure. ASX Governance Recommendation **4.3** asks listed entities to disclose the process used to verify periodic corporate reports not audited or reviewed by an external auditor. Recommendation **4.2** concerns CEO/CFO declarations before approval, with statutory s 295A distinguished. [X1–X3; AICD[GAPP, 2025/26 V1, pp. 5–7]]

| Public communication | Assurance status question | Governance evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Preliminary final results | Audited, partly audited, undergoing audit, or genuinely unaudited? | Draft-to-final bridge, CEO/CFO sign-off, materiality and ASX timing check. |
| 'Underlying' EBITDA/NPAT or adjusted metric | Is reconciliation to statutory results complete, neutral and consistent? | Reconciliation, one-off classifications, cash conversion and future AASB 18 MPM mapping. |
| Guidance revision | Are assumptions consistent with impairment models, liquidity and board strategy? | Updated forecast, sensitivity, disclosure committee rationale and board approval as appropriate. |
| Investor presentation | Does it add substantive information that must be first released to ASX? | Announcement log, presentation version and release timing. |
| Sustainability/transition statement | Is it statutory or voluntary; what standard/assurance covers it? | Disclosure register, underlying climate data and legal review of the specific claim. |
| Corporate governance statement | Which fourth-edition recommendations were followed; where is 'if not, why not' explanation? | Committee composition, charters, internal audit and management representation evidence. |

**Analysis.** Disclosed profit is not the whole risk profile. A director should assess whether a report's prominent 'growth' narrative is economically compatible with negative free cash, leverage, capital intensity, material impairments and outlook uncertainty. Inconsistent adjusted-profit claims and unsupported climate forecasts can mislead even where the full financial statements are technically compliant. [AICD[FIN, 2025/26 V1, pp. 64–79]; X1–X3]

## 05.14 Original director-facing sign-off framework: 2016 *Fifty Matters* to current reporting practice

### 05.14.1 Provenance, purpose and boundaries

**Historical teaching, not binding law.** The AICD's *Fifty Matters to be Considered before Signing a Company's Financial Statements*, third edition, **October 2016**, was designed as an inquiry aid for directors preparing to approve company accounts. Its enduring insight is that directors need to understand major areas of financial risk and judgment and pursue unsatisfactory explanations. It expressly was not an exhaustive checklist for every company. The separate, more recent AICD Director Tools collection includes a differently presented financial-statement sign-off tool. Neither is a legislative instrument, an Australian Accounting Standard or an auditor's work program. [AICD[FIFTY, 2016, pp. 4–12]; AICD[TOOL, 2025/26 V1, pp. 47–52]]

This section sets out an **original risk-to-evidence framework**, not a paraphrase of the original fifty numbered questions. Its unit of analysis is a *sign-off proposition*: what must be true, what independent evidence tests it, what inconsistency could falsify it and what the board must do before approving the report. A checklist can identify a question; it cannot establish that the answer is supported. For a registered charity, association or CATSI corporation, use the framework proportionately but replace the underlying legal sign-off statement and filing route with its own regime (05.02 and 05.12).

### 05.14.2 Twelve sign-off propositions with director questions and minimum evidence

| Proposition directors should establish | New director-level inquiry | Required evidence or countercheck | Potential board response if not established |
|---|---|---|---|
| **01. Reporting perimeter is lawful and complete** | Which entities, branches, funds, joint arrangements and controlled operations have been included, and why? | Entity register; legal-structure map; control analyses; consolidation entries; unresolved intercompany balances; external accountant opinion on disputed perimeter. | Amend scope, obtain technical opinion and defer approval if material. |
| **02. Statements tell a consistent economic story** | Why does reported profit differ from operating cash and management's prior forecasts? | Profit-to-cash bridge; year-on-year movements; budget-to-actual bridge; adjusted-to-statutory reconciliation; board reporting history. | Reconcile differences; assess control failure or misleading narrative. |
| **03. Every material asset is supportable** | How do we know the carrying value is recoverable, given market, performance and strategy? | Impairment models, fair-value reports, useful lives, valuation assumptions, forecast approvals, post-year-end sales evidence. | Re-run cases, use external specialist, revise or disclose uncertainty. |
| **04. Every material liability has been recognised or disclosed** | Which legal, contractual, environmental, employee and tax obligations could still be missing? | Contracts, legal confirmations, claims register, supplier statement reconciliations, provisions paper, contingent-liability register. | Commission omitted-liability search and legal review. |
| **05. Cash is available when obligations fall due** | Which balances are unrestricted, and how are the next twelve months' debts funded under stress? | Bank statements and restricted-cash schedule; 13-week cash forecast; longer runway and facility schedule; covenant tests; funding confirmations. | Challenge solvency; secure funding or obtain restructuring advice; do not make an unsupported declaration. |
| **06. Recognition and presentation follow the correct framework** | Are revenue, expenses, leases, debt and distributions recognised in the right period and category? | Accounting policy papers; cutoff tests; major contracts; restatement/standards-change register; classification memoranda. | Correct accounting, expand notes and explain changes. |
| **07. Management's estimates withstand challenge** | What is the plausible range of outcomes, and what would make management's chosen point estimate wrong? | Assumption owners, sources, sensitivities, back-testing, alternative scenarios and model validation. | Require independent review or appropriately disclose estimation uncertainty. |
| **08. Transactions and interests are transparent** | Which insiders or related parties benefited, and were approval and disclosure obligations satisfied? | Director interest declarations; related-party ledgers; board approvals; counterparty mapping; market terms evidence. | Seek legal advice and correct recognition/disclosure or governance actions. |
| **09. Controls make the numbers credible** | What failed this year; how do we know remaining exceptions do not compromise the report? | Close-control results; reconciliations; fraud reports; control deficiencies; remediation validation; management sub-certification. | Expand substantive checks, disclose unresolved material issues, commission assurance. |
| **10. All external assurance findings are understood** | Which audit adjustments, disagreements or control weaknesses remain uncorrected, and why? | Auditor findings, uncorrected-misstatement schedule, independence declaration, audit committee minutes, private-session notes. | Obtain auditor explanation, reassess aggregation, correct or decline to approve. |
| **11. Events and communications agree** | What happened after balance date; what have we already told regulators, investors, members and funders? | Subsequent-events roll-forward; new contracts; lawyer's updated claims view; announcement log; funding correspondence. | Amend recognition/disclosures, give timely market notice where required. |
| **12. Applicable climate and other statutory statements are evidenced** | Does the entity actually qualify, which version of the standard applies and do numbers connect to the financial accounts? | Size test and period-start memo; sustainability boundaries; emissions evidence; climate-to-financial reconciliation; assurance scope; legally correct directors' declaration. | Resolve regulatory scope, correct data or assumptions, qualify and obtain advice as necessary. |

**How to use it.** Give each proposition a single accountable management owner, evidence index, materiality level, assurance source, exceptions and closure status. The director asks at least one *disconfirming* question: for example, whether an adverse external transaction contradicts a valuation model, or whether an omitted contract would change the cash runway. The audit committee should present a brief exceptions report to the full board, rather than certify that all boxes were ticked. An unresolved material item has to remain visible as unresolved. [L1, ss 180, 189, 190, 295, 344; AU1; AICD[FIN, 2025/26 V1, pp. 35–44]; AICD[TOOL, 2025/26 V1, pp. 17–23, 47–52]]

### 05.14.3 Documented **2016-to-2026 comparison**

| 2016 *Fifty Matters* domain (historic location) | Enduring governance principle | Verified contemporary change or qualification as at 27 October 2026 | Implication for present sign-off |
|---|---|---|---|
| True and fair; areas of judgment (2016 pp. 6–7) | Directors interrogate major discretion and completeness. | Current materiality, accounting policy and disclosure requirements depend on period and tier; director obligations remain under Chapter 2M. [L1; ST2, ST5–ST6] | Demand quantified sensitivities, not assurance that 'nothing significant changed'. |
| Special-purpose financial reports (p. 7) | Decide whether the claimed reporting framework is justified. | AASB 2020-2 removed SPFS for specified for-profit private-sector entities for periods beginning on/after **1 July 2021**; remaining SPFS possibilities and transitional approaches must be tested, not presumed. Future NFP Tier 3 is not yet operative. [ST6–ST8] | Written eligibility and users analysis; do not replicate the 2016 general SPFS option. |
| Emerging accounting standards (p. 7) | Assess adoption and transitional comparatives. | AASB 9, 15 and 16 subsequently changed classification, revenue and leasing; **AASB 18** is prospective from **2027** for most relevant for-profit entities and **2028** for NFP/superannuation entities, subject to early application. [ST1, ST3, ST7] | Obtain detailed standards-impact plan, especially management-defined performance measures and restated comparisons. |
| Controls and regulatory compliance (pp. 7–8) | Ensure systems and compliance are adequate. | Modern data/cloud, third-party outsourcing, cyber and climate data carry cross-system risks. [L1; R2; AU1] | Test completeness across tax, legal, treasury, sustainability and operations systems. |
| Listed disclosure and CEO/CFO assurance (p. 8) | Ensure timely reporting and corroborated senior assurances. | ASX LR 3.1/4, fourth-edition Corporate Governance Recommendations 4.2/4.3, and applicable s 295A have distinct legal force. | Investments, goodwill, stock and receivables (p. 9) | Avoid overstated assets and omitted losses. | AASB 9 expected credit loss, AASB 16 right-of-use assets, contemporary impairment methodology and sector-specific asset exposures can change tests. [ST3–ST4] | Ask what market transactions and stress cases would invalidate each value. |
| Obligations, covenants and related parties (pp. 10–11) | Record all present liabilities and conflicts. | Later practice is more attentive to lease liabilities, supplier financing, guarantees, side arrangements, climate-related restoration obligations, and group/related-party mappings. [ST3–ST4] | Request contract and covenant inventories, not merely general CFO confirmation. |
| Dividends and going concern (p. 11) | Fund distributions prudently and assess cash. | Corporations Act **s 254T** governs dividends for companies; AASB 101's going-concern horizon is not the s 95A statutory solvency test. A 12-month forecast from the *approval date* is a prudent additional director test, not the precise universal AASB 101 minimum. [L1; ST2; AU1] | Separate distribution legality, short-term debts, committed facilities and going-concern disclosures. |
| External/internal auditors and final review (pp. 11–12) | Obtain independent views and address exceptions. | Auditor reporting, material uncertainty, key audit matters, significant deficiencies and limited-scope sustainability reviews have differentiated standards. ## 05.15 Worked reporting cases and evidential lessons

### 05.15.1 **Centro: ASIC v Healey**, Federal Court, **2011** (real litigation)

**Primary judicial holding and regulatory facts.** In *Australian Securities and Investments Commission v Healey* **[2011] FCA 717**, Middleton J found that Centro directors had failed to exercise required care and diligence in relation to 2007 accounts. The case concerned mistakes directors could have detected on reading the reports and applying their knowledge of the organisation, notably classification of substantial short-term borrowings as non-current and disclosure omissions concerning major guarantees. ASIC's contemporaneous annual report describes approximately **$1.5 billion** of Centro Properties Group debt and **$598 million** of Centro Retail Group debt requiring current classification, and guarantees of approximately **US$1.75 billion** requiring appropriate reporting disclosure. These figures are ASIC's reported case summary, not amounts reconstructed from a full independent group accounts review. [J1; AICD[FIN, 2025/26 V1, pp. 11, 35–37]; AICD[TOOL, 2025/26 V1, pp. 95–96]]

**Verified judicial emphasis.** The judgment at **[20]** and **[22]–[24]** addresses directors' responsibility to read and understand financial reports and apply their own minds; the findings at **[583]–[584]** distinguish the directors' errors from any technical accountant-only responsibility. Directors were not required to be technical accounting specialists. The critical facts were prominent enough that reasonable directors familiar with their business should have interrogated them. Management and audit committee processes were not substitutes for individual scrutiny. In *ASIC v Healey (No 2)* **[2011] FCA 1003**, the court declined to relieve directors from liability merely because they had acted honestly and relied on professional advice. See also AICD Legal Appendix pp. 3–4. [J1–J2; L1, ss 180, 189, 190, 344]

**Original board counterfactual.** Assume a board pack shows $2.1 billion of facilities maturing within twelve months but the balance sheet classifies only $0.4 billion as current. Before approval a director would (1) compare the facilities maturity schedule with the current/non-current debt note; (2) ask whether an **unconditional contractual right at the reporting date** to defer settlement exists; (3) demand the accountant's standards memo and auditor's explicit view; (4) rerun the 12-month cash and covenant forecast; and (5) minute the resolution only after correcting classification and explaining any liquidity risk. If the $1.7 billion difference remained unresolved, the correct action is to **not approve the present draft**, rather than merely record a question and sign. These numbers are **hypothetical for teaching** and are not the Centro accounts.

**Limit of the precedent.** Centro does not mean directors must duplicate all audit work, personally verify every invoice or never rely on specialists. It means reliance has limits, especially where obvious features contradict what directors know or should notice. The specific Centro findings do not establish a universal current ratio, audit committee size or automatic liability for a later commercial loss. [J1–J2]

**Worked test 1: operating cash and a non-GAAP 'cash available' bridge.** The historical directors' commentary reports **$87.1m** operating cash after lease-liability and lease-interest payments. Cash flow statement net operating cash was **$131.176m**. Subtracting reported lease principal **$37.072m** and lease interest **$10.816m** yields **$83.288m**, not $87.1m. This is **an unreconciled difference between two stated measures on the evidence extracted**, not proof of a misstatement: the definitions, treatment of interest and other components require the preparer's full bridge. A director should request that bridge and identify which number funds dividends or capital investment. The reported cash-flow line remains $131.176m. [AICD[NICK, 2024 annual report, pp. 5, 25, 30]]

**Worked test 2: illustrative self-funding after selected cash obligations.** Starting with CFO **131.176**, less lease principal **37.072**, lease interest **10.816**, property, plant and equipment purchases **27.898**, purchased intangibles **0.557**, and cash dividends **56.700**, gives **–$1.867m** for this particular analytical cash residual. This is **not** statutory free cash flow, nor a finding of financing weakness: it explicitly omits acquisitions, bank-loan principal, term-deposit investments and other investing/financing movements, and avoids claiming every line is accounting-equivalent. It shows why a director should not equate high operating cash before leases with internally financed acquisitions and dividends. [AICD[NICK, 2024 annual report, pp. 25, 30]]

**Worked test 3: goodwill and management's UK outlook.** The 2024 UK transaction created approximately **$27.6m** of provisional goodwill despite purchase consideration of **$6.5m**, because fair-valued identifiable net liabilities and other adjustments shape purchase accounting. The report anticipated UK trading disruption after acquisition. The auditor identified business-combination accounting as a **key audit matter**, not a modified audit opinion. Directors should demand the provisional purchase-price allocation, valuation of identifiable assets/liabilities, forecast conversion/integration costs, cash-generating-unit (CGU) boundary and the evidence supporting goodwill recoverability. Do not describe the acquisition as uneconomic merely because the goodwill exceeds cash consideration. [AICD[NICK, 2024 annual report, pp. 5–7, 34–36, 46–48, 52–55]]

**Board resolution example (analytical, not company history).** The minutes would identify the cash/deposit distinction, note the reconciliation requested for the $87.1m management measure, confirm the accounting papers and independent auditor discussion on the Fabb combination, and identify any follow-up review of goodwill assumptions. It would **not** conclude from the historical report alone that Nick Scali had a defective audit, breached a covenant or was insolvent.

Surf Life Saving Queensland became a **company limited by guarantee on 20 January 2021** after previously operating as a Queensland incorporated association. Its registration and legal-form history are reasons to confirm the particular reporting framework and ACNC/Corporations Act relief in the year under review. [AICD[FAPP, 2025/26 V1, pp. 7–14]]

AICD's appendix identifies an approximately **$55m** construction borrowing facility for new aircraft. Directors should request the full debt roll-forward, acquisitions/noncash funding details and documentary confirmations rather than assume those two schedules must match without adjustments. The dramatic increase in assets/liabilities is economically compatible with aircraft investment, but makes cash forecasting and debt-service concentration more important. [AICD[FAPP, 2025/26 V1, pp. 9–14]]

The appendix's current-ratio teaching treats an NFP ratio of 2–3× as a preferred benchmark and concludes favourably on ability to pay debts. **Analytical qualification:** no single working-capital ratio or apparent availability of cash proves compliance with every future payment, unrestricted-grant condition or legal cash-flow insolvency test. Seek a 13-week cash forecast, funding restrictions, seasonal fundraising sensitivities, aircraft maintenance/repayment schedule, grant deliverables and board-approved aviation strategy before forming an opinion. A grant received in cash may be accompanied by an obligation to deliver services and cannot simply be distributed to other programs. The directors' risk is a mistaken inference from *accounting liquidity* to *available cash*. [AICD[FAPP, 2025/26 V1, pp. 10–14]; L1, s 95A; ST2]

**A realistic small-charity contrast.** A completely different, *hypothetical*, volunteer-run WA charity association with **$420,000 annual revenue**, no special member/Commissioner direction, no group or other special reporting conditions, would ordinarily be a **small** ACNC charity and a **Tier 1** WA association. It would not automatically need an externally audited annual financial report under either base-size test, although it still needs accurate records, the ACNC Annual Information Statement, member statements, grant acquittals, cash controls and any assurance required by the constitution or funding deed. Contrast this with the multi-million-dollar SLSQ group without imposing its audit architecture on every small charity. [L2; R3, R7, R14–R15]

### 05.15.4 **Hypothetical: climate disclosure and financial-impairment inconsistency**

**Entirely fictional case.** *Aurora Metals Ltd* is an invented ASX-listed mining group. Its financial year starts **1 July 2026** and ends **30 June 2027**. Assume its applicable size measurements are group revenue **$280m**, consolidated gross assets **$550m** and **210** employees. It is a Chapter 2M annual reporting entity, not an ACNC charity or CATSI corporation. Under the phased size test it meets **two of the three Group 2** criteria (revenue at least $200m and assets at least $500m) despite fewer than 250 employees. Its FY2027 reporting period is therefore **within Group 2's commencement window**, subject to confirming the statutory measurement rules and any exemptions. The firm did not early adopt any revised standard. [L1, s 292A; R2]

Management announces that a coal-linked processing plant will be phased out by **2035** under its public transition strategy. Yet the FY2027 impairment model assumes cash flow from that same plant through **2045**, excludes the material transition costs included in board planning, and uses favourable long-term commodity assumptions inconsistent with the adverse case approved in strategic reviews. Its recorded CGU carrying value is a fictional **$300m**, with model headroom of only **$15m**. A **$20m** fall in recoverable amount, all else equal, would more than consume that headroom. This is a sensitivity, **not a prediction or independently justified impairment charge**. The first director question is not which answer the market would prefer, but *whether the plans and assumptions genuinely differ and why*. [ST4; ST9]

**Director evidence and response:**

1. **Applicability and version.** Document Group 2 calculation, 1 July 2026 period start, the *original September 2024* AASB S2 version applying for that period absent permitted early application of a later revision, and relevant first-year transitional relief. AASB S1 is not made universally mandatory by this example. [R2; ST9, ST11]
2. **Model-to-model bridge.** Obtain a table of FY2027–2045 operating years, carbon-related costs, replacement capex, closure provisions, growth assumptions and terminal values for the impairment model, transition strategy, cash forecast and sustainability report. Name the owner of every material difference. [ST4; ST9]
3. **Evidence and scenario challenge.** Ask whether the public 2035 date is an enforceable policy, funded plan, aspirational target or strategy under review. If the 2035 cessation is genuinely management's best estimate, identify the accounting consequences for useful lives, impairment and restoration provisions; if not, correct or explain the external transition claim. [ST4; ST9]
4. **Assurance boundary.** Under the **December 2025-amended ASSA 5010**, the first reporting year's mandatory assurance concentrates on prescribed limited-assurance subject matter, **not an audit opinion over all climate statements**. Audit/limited assurance may still raise inconsistencies that require a director response. [AU2–AU3]
5. **Declaration, minutes, disclosure and timing.** Test the applicable transitional reasonable-steps directors' sustainability declaration, document reconciliation and material uncertainty, and determine whether an ASX LR 3.1 announcement is already required. If the contradiction remains material and unexplained, postpone the declaration, correct assumptions or disclosures, seek specialist advice, and document the board's reasoning. [L1; X1; R2]

**So what.** Even technically compliant climate reporting can reveal a weakness in the financial accounts, and vice versa. An auditor's unmodified *financial* opinion and the limited sustainability review cover different scopes; neither resolves the board's strategic inconsistency merely by existing. Nor does in-scope status imply that every smaller charity, RNTBC or non-reporting association must file AASB S2 statements.

### 05.15.5 HIH and Slater & Gordon: historical comparators, not generic allegations

**HIH Insurance (real 2001 collapse).** The AICD governance appendix, summarising the **2003 HIH Royal Commission**, identifies chronic under-provisioning of outstanding insurance claims, inadequate evaluation of actuarial assumptions, questionable reinsurance treatment, weak financial systems and insufficient reporting at the individual regulated-entity level. The AICD extract estimates under-provisioning contributed approximately **$2.6–$4.3 billion** of the ultimate deficit; this is an attributed Royal Commission-era historical range, not a new calculation. It reports that actuarial material was not directly scrutinised by the full board or audit committee despite the exceptional sensitivity of reserves. **Director application:** require independent reserve range estimates, underlying loss triangles and catastrophe assumptions, evidence of *real* risk transfer, legal-entity reconciliations, dissenting actuary views and access to technical experts before approving statements. The risk is not merely 'weak governance'; it is the potential failure of a central liability valuation and the masking of that failure through inappropriate transactions. [AICD[GAPP, 2025/26 V1, pp. 26–31]; J5]

**Slater & Gordon (real 2015–17 distress).** The same AICD appendix recounts aggressive international acquisition growth, material work-in-progress valuation uncertainty, high leverage, disputed historical cash conversion and substantial later goodwill-related write-downs. It reports a **nearly $1 billion** impairment charge in February 2016 and a subsequent restructuring with severe shareholder dilution. These are historical course-sourced facts, not judicial findings made in this document about directors' individual culpability. **Director application:** after acquisitions, separately measure purchased revenue, cash collection of unbilled work, contingent fee recoverability, receivables ageing, goodwill headroom, covenant sensitivity and the path from forecast synergies to free cash. A profitable accounting growth narrative can coexist with worsening debt and poor cash conversion. [AICD[GAPP, 2025/26 V1, pp. 20–25]]

**Comparative lesson.** HIH highlights **understated liabilities and distorted risk transfer**; Slater & Gordon highlights **possibly overstated assets, aggressive growth and financing resilience**; Centro highlights **directors' responsibility to notice and correct material reporting omissions**. These are three different factual mechanisms. Avoid collapsing them into a universal allegation that every unsuccessful strategy constitutes unlawful financial reporting. The shared director discipline is *to identify what valuation or classification proposition could fail, what independent evidence would expose that failure, and what action follows before signature*. [AICD[GAPP, 2025/26 V1, pp. 20–31]; J1; J5]

## 05.16 Comprehensive directors' pre-signing process: approximately **30, 14 and 7 days** before approval

### 05.16.1 Purpose and assignment of decision rights

The timetable is a **governance design**, not a statutory rule requiring meetings on those exact days. It assumes a significant audited annual Chapter 2M report with an audit committee; the tasks can be compressed or adjusted proportionately for a medium charity review, a CATSI filing or a small association. Statutory deadlines are fixed independently. **D** means the date the full board is scheduled to resolve to approve the statements and make its applicable directors' declaration. Work must start earlier for complex multinationals, acquisitions, material estimates, or first-year mandatory climate reports. [L1; AICD[TOOL, 2025/26 V1, pp. 3–11, 17–23, 47–52]; AICD[FIN, 2025/26 V1, pp. 35–44]]

| Gate | Owner of preparation | Required submissions and director enquiry | Required decision and documentation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **D–30: scope, completeness and risk** | CFO, financial controller, company secretary, legal, tax, treasury, sustainability lead as relevant | Entity/reporting and climate classification memo; statutory calendar; consolidated trial balance; draft statements; major accounting papers; all material contracts; legal claims register; impairment and cash models; draft audit findings; prior-year action log; filing routes. | Audit committee sets *open matters register*, materiality thresholds, issues requiring external advice, ownership and delivery dates. Document any new reporting regime, standards transition and unresolved 2016/current-law differences. |
| **D–30: systems and audit readiness** | Management control owners, internal audit and external audit partner | Accounts reconciliations, bank confirmations, access rights, estimated loss reserves, audit requests outstanding, fraud inquiries, known cyber incidents, related-party completeness checks. | Require a full list of significant deficiencies and outstanding audit evidence. Where a pervasive control break is identified, require wider independent testing, not only a remediation promise. |
| **D–14: challenge judgments and risk** | CFO/CEO, audit committee chair, auditor, legal/technical advisers | Near-final financial/notes draft and subsequent-event update; valuation ranges; covenant and lender letters; 13-week and 12-month liquidity forecasts; signed management representation draft; uncorrected audit adjustments; climate-financial consistency map. | Committee works through individual contentious issues; identify adjusted/unadjusted numbers, consensus and disagreements, accounting positions and material risks. Recommend disclosure changes and required follow-up; no board approval yet. |
| **D–14: separate auditor dialogue** | Audit committee chair and audit engagement partner | Private meeting without management: fraud concerns, resistance to audit enquiries, management bias, impairment challenge, significant misstatements, material uncertainty about going concern, audit scope/independence, report form. | Record substantive concerns and follow-up ownership in committee minutes. Preserve access to audit committee and board chair for immediate escalation. |
| **D–7: near-final sign-off pack** | CFO, company secretary, legal, assurance leads | Version-controlled near-final statements, directors' report and CEDS if required, sustainability report/assurance status where applicable, updated liquidity and covenants, audit report near-final, signed CEO/CFO declaration where required, material judgment register, disclosure/market announcement reconciliations. | Circulate clear exception summary to *all* directors; require each member to read and ask questions. Document risks where financial-report approval could be delayed. |
| **D–7: closure discipline** | CFO, company secretary, audit chair | Open-item log with proof of corrections, owner, auditor response and independent check; confirmation of time-bound subsequent-events review to D; final wording of declarations, signatures and filing instruction. | Classify each item: **closed with evidence; immaterial accepted with rationale; material unresolved, blocks approval**. No 'deemed closed' by passage of time. |
| **D day: board decision** | Whole board, chair, company secretary | Exact final version and change log; latest management/auditor reports; resolution wording; current cash, covenant and events update; dissent or abstention disclosures. | Resolve to approve **only if satisfied**; record basis, advice, key judgments, exceptions, dissent and necessary signatures; authorise actual lodgement and disclosure. |
| **D+1 and afterwards** | Company secretary, finance, investor relations, charity/CATSI reporting officer | ASIC/ASX/ACNC/ORIC or state lodgement receipts, market release, public availability, member distribution, closed action register, lessons learned. | Confirm filing completion and address any subsequent material discovery through legal disclosure/correction processes. |

### 05.16.2 The five matters that must not be lost in formal minutes

Minutes should reliably record **what the board decided and why**, not indiscriminately reproduce privileged advice or every individual question. A proportionate record includes: **(1)** the exact version(s), period and legal entity approved; **(2)** the evidence, audit committee and auditor materials on which the board substantially relied; **(3)** major accounting, cash, going-concern and climate judgments actually debated, including significant alternatives; **(4)** exceptions, material disagreements, advice sought and how each was resolved or why an immaterial item was accepted; and **(5)** the resolution, signatories, any dissent/abstention and assigned filing/deadline responsibilities. An accuracy-checked action register and the evidence pack should remain linked to the board record. Minutes should not falsely assert 'auditor approved the accounts' when the auditor merely gave an opinion. [L1, ss 180, 189, 295(5), 344; J1, J3; AICD[TOOL, 2025/26 V1, pp. 118–123]]

### 05.16.3 Escalation if information is incomplete or management and auditor disagree

An apparent contradiction is not cured by asking management to restate its confidence. The chair or audit committee should articulate the **specific assertion at risk** (such as recoverability, liability completeness, fraud exposure or solvency), quantify its possible financial effect, request underlying documents and alternate scenarios, and obtain the external auditor's independent position. The board may commission specialist accounting/legal valuations advice, ask management to amend the draft, increase audit scope or initiate an independent investigation. It must consider whether shareholders, a regulator or the market must already be informed of the matter. [L1; AU1; X1]

If the evidence is absent or a potentially material misstatement remains unresolved at D, **defer approval** and record that decision, even if a deadline is imminent. Negotiate lawful reporting extensions or regulator contact where available and seek advice about consequences; do not backdate a declaration or claim that missing evidence was received. A director unable to concur should state the substantive reason, seek its inclusion in the minutes and obtain independent advice if necessary. Resignation alone does not discharge past duties or fix defective reporting. If a liquidity forecast indicates possible inability to pay debts when due, activate urgent solvency and restructuring governance as a separate path; an accounting going-concern note is not a substitute. [L1, ss 95A, 180, 189, 190, 295, 344, 588G; AICD[LEG, 2025/26 V1, pp. 38–53]; J1]

**One-page final decision rule:** *Classify the entity and period; identify who must declare what; reconcile the statements, notes, forecasts and external claims; interrogate all material judgment and control exceptions; understand the exact assurance opinion and its limits; establish cash capacity; approve only when the evidence reasonably supports the required statements; record the decision and complete lodgement.*

## Appendix D. Open issues, update triggers and confidence limits

1. **Entity-specific relief.** A public company, foreign-controlled proprietary entity, listed trust, registrable superannuation entity, NGER reporter, CLG charity, CATSI corporation or state association may have relief, exemptions, extra prudential requirements or a unique filing deadline not captured by a generic row. Obtain a legal form, constitutional and statutory applicability opinion before live sign-off. [L1–L4]
2. **ORIC exactly $100,000 CGOI.** Current ORIC webpage descriptions differ on inclusive versus exclusive audit boundary for a *small* CATSI corporation. Seek the operative statutory instrument and ORIC written determination; do not infer from an ambiguous web table. [R5, R16]
3. **ACT incorporated-association review/audit detail.** This paper verifies the reporting/annual-return reform but does **not claim** a specific 2026 ACT monetary audit threshold without primary instrument support. Obtain current ACT sections and Regulations for an ACT client. [R12]
4. **Tasmanian association at exactly $250,000.** Confirm threshold inclusivity and latest subordinate legislation; published plain-language 'below' versus 'over' descriptions do not resolve equality without checking operative drafting. [R13]
5. **Nick Scali 2024 historical non-GAAP bridge.** The management narrative's $87.1m post-lease figure is not reproduced by subtracting separately presented lease principal and interest from operating cash. A full preparer reconciliation would be required before asserting error; this document makes **no allegation**. [AICD[NICK, 2024, pp. 5, 25]]
6. **SLSQ borrowings.** The historical change in debt exceeds the cash statement's separately labelled borrowing proceeds. Confirm all noncash movements, group changes and financing notes before interpreting the source as a failed reconciliation. [AICD[FAPP, 2025/26 V1, pp. 9–14]]
7. **Standard-version updates.** Recheck AASB 18, AASB S2, Australian-specific amendments, AASB 1061 and AUASB assurance instruments for any amendments effective **after 27 Oct 2026**; this is an as-of research snapshot, not a continuously updated legal service. [ST1, ST8–ST11; AU2–AU3]
8. **Case/judgment limits.** Centro pinpoints refer to *Healey* liability judgment, not its relief judgment; HIH and Slater & Gordon historical teaching is not a court holding on another firm's facts. Avoid transferring case-specific penalties or professional obligations mechanically. [J1–J5]
9. **Unprovided cross-file corpus context.** This standalone file does not certify existence, wording, source audit or legal consistency of the other eight intended corpus files. The master index should eventually reconcile those files' specific definitions and source registers.
10. **Use and responsibility.** This document is for private study and professional decision preparation, not a substitute for appointed directors' personal reading, external assurance, entity-specific advice, or the board's own contemporaneous evidence and resolution.
