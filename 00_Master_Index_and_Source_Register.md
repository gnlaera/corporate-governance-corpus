## [00.01] Corpus purpose, limits, jurisdictions, curriculum vintage and law-verification date

### [00.01.1] Design objective

This file is the **catalogue, authority resolver and navigation layer**, not a substitute for a governance handbook. In the intended nine-file corpus, `01`–`08` provide the substantive explanations, tools and worked applications. File `00` connects the same subject across different legal forms without repeating those chapters. Use it to select the correct source, distinguish what the AICD taught from law now in force, identify questions for independent review, and find the most appropriate decision-maker.

The scope has three principal families: (i) companies under the *Corporations Act 2001* (Cth), including proprietary, public, not-for-profit companies limited by guarantee (CLGs) and ASX-listed companies; (ii) not-for-profits, ACNC-registered charities, incorporated associations, trusts and other eligible legal forms; and (iii) Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander corporations under the *Corporations (Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander) Act 2006* (Cth) (CATSI Act), including registered native title bodies corporate (RNTBCs), which are also called prescribed bodies corporate (PBCs) in their native title context. These labels describe different things: **legal form**, **tax/charity registration**, **special-purpose native title role**, **regulator**, and **member or rights-holder status**.

The geographical scope is the Commonwealth plus all eight states and territories: New South Wales (NSW), Victoria (Vic), Queensland (Qld), Western Australia (WA), South Australia (SA), Tasmania (Tas), Australian Capital Territory (ACT) and Northern Territory (NT). Sector-specific obligations (such as prudential, workplace safety, environment, privacy, taxation and funding conditions) remain additional overlays. Foreign governing laws, overseas activities and entity-specific constitutions require separate examination.

## [00.03] Entity classification and applicability matrix, including overlapping registrations

### [00.03.1] The five-axis classification test

An organisation may simultaneously be: (1) a **legal person/form** (company, incorporated association, CATSI corporation); (2) **registered under another scheme** (ACNC charity, deductible gift recipient, tax concession status); (3) **subject to a specialist regulatory framework** (ASX, APRA, funding/licence laws); (4) **acting in a governance capacity** (director, charity Responsible Person, trustee, native title agent/trustee); and (5) **bound by distinct decision rights** (directors' resolution, members' special resolution, common law holder consent, regulator approval). Record all five axes independently. The colloquial words “board”, “member”, “corporation” and “charity” cannot determine the governing statute on their own.

| Form / possible overlay | Created under and primary public regulator | Governance body and relevant duties | Decision-right warning | Typical additional overlay |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ASX-listed public company | Corporations Act; ASIC; ASX as market operator | Directors/officers, common law/equity; Corporations Act Chapter 2D | Board vs shareholders; reserved matters; Listing Rules continuous disclosure | ASX Listing Rules; fourth-edition governance disclosure; sometimes APRA |
| Unlisted public company / CLG | Corporations Act; ASIC | Directors/officers; CLG has members not shareholders in the usual share-capital sense | Constitution, member rights and company-purpose rules | ACNC if charity; fundraising; other licences |
| Proprietary limited company | Corporations Act; ASIC | Directors/officers; close-company governance still subject to statute | Shareholder agreement cannot authorise unlawful conduct | ACNC if appropriately registered; tax/industry |
| Registered charity **as CLG** | Corporations Act plus ACNC Act/Regulations; ACNC and ASIC in distinct roles | Company directors are also typically ACNC Responsible People | ACNC Governance Standard 5 is a charity obligation; do not mechanically apply every Corporations Act civil duty without checking statutory modifications | Charity reporting, AIS, related-party and purpose controls |
| Incorporated association | Relevant state/territory Associations Act; local state regulator | Management committee/office bearers under that Act | Members' powers, committee powers, winding up and reporting vary by jurisdiction | ACNC if registered charity; grants, fundraising |
| Registered charity **as incorporated association** | State Act plus ACNC; state regulator and ACNC | Committee members are generally Responsible People too | Dual registers do **not** change entity into a Corporations Act company | Some reporting exemptions/substitutions; confirm jurisdiction |
| CATSI corporation | CATSI Act; Office of Registrar of Indigenous Corporations (ORIC) | CATSI directors/officers; registered rule book | CATSI membership eligibility and director selection; not automatically Corporations Act director provisions | ACNC charity possible; funding, tax, employment |
| CATSI RNTBC/PBC, agent type | CATSI plus Native Title Act/PBC Regulations; ORIC/native title institutions | CATSI corporation, plus native title-agent duties | Affected common law holders direct native title decisions; not necessarily same as members | ACNC charity optional; ILUA/other native title processes |
| CATSI RNTBC/PBC, trustee type | CATSI plus Native Title Act/PBC Regulations; ORIC/native title institutions | Corporation holds determined native title on trust for holders | Trustee status and native title consents are separate from corporate voting | Trustee obligations, funds and benefits governance |
| Charitable trust / trustee company | Trust deed, state trust law plus applicable corporate law for trustee; ACNC if registered | Trustee (individual or corporate); directors of corporate trustee also owe company duties | Beneficiary and trust-purpose constraints; trustee cannot treat assets as unconstrained corporate property | ACNC/ATO, trust reporting, grant/fund restrictions |
| Unincorporated association / community group | Common law, contracts and any applicable statutes | Committee, members or trustees may have personal obligations | No assumed separate corporate personality or limited liability | ACNC may register certain forms; check eligibility |
| Cooperative or statutory body | Cooperative statute or establishing Act; relevant regulator | Governing board under own statute | Member-benefit or public-law decision powers may differ | ACNC, APRA or other overlay if applicable |

**Overlaps are cumulative subject to statutory modifications, exemptions and interaction rules; they are not a menu from which a board picks one regulator.** A CLG charity still exists as a company. A CATSI charity remains a CATSI corporation. An RNTBC may be an ACNC charity yet still must follow affected common law holder native title decision procedures. A WA association charity remains a WA incorporated association, despite filing information with the ACNC. Check regulatory allocation and reporting concessions, rather than assuming duplicate filings are always necessary.

| Question asked by the board | First controlling source to inspect | Evidence to retain |
|---|---|---|

### [00.03.3] Non-substitutable distinctions

- **Charity registration** is a regulatory status, not a new legal entity form or proof that all board members are “company directors” under the Corporations Act.
- **ORIC registration** is a CATSI corporate scheme, not a subdivision of ASIC registration. The CATSI Act's own director duty and financial-reporting provisions require separate checking.
- **RNTBC member** describes corporate membership; **common law holder** describes the persons/group holding rights recognised by native title law. The latter can be a non-member and still have decisive legal rights. Consult ORIC's *Decision-making in registered native title bodies corporate* (accessed 27 October 2026) and PBC Regulations rr 8, 8A, 8B and 9.
- **Trustee** is a legal capacity. In a trustee RNTBC, the corporation holds recognised native title as trustee; a CATSI corporation may otherwise be an agent/representative. The decision process is not interchangeable.
- **Aboriginal community governance** is not fully defined by the CATSI Act. Locally legitimate authority, relationships and culturally appropriate deliberation must be established with the relevant people, not inferred from generic examples.

## [00.04] Authority hierarchy: binding law, guidance, principles and analysis

A single universal linear ranking would be misleading. Statute, case law and delegated regulation interact through jurisdiction and interpretation; an ASX Listing Rule is binding by the listing relationship and legal framework; a constitution regulates internal powers but cannot displace statute; accounting standards become mandatory **where incorporated by the applicable reporting law**. Apply this operational order:

| Authority class | Legal force / method | Retrieval and conflict rule |
|---|---|---|
| Commonwealth/state/territory **Acts** | Binding within scope; priority depends on valid enactment, constitutional rules and inconsistency | Identify jurisdiction, commencement, latest applicable text, transitional provisions and class of entity. Never transplant Corporations Act sections directly into CATSI or state-Act entities. |
| **Regulations, rules and legislative instruments** | Binding within enabling authority | Cite enabling Act, exact instrument and regulation/section; check revocation and replacement (for example ACNC 2013 Regulation repealed, 2022 Regulations in force). |
| **Judicial decisions** | Authoritative interpretation subject to court hierarchy, later decisions and statutory change | Prefer judgments over headlines; identify ratio, court, year, facts and scope. No invented paragraph pinpoints. |
| **ASX Listing Rules** | Binding for entities to which the rules apply; distinct from recommended ASX governance practices | Listing Rule 3.1 and 4.10.3 are not equivalent to voluntary adherence to every principle. Apply exceptions and listing category rules. |
| **APRA prudential standards** | Binding where an institution is within the standard's scope | Record industry, instrument version, effective and transition dates; a draft CPS 510 consultation is not operative law. |
| **Australian Accounting Standards (AASB)** | Accounting standards with applicable statutory/reporting force for required financial or sustainability reports | Confirm for-profit/NFP entity, reporting tier and **reporting period**; distinguish AASB 101 and forthcoming AASB 18, AASB S1 voluntary and mandatory AASB S2 cohorts. |
| **AUASB auditing/assurance standards** | Binding for relevant audit/assurance engagements as prescribed, with effective dates | Audit opinions and directors' declarations are different duties. Verify standards operative in the audit period. |
| **Constitution, trust deed, CATSI rule book, by-laws and lawful delegations** | Legal/governance allocation of rights and authority subject to superior law | Read the entity's actual current documents, registers, shareholder agreements and amendment history. |
| **Regulator guidance (ASIC, ACNC, ORIC, state regulators)** | Influential interpretation and compliance expectations, normally not legislation itself | Link specific guidance date to relevant law; do not replace a statutory test with a regulator summary. |
| **AICD NFP principles, ISO/COSO, ASX principles, AIGI frameworks** | Voluntary/educational unless adopted via binding instrument or contract | Explain adoption, entity relevance and evidence of implementation. ASX recommendations connect to compulsory *disclosure* via Listing Rule 4.10.3. |
| **Indigenous cultural governance principles** | Source of legitimate and culturally appropriate process, potentially embodied in local customary law, native title law, rule book or agreements | Distinguish general AIGI principles from community-authorised rules. Do not claim to speak for a Nation or prescribe universal protocols. |
| **AICD 2025/26 teaching and older 2016/2024 annexes** | Historical curriculum, not law or bespoke advice | Use source-pinned `AICD[...]`; compare with dated current authority. |
| **Independent corpus analysis, generated case exercises, heuristics** | No independent legal authority | Label assumptions and inferences; validate against original sources and actual financial information. |

**Interpretation example:** The AICD's 2025/26 appendix reproduces the *ASX Corporate Governance Principles and Recommendations*, fourth edition. It is a useful teaching extract; the **current** ASX website, Listing Rule 4.10.3 and the original fourth edition control the October 2026 disclosure baseline. The ASX consultation opened 21 July 2026 on a fifth edition, but ASX's 13 October advisory-group update continued to describe market feedback, not an adopted fifth edition (official ASX URL in [00.07]).

## [00.05] Core legal and governance definitions and capacity distinctions

These are **index definitions** for retrieval. They identify the legal question; use the applicable statute and cases in [00.07] for a definitive interpretation.

| Term | Context-sensitive meaning and caution | Authoritative route |
|---|---|---|
| **Company** | Body corporate registered under the Corporations Act; separate legal personality. “Company” should not be used as a generic name for every association or CATSI corporation. | Corporations Act ss 9, 112, 119, 124 |
| **Corporation** | Wider expression that can include companies and other bodies corporate for specified legal purposes. CATSI “corporation” is a separate statutory category. | Check definition in each Act; Corporations Act s 57A; CATSI Act |
| **Director** | Formally appointed, alternate in capacity, de facto, or a person whose instructions/wishes directors are accustomed to act on (shadow) where the statutory tests apply. Not every adviser or influential stakeholder is automatically a director. | Corporations Act s 9; CATSI definitions; GOV 2.1, LEG 2.3 |
| **Officer** | Includes company secretary and statutory functional tests: participation in substantial corporate decision-making, capacity significantly to affect financial standing, or instructions followed by directors in certain cases. Can extend beyond job titles. | Corporations Act s 9; *Shafron v ASIC* [2012] HCA 18; *ASIC v King* [2020] HCA 4 |
| **Non-executive director** | Director not in an executive employment role; owes statutory duties as a director. Not necessarily “independent” or exempt from financial literacy. | Corporations Act and ASX recommendations; GOV 1.3, 2 |
| **Independent director** | Governance assessment of relationships and independent judgement, particularly relevant to ASX principles and board composition; not a separate general statutory director class. | ASX 4th ed Recommendation 2.3–2.5 |
| **Chair** | Director elected/appointed to lead board proceedings under constitution/statute; usually no automatic unilateral power to substitute for the collective board. | Entity constitution/rule book; GOV 4; BE |
| **Chief executive officer (CEO)** | Senior executive accountable for management within delegated powers; may independently satisfy “officer” or “director” definitions according to facts. | Corporations Act s 9; governance documents |
| **ACNC Responsible Person** | Person responsible for governing a registered charity, including a director or association committee member; regulatory label is **not** interchangeable with every company-law “officer”. | ACNC Governance Standards and ACNC official *Responsible People* guidance |
| **Association committee member** | Governance decision-maker or office bearer under the *particular state/territory* incorporation statute and association rules; not automatically a Corporations Act director. | Applicable state Associations Act; LAPP March 2024 comparison as historical aid |
| **Member** | Statutory or constitutional participant in a company, association or CATSI corporation. Eligibility, register, vote, removal and special resolutions differ. | Governing Act and constituent document |
| **Trustee** | Person or body holding legal property or rights subject to trust obligations for beneficiaries/purposes. Directors of a corporate trustee retain their own corporate duties. | Trust deed, general trust law; Native Title Act/PBC Regulations for trustee RNTBC |
| **Common law native title holder** | Person or group whose native title rights and interests are recognised under native title law, including under a determination. **May not be an RNTBC member.** | Native Title Act; PBC Regulations rr 8–9; ORIC *Native title common law holders* |
| **Registered native title body corporate (RNTBC)** | Corporation registered in the native title framework to act as trustee or agent/representative for native title holders; generally CATSI-registered. “PBC” can refer to prescribed corporation before/during the determination-to-registration process. | Native Title Act ss 56–59; PBC Regulations rr 4–9; ORIC |
| **Rule book** | Registered internal governance rules of a CATSI corporation; can provide governance and some consultation arrangements subject to higher law. | CATSI Act; ORIC register, entity-specific rules |
| **Charity** | Organisation registered with ACNC as charitable, with eligible charitable purposes and public-benefit requirements; legal status overlay, not necessarily CLG. | ACNC Act; *Charities Act 2013* (Cth); ACNC register |
| **Beneficiary, funder, donor or stakeholder** | Interests can be legally or ethically relevant but do not automatically confer statutory member voting powers or native title holder decision rights. | Governing documents, trusts, contracts, funding agreements, charity-purpose law |
| **Insolvency** | For relevant company-law contexts, inability to pay debts as and when due; not synonymous with negative profit, negative working capital or accounting impairment. | Corporations Act s 95A, s 588G; applicable CATSI/state provisions |
| **Solvency declaration** | Directors' declaration differs by entity/reporting law; often requires reasonable grounds for paying debts as and when due. | Corporations Act s 295(4) and applicable exemptions; FIN 3.3 |
| **Risk appetite** | Amount/type of risk the board is prepared to take in pursuing objectives; a governance decision integrated with strategy, distinct from risk limits and realised risk exposure. | RISK 1.2, 3.4; TOOL pp. 30, 91–92 |
| **Reserve matter / delegation** | Matter retained for board/member decision versus delegated to CEO/management subject to lawful limits; delegation is not removal of director duties. | Governing Act, constitution, delegation schedule; TOOL pp. 36–38 |

**Search synonyms:** PBC = prescribed body corporate; RNTBC = registered native title body corporate; CLG = company limited by guarantee; “Committee of Management” can mean incorporated-association governance body; “Responsible Person” is ACNC vocabulary; “officer” and “director” can be statutory functional classifications regardless of a job title. Every search answer should state the governing definition used.

## [00.10] Glossary, abbreviations, financial formulas, citation guide and applicability matrix

### [00.10.1] Governance and legal glossary

**Governance:** system for directing, controlling and holding an organisation accountable, including formal authority, purpose, structure, relationships, information and culture. **Management:** execution of activities within delegated powers; directors generally oversee and decide reserved matters rather than supervise every operation. **Director mindset:** independent inquiry, duty to the entity, attention to material risk, evidence and the board's collective decision; not a separate statutory doctrine. **Fiduciary:** person in a relationship requiring loyalty and proper handling of conflicts; particular duties and beneficiaries depend on law and circumstance. **Duty of care and diligence:** statutory/common law standard of competent decision-making in an office and its responsibilities, not a guarantee of outcomes. **Business judgment rule:** conditional protection under Corporations Act s 180(2) for qualifying judgments satisfying its statutory conditions; not a blanket excuse for regulatory non-compliance, all omissions or every statutory breach. **Proper purpose:** use powers for authorised ends; contractual authority does not excuse improper use. **Material personal interest:** conflict-disclosure term in company law, requiring separate assessment of exceptions and voting/attendance rules. **Related party:** a legally defined relationship for relevant transaction, disclosure and conflict rules, not every acquaintance. **Shadow/de facto director:** fact-sensitive categories of persons treated as directors although appointment may be absent/defective. **Board charter:** allocation of board/management functions and operating practices; cannot override legislation. **Delegated authority:** lawful grant of power with limits, monitoring and accountability; retaining directors' own duties. **Risk tolerance:** measurable permitted variation/limits around objectives and appetite; distinguish appetite from capacity, limit and residual risk. **Risk capacity:** maximum risk absorbable given resources, obligations and survival constraints. **Risk assurance:** evidence on design and operating effectiveness of controls, preferably independent where material. **Management representation:** statement by management providing assurance; not substitute for directors' independent understanding. **Going concern:** accounting premise of continuing operations subject to applicable standard and disclosures, not identical to cash-flow solvency. **Solvency:** capacity to pay debts when due where the relevant legal test applies. **Native title consent:** agreement of affected common law holders through the applicable legally prescribed or locally authorised process, not a generic shareholder vote. **Cultural legitimacy:** governance authority and process accepted as credible by the people affected; must be locally grounded. **Two-way accountability:** Indigenous governance both to members/communities and to external institutions/partners, per AIGI. **Corporation member:** status under the corporate register/rule book; may not be a native title holder. **Common law holder:** holder of determined native title rights; may not be a corporate member. **Charitable purpose:** eligible purpose under charity law; also constrains governance and application of resources. **Restricted grant funds:** assets subject to donor, grant or trust terms; unrestricted cash available for overheads is a separate question.

### [00.10.3] Financial formula directory and interpretation controls

Formulas are **analytical conventions**, not automatically prescribed accounting ratios. Use consistent financial periods and average balance-sheet denominators where appropriate; reconcile non-GAAP and alternative performance measures to reported values. Percentages multiply the stated ratio by 100.

| Metric | Formula or conceptual identity | Use / qualification |
|---|---|---|
| Accounting identity | **Assets = Liabilities + Equity** | Must reconcile at statement date; valuation/accounting basis still matters |
| Working capital | **Current assets − Current liabilities** | Negative amount not by itself proof of insolvency |
| Current ratio | **Current assets / Current liabilities** | AICD's ~1.5× is an educational heuristic, not legal minimum |
| Quick ratio | **(Cash + cash equivalents + short-term investments + receivables) / Current liabilities** | Excludes inventory/prepayments; define included receivables |
| Cash ratio | **Cash and cash equivalents / Current liabilities** | Restricted funds or committed cash may be unavailable |
| Net debt | **Interest-bearing debt − unrestricted cash** | Define leases and restricted cash clearly |
| Net debt / EBITDA | **Net debt / EBITDA** | Cash generation and debt covenant definitions may differ |
| Debt-to-equity | **Interest-bearing borrowings / total equity** | Equity may be small/negative; compare alternatives |
| Interest coverage | **EBIT / net finance interest expense** | Denominator conventions vary; cash interest test also relevant |
| Gross profit | **Revenue − cost of goods sold** | In NFP entities may not measure service mission efficiency |
| Gross margin | **Gross profit / revenue** | Compare operating models and cost classifications |
| EBITDA margin | **EBITDA / revenue** | Excludes depreciation, interest, tax; not cash or economic profit |
| Net margin | **NPAT / revenue** | Subject to tax, one-offs, impairments and accounting judgments |
| ROE | **NPAT attributable to ordinary equity / average ordinary equity** | Leverage and accounting equity can distort comparison |
| ROA | **Operating profit after tax or NPAT / average total assets** | Specify numerator; operating after-tax often isolates financing |
| ROIC | **Net operating profit after tax (NOPAT) / average invested capital** | Define goodwill, leases, cash and invested capital consistently |
| Economic profit | **(ROIC − WACC) × invested capital** | Return vs capital-cost lens; use same nominal/real and tax basis |
| Asset turnover | **Revenue / average operating assets** | Mission output may dominate revenue in NFPs |
| DSO | **Average trade receivables / credit sales × days in period** | Use sales on credit and comparable periods |
| DIO | **Average inventory / COGS × days in period** | Inventory-valued entity-dependent; not for service-only NFP |
| DPO | **Average trade payables / COGS × days in period** | Approximation when purchases materially differ from COGS |
| Cash conversion cycle | **DSO + DIO − DPO** | Negative may be structurally normal, not automatically good or bad |
| OCF / EBITDA | **Operating cash flow / EBITDA** | Timing, working capital, tax and interest classification affect result |
| OCF / NPAT | **Operating cash flow / NPAT** | Distorts with small/negative denominators and one-offs |
| Free cash flow | **OCF − cash capital expenditure** (basic definition) | Clarify lease principal, acquisitions, financing and sustaining vs growth capex |
| Cash-flow continuity | **Opening cash + OCF + investing CF + financing CF ± FX/other cash movements = closing cash** | Source standard may separately report exchange effects |
| Dividend payout | **Dividends / NPAT** or **dividends / free cash flow** | State which denominator and recognition period |
| NPV | **Σ[CF_t / (1+r)^t] − upfront investment** | Use consistent discount rate, taxes, currency and inflation |
| IRR | Discount rate satisfying **NPV = 0** | Reinvestment and scale issues; compare NPV and capital constraints |
| Liquidity runway | **Available unrestricted cash / expected net cash burn per period** | Scenario-dependent; funding commitments and restricted funds matter |
| Restricted-fund coverage | **Unrestricted liquid resources / unrestricted near-term commitments** | Useful charity-specific overlay, not a named statutory ratio |

**Finance cross-check:** The *Discount Depot* source provides a self-contained example. At 30 June 2024, its **current ratio** is A$124.566m / A$83.805m ≈ **1.49×** and **working capital** ≈ A$40.761m, calculated from DISC p. 3. FY2024 operating cash flow A$25.608m versus FY2023 A$46.852m represents a fall of about **45%**; FY2024 profit A$17.100m versus A$14.239m is higher. This is an original calculation for teaching the **profit vs cash-flow divergence**; it is not evidence about an actual company. Raw figures: AICD[DISC, 2025/26 V1, pp. 2–5]. Other revenue and working capital items must be assessed to explain the divergence; the limited statements do not prove its precise causes.

## [00.12] Source and copyright boundaries, responsible use, integrity and cultural authority

### [00.12.1] AICD material and third-party Notebook boundaries

AICD course notes and director tools are **copyrighted educational resources**. ### [00.12.2] Legal and financial use limits

AICD source content and AI-generated explanations may contain omissions, historical assumptions, uncertain facts or inconsistent page headers. A Notebook-generated answer must be checked against the **specific entity's law, constitution or rule book, registrations, contractual restrictions, reporting date, financial statements and advisers' professional opinions**. This document does not certify that a director's statutory duties were discharged, that financial statements give a true and fair view, that an entity is solvent, or that an organisation is suitable for charity registration. The 2016 sign-off guide and teaching heuristics must be clearly marked historical or analytical, not mandatory thresholds.

No regulator guidance page, general sector principle or source-summary paragraph can supplant a statute, court order or governing instrument. Case examples may be fictional; real-world examples must be dated and tied to genuine primary documents. Financial models should distinguish reported accounting information, independent derived calculations, estimates and assumptions. Never quote an unverified judgment paragraph or a source page not actually checked.

### [00.12.3] Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander cultural and data governance

This corpus is **not an Indigenous cultural authority** and does not claim to speak for any people, Nation, Elders or native title holders. AIGI's principles are valuable external reading, but they cannot define local law, membership, authority, consent, lore, confidential knowledge or who may speak for Country. Native title holder decision rights are legal rights, not merely stakeholder engagement. A signed corporate resolution is **not proof** of consultation or consent by affected common law holders. Check the specific RNTBC rule book, Native Title Act and PBC Regulations, identify the affected holders, and respect the actual group-authorised process. Restricted cultural information, personal data, locations, agreement terms and internal disputes should not be placed in an AI platform absent authority, consent and suitable controls.

Respect Indigenous data governance and community-controlled decisions about circulation, attribution and use of knowledge. Do not infer a traditional practice from an AICD classroom example or generate plausible-sounding cultural procedures.
